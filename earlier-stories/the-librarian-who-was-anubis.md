The first time we noticed Mr Jackson was on a Tuesday, when Year 1 got LOST.

It was the whole class. They had gone to the toilets and then come back out and every single one of them had turned LEFT instead of right, and they ended up in the boiler room, and nobody could find them.

Miss Riley was in a total PANIC. Mr Harris was running up and down the corridor shouting "Has anybody seen twenty-eight Year 1s?"

And that's when Mr Jackson, the new librarian, put down his tea, sniffed the air ONCE, and said, "This way."

He walked straight to the boiler room. He did not look at a map. He did not ask anyone. He opened the door, and there they were, all sitting in a line in the dark, singing.

He led them back to class in a neat line, and he didn't even look out of breath.

## THE THEORY

At break, we all sat in the corner of the playground, because Nell Brooks said she had something VERY IMPORTANT to tell us.

Nell is the only person in our class who has read a whole book about ancient Egypt, and she said it so many times that we all know it's true.

She took a deep breath and said, "I think Mr Jackson is ANUBIS."

Nobody said anything, because none of us knew who Anubis was.

Kofi Mensah put up his hand like we were in class and said, "Who's Anubis?"

Nell GASPED, and then she looked a bit pleased, because she loves explaining things.

## WHO IS ANUBIS?

Nell said that thousands of years ago, in a country called Egypt, people believed in LOTS of gods. And one of them was called Anubis.

She said Anubis has the head of a JACKAL, which is a sort of wild dog with big pointy ears and a very good nose. He has a person's body, but his head is black, and the Egyptians chose black on purpose because it was the colour of the rich, soft mud beside the river Nile where all the crops grew. So black meant new life, not scary.

Kofi said, "What does he DO?"

Nell said that he has two main jobs.

**JOB NUMBER ONE:** He is the guide for people who have died. When someone dies, the Egyptians believed they had to travel through a dark place full of twisting tunnels and doors, and they could get LOST. So Anubis takes them gently by the hand and shows them the way, and he's never lost anyone, not once.

We all looked at each other. Then we all looked back at the boiler room.

**JOB NUMBER TWO:** He looks after the weighing of hearts. We didn't know what that meant either, so Nell explained.

## THE FEATHER

Nell said that when people reach the end of the journey, there is a big golden pair of scales. And Anubis puts your HEART on one side. (Not your actual heart, said Nell, more like a magic one.) And on the other side, he puts a single white FEATHER.

The feather belongs to a goddess called Maat, who stands for being fair and honest and kind.

If your heart is light, and as light as the feather, it means you have been good, and you get to go to a beautiful, peaceful place called the Field of Reeds, where there is plenty of food and everybody is happy.

Kofi said, "What if your heart is heavy?"

Nell said, "It's not very likely, if you are mostly kind."

We were all quiet for a moment, and everyone was secretly trying to remember if they'd been kind that week.

I had given Archie half my cereal bar on Monday, so I was fairly sure I was fine.

## THE EVIDENCE

Nell had a list. She said that every good theory needs a list.

**1. HE FINDS LOST THINGS.** This is the guide thing. We had to agree. Mr Jackson had also found Priya's lost PE bag, a missing library card, and a very small shoe that nobody could explain.

**2. HE WEARS BLACK.** Every single day. Black jumper, black trousers, black shoes. He has a NOTICEABLY black coat, and Kofi said that it's the sort of coat a jackal would wear if he had to wear a coat.

**3. HE HAS A GOOD NOSE.** Mr Jackson can always tell when someone is eating a crisp in the library, even if they are hiding in the fiction section and the crisp is cheese and onion. He just sniffs and says, "Please put it away."

**4. HE HAS A VERY POINTY HAIRCUT.** His hair sticks up on both sides of his head in two triangles, like ears. We weren't going to mention it, but Kofi did.

We all agreed it was the best list we'd ever seen.

## THE MUMMY

Then on Wednesday, something happened that made everyone SURE.

Mr Harris had told us it was Egypt Week for Year 2 (which Nell said was a MASSIVE COINCIDENCE), and Mrs Okafor said we were going to make a MUMMY.

Nobody knew what a mummy was, except Nell.

Nell explained that in Egypt, they wrapped people carefully in long strips of linen cloth, to keep them safe for the journey. It was a very important and respectful job, and the one who looked after it was ANUBIS. He was called the god of the "wrapping".

So Mrs Okafor brought out about forty rolls of toilet paper and asked who wanted to be the mummy.

Freddie Ng put up his hand, because he thought it would be funny.

Mrs Okafor said, "Right, we need someone who is very good at wrapping."

And Mr Jackson, who had just come in to bring us some books, said, very quietly, "I could help."

## THE WRAPPING

We all watched him closely.

Mr Jackson took a roll of paper, and he began at Freddie's feet. He wrapped it round and round and round, so smoothly that there was no wobble, no gap, and not a single crease. He did Freddie's legs, then his tummy, then his arms, and he even did the fingers one at a time.

He hummed a tiny, soft tune while he did it, and every few seconds he checked that Freddie was comfortable.

"Alright in there?" he whispered.

"Yes," said Freddie, in a muffled voice, because his mouth was covered.

When he was done, Freddie looked like the neatest mummy in the whole history of Egypt. Mrs Okafor said it was the best wrapping she'd ever seen, and she took six photos.

Nell turned to us with her eyes SHINING and whispered, **"HE KNEW HOW TO DO IT PROPERLY."**

Mr Jackson looked round at us and said, "Egyptians took great care over wrapping. They believed that everyone, no matter who they were, deserved to be treated with respect."

Then he went very red and said, "I read it in a book."

Kofi nodded very slowly and said, "Obviously."

## GARY

On Thursday, something sad happened.

Our class goldfish, Gary, who had lived in a tank by the window for two whole years, was found floating at the top. Mrs Okafor said that Gary had died in the night, and he was very old for a goldfish, and he'd had a good life.

Lots of us cried. Archie Bell cried so hard he got hiccups.

Mrs Okafor said we could say goodbye if we wanted to, and she asked if anyone knew what to do. Nobody did.

And then Mr Jackson, who had just wandered in to return a book, stopped in the doorway.

He didn't say a word. He just walked up to the tank and gently scooped Gary into his hands. He held him for a moment, as if he was a very small and precious thing, and he whispered something that we couldn't quite hear.

Then he said, "Would you like to come to the garden?"

We all followed him out, and we stood in a circle under the big oak tree while Mr Jackson dug a small hole. He placed Gary inside, very gently, and said, "He was a kind fish, and everybody here loved him. That's the most important thing."

And Nell leaned over and whispered, "Anubis looks after everyone who has died. Even the animals."

I thought it was the nicest thing anyone had ever said. Archie said so too, and then he got hiccups again.

## THE SCALES

On Friday, we decided to test him.

Nell said that we couldn't just ASK Mr Jackson if he was Anubis, because gods are very private about that sort of thing. So she had a plan.

We went to the library at lunch, and Nell put a SMALL WHITE FEATHER on the library desk, one that she had found in the playground. Then we waited.

Mr Jackson came out of the back room and saw the feather. He stopped.

He picked it up and looked at it for a long time. Then he placed it very carefully on the edge of his desk, next to a little set of kitchen scales that he used to weigh the parcels.

We all held our breath.

Then he looked over at us, with his head tilted slightly to the side, like a dog who has heard a very interesting noise, and said:

**"Do any of you have a heavy heart today?"**

Nobody said anything. We just stood there. Kofi's mouth had fallen open.

Then Archie said, "I stood on a snail on Monday, but it was an accident."

Mr Jackson said, "Accidents don't count. You felt bad about it, which means your heart is light."

Archie smiled all over his face and said, "Really?"

"Really," said Mr Jackson.

## THE PHONE CALL

At home time, we were walking past the library window when we heard Mr Jackson talking on the phone.

He had his back to us, and he was speaking in a quiet, serious voice.

"Yes, Thoth," he said. "I know. No, it's been quite a busy week. A fish, a lost class, and a mummy. Could you write it down?"

There was a pause.

"Good. And the Year 2s? All lighter than a feather, as far as I can tell. Yes. Every single one."

He put the phone down and turned round. He saw us all standing at the window with our noses squashed against the glass.

He looked at us for a very long time.

Then he gave the tiniest wink, and he turned off the lights.

## THE END (BUT NOT REALLY)

On the way home, Kofi asked who Thoth was.

Nell said that he was the Egyptian god of writing, and that he has the head of a bird called an ibis, and that he writes down the results of the heart weighing, so there is a record.

I said, "That's a very sensible job."

Nell said, "Yes. I think we might meet him next."

And that's when we all turned around very slowly and looked back at the school, where the new dinner lady, who has a long, thin nose and walks with her head going **forwards and backwards** like a bird, had just waved at us from the kitchen window.

**The End.**
