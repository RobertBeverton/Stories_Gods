The first time we properly spoke to the postman was on a Thursday, and he was WALKING BACKWARDS.

He came through the school gate with his bag over his shoulder, going backwards the whole way, and he didn't bump into a single thing. Not the bin. Not the bench. Not even Mr Harris, who stepped out of the way at the last second and said, "Morning, Mr Hermes, is there a reason for...?"

"Habit," said the postman, with a wink. "Old trick."

His badge said **MR HERMES, ROYAL MAIL (ISH).** He wore a red jacket and a little red cap with two tiny wings on the sides, and his white trainers had wings on the heels that flapped a bit even when he was standing still. Alex Bailey said the wings were probably just really good laces. Felix Bailey said laces don't flap. They agreed to check it out properly.

## THE TWINS' PLAN

Alex and Felix are twins, which means they have a system. Alex notices things and Felix asks about them, and then they swap, and it works every time.

On Friday, at break, Alex noticed that Mr Hermes was never late. Felix asked Mr Harris how many schools he delivered to, and Mr Harris said, "About forty, but he's always here first, and always has been." Then Felix noticed that the postman was never actually *seen* arriving. One minute the gate was empty, and the next he was just there.

Alex said, "That's not normal."

Felix said, "It's not even slightly normal."

They both said, "We need Maya."

## WHO IS HERMES?

Maya Singh was already waiting by the compost bin. She had been waiting since Wednesday.

She said, "I wondered how long it would take you."

Maya is the one who has read the Roman gods book AND the Greek one, so she explained everything while Ollie and Kenji sat on upside-down buckets.

She said that in ancient Greece, people believed in a god called **Hermes** (the Romans called him Mercury). He is the **messenger of the gods**, which means that when one god needs to send a message to another, Hermes delivers it. He's very, very fast, because he has **winged sandals** on his feet and a **winged hat** on his head.

Kenji said, "Like a postman."

Maya said, "Exactly like a postman. Except that he's also the god of **travellers**, so he keeps people safe on the road. And he's the god of **luck**, and of **traders**, and of **thieves**."

Ollie said, "Thieves?"

Maya said, "I'm getting to that."

## THE BABY WHO STOLE THE COWS

Maya took a deep breath, because it was her favourite story.

She said that Hermes was born in a cave one morning. By lunchtime, he was bored of his cradle, so he crawled out and wandered about. He found a **tortoise** and its shell, and he thought, "That looks like it could make a noise." So he scooped out the inside, stretched seven strings across it, and made the first ever **lyre**. It's a little harp. It played beautiful music.

Felix said, "He was ONE DAY old?"

Maya said, "He was about six hours old."

By the afternoon, he was bored of the music, and he was hungry. He remembered that his big brother **Apollo** had fifty beautiful cows in a field. So he crept out and stole them.

Alex said, "He STOLE them?"

Maya said, "Yes. But he was clever about it. He made little shoes out of tree bark and tied them to the cows' feet, so the tracks were all smudged. And then he made the cows walk **BACKWARDS**, so that the footprints looked like they were going into the field instead of out of it. Then he hid them in a cave, wriggled back into his cradle, pulled up the blanket, and pretended to be asleep."

Kenji clapped. Maya said, "Don't clap, it was naughty."

Kenji stopped. Then, a little quietly, he clapped again.

When Apollo came looking for his cows, he found a baby in a cradle who said, "Cows? What cows? I'm only a day old." Apollo took him to **Zeus**, the king of the gods, and Zeus laughed so hard that he nearly fell off his throne. But he said, "Give them back."

So Hermes did. And to make up for it, he gave Apollo the lyre. Apollo loved it so much that they became best friends, and Hermes got his job as messenger.

Felix said, "So he said sorry and shared something."

Alex said, "That's the right way to fix it."

Maya said, "Yes. That's the important part."

## THE LIST

Alex had the pencil. Felix held the notebook, because they always share jobs.

**1. THE WINGS.** The cap and the trainers. We all tested the trainers by standing very close to them, and they flapped.

**2. THE BACKWARDS WALKING.** He did it on Thursday, and again on Friday, and Maya said it was a classic Hermes move.

**3. HE'S ALWAYS THERE FIRST.** Nobody ever sees him arrive.

**4. HE KNOWS EVERYONE'S NAME.** Even the Reception children. Even the hamster.

**5. THE BAG.** It's a big red sack, and it's always full, and it never gets any lighter, no matter how many parcels come out.

Felix said, "It's the strongest list we've ever had."

Alex said, "Apart from Mr Thorley's."

Felix said, "We don't talk about Mr Thorley's."

## THE BAG GOES MISSING

On Monday, Mr Hermes arrived at school panting, red in the face, and **WITHOUT HIS BAG.**

It was the first time we had ever seen him out of breath. His little cap was crooked, and one of his sandal wings had gone wonky.

He said, "Mr Harris, I've got a problem. I put my bag down for **one second** to tie my shoe, and somebody took it."

Mr Harris said, "How long ago?"

Mr Hermes said, "About four minutes."

Mr Harris said, "That's not very long."

Mr Hermes said, in a rather strangled voice, "That bag has every message for every god, in every country, going out today. If it isn't delivered by three o'clock, everything gets MIXED UP."

Mr Harris said, "Mixed up how?"

At that very moment, a very strange announcement came over the speaker.

It was Mrs Ibbs, the dinner lady, and she said: **"Good morning, boys and girls! Today for lunch we are serving MATHS. Please queue by your own shadow."**

We all looked at each other.

## THE MIX-UP

It got worse by the minute.

By ten o'clock, Mr Thorley was in the hall with a ladder, shouting, "WHO TOLD ME TO TURN THE SUN OFF?" Mrs Felicity gave out forty-two raffle tickets labelled **"EVERYBODY LOSES."** Mr Jackson in the library was sent a message that said **"PLEASE LIE DOWN IN THE ROAD"**, and he was very polite about it, but he did put his coat on.

In Miss Persey's Gardening Club, all the flowers started blooming **upside down.** In the music room, Mr Shah was sent a message telling him to **DANCE VERY QUIETLY AND VERY LOUDLY AT THE SAME TIME**, and he kept trying.

Mr Harris was told by a mysterious voice in his ear to **swap places with the hamster.**

Nobody was hurt, but everything was **upside down and back to front.**

Felix said, "That's what Mr Hermes meant."

Alex said, "Every message is getting muddled because the bag isn't where it's supposed to be."

Maya whispered, "It's just like the stories. If the messenger of the gods gets lost, nobody knows what anyone is saying."

## THE FOOTPRINTS

Mr Hermes tried to fly after the thief, but when he flapped his sandals, one of them gave a sad little *pffft*, and fell off.

He said, in a very small voice, "I can't fly without both."

He sat down on the bench with his head in his hands.

Alex went over and sat beside him. He said, "Mr Hermes. You taught us something with the cows."

Mr Hermes said, "Did I?"

Felix sat on the other side of him. He said, "You walked them backwards so nobody could find them."

Alex said, "So if someone took your bag..."

Felix said, "...maybe they're doing the same thing. Walking backwards."

Mr Hermes lifted his head. A small, dazzling smile began to spread across his face. He said, "Go on."

The twins ran to the place where he had put the bag down. They looked at the muddy grass. There were little footprints.

Alex crouched down. He said, "These footprints are very small. And they're all pointing TOWARDS the gate, like someone walked in."

Felix said, "But look at the toes. And the heels."

Alex said, "The heel marks are deeper than the toe marks."

Felix said, "That means they were walking BACKWARDS. Away from the gate. Towards..."

They looked up. The footprints led all the way across the playground, round the side of the school, and up to the **bottom of the old fire escape**.

Alex said, "...the roof."

## THE ROOF

The fire escape was very old and rusty, and it went up and up, past the windows, all the way to the flat roof where nobody was ever allowed to go.

Mr Hermes said, "I can't take you. It's far too dangerous. I'll go."

But he couldn't fly with only one sandal, and his hands were shaking, because he's actually scared of heights, which is a very funny thing for a god with wings.

Maya said, very gently, "Mr Hermes. Can I tell you something my gran says?"

He said, "Please."

Maya said, "She says you can be scared **and** brave at the same time."

Mr Hermes's wings gave a little flutter. He said, "That's very good."

Alex said, "We learned it from Miss Persey."

Felix said, "We'll go together."

Hermes looked at them for a long time. Then he said, "All right. But I go first, and you hold on to the rail, and if anyone feels wobbly, we stop."

We climbed.

## THE CLIMB

It was a very long climb. The wind blew, and the metal creaked, and every step wobbled a little. Ollie held on to the back of my jumper. Kenji held on to Ollie, and he was counting the steps out loud, very fast.

Halfway up, Felix stopped. He had looked down.

He said, in a very small voice, "I'm not sure I can."

Alex said, "Look at me. Not down. At me."

Felix looked.

Alex said, "Say it with me. **I CAN DO HARD THINGS.**"

They said it together. Then Felix took a deep breath and put his foot on the next step.

A bit higher, Alex stopped. A gust of wind had blown his cap off, and he'd watched it fly away. He went very pale.

Felix said, "Look at me. Not at the cap."

Alex looked.

Felix said, "**I AM BRAVE EVEN WHEN I'M SCARED.**"

They said it together. Alex took the next step.

That was when we noticed that neither twin had let go of the other's hand since the second step.

## THE THIEF

At the very top, there was a flat grey roof with a low wall and, in the corner, a huge red bag, as big as a beanbag, with a tiny figure sitting on top of it.

It was a **little girl**, with two wonky plaits and a muddy knee. She was in **Reception**, which is the smallest class in the school, and she had a Peppa Pig jumper. She was holding something in both hands, and she was whispering to it.

Maya whispered, "That's Dot."

Everyone knew Dot. She had only joined in September, and she was very quiet. She hardly said anything. She spent most of her breaks in the corner of the playground, drawing in the dust with a stick.

Mr Hermes stepped forward very slowly. He said, "Hello, Dot."

Dot jumped. She hugged the bag. She said, "I'm not giving it back."

Mr Hermes said, "That's all right. You don't have to yet."

Dot's eyes were huge. She said, "I didn't want to steal it. I only wanted to look."

Mr Hermes said, "At what?"

Dot looked down at the thing in her hands. It was a **letter**. She said, "You bring everyone letters. Everyone in the whole school. The big kids and the teachers and the dinner ladies. But you never bring me one."

Nobody said anything.

She said, "So I thought if I opened the bag, I might find one. Just one. With my name on it."

## THE LETTER

It was the quietest moment of the whole week. Even the wind stopped.

Felix looked at Alex. Alex looked at Felix. They didn't need to say anything.

Felix stepped forward, took a piece of paper out of his pocket (it was a half-finished drawing of a dragon), and a pencil from behind his ear. He wrote something on the back, very quickly, and folded it up.

At exactly the same moment, Alex took out a crayon and drew something on the back of his hand, then tore a page from his school diary and wrote on that.

They both walked over to Dot and held out their folded notes, one each.

Dot took them, her hands trembling. She unfolded Felix's first.

It said: **"Dear Dot, I think you are brave. Love from Felix. P.S. I like your plaits."**

She unfolded Alex's.

It said: **"Dear Dot, I am glad you are in our school. Love from Alex. P.S. Do you want to play on Tuesday?"**

Dot read them twice. Then three times. Her bottom lip wobbled, and a large tear rolled down her cheek.

She said, "Nobody ever wrote to me before."

Maya sat down beside her. She said, "Now two people have. And I'm going to write one too. And so is Kenji. And Ollie. And Mr Hermes, if he wants."

Mr Hermes said, very softly, "I'd be honoured."

## THE ROOF GETS NOISY

Dot said sorry. She said it about nine times. She said she had only wanted a letter, and she hadn't meant to muddle up the whole world, and she was very, very sorry.

Mr Hermes knelt down. He said, "Dot, do you know the first thing I ever did, when I was a very little god?"

Dot sniffed. "No."

He said, "I took something that wasn't mine. And I got it wrong, and I said sorry, and I made it right. And it turned out okay."

Dot said, "Really?"

He said, "Really. And I became a messenger because of it."

Dot looked at the bag. She wiped her nose on her sleeve. She stood up, picked up the bag, which was far too big for her, and held it out in both arms.

She said, "I'm sorry. Here."

Mr Hermes took it. He said, "Thank you for giving it back. That was a very brave thing to do."

Dot grinned, and it was a wobbly sort of grin, but it was real.

## THE DEADLINE

Then Kenji, who had been quietly counting, gasped.

He said, "It's **TWO FIFTY-FIVE.**"

Everyone froze.

Mr Hermes said, "I've got five minutes to deliver every message in the world."

Maya said, "Can you do it with one sandal?"

He looked at his foot. He looked at the rest of us. He looked at the huge, wobbly fire escape.

Felix said, "You don't need to do it alone."

Alex said, "We can help."

So Mr Hermes opened the bag, and an enormous heap of letters and parcels and messages came tumbling out, in all colours, and every one was addressed to a god. We sorted them in a flash. Kenji read out the names. Ollie held the bundles. Maya made piles. Alex and Felix, side by side, raced up and down the roof, flinging each pile towards Mr Hermes, who caught every one, with his lost sandal tucked in his belt.

Dot's job was the most important: she held the last, enormous parcel, which was addressed to **ZEUS**, and which she had to carry up to the highest point of the roof.

At exactly three o'clock, the last parcel flew out of her hands, and into the sky.

**WHOOOOSH.**

There was a clap of thunder, and a silvery light, and a faint far-off voice that said, "Thank you, Hermes."

Then every message in the world un-muddled all at once.

## EVERYTHING GOES BACK TO NORMAL

The announcements stopped being strange. Mrs Ibbs told us that lunch was fish pie. Mr Thorley fixed the light and went home. Mrs Felicity's raffle tickets turned back into raffle tickets. Mr Jackson took off his coat. Mr Harris's chair was no longer the hamster's.

Mr Hermes tied his sandal back on with a spare bootlace and gave his wings a happy flap, and the cap on his head fluttered like a bird.

He said, "I've been delivering for **three thousand years**, and I've never had help before. I didn't know how nice it was."

Maya said, "That's what friends are for."

Mr Hermes said, "That's what messengers should be for, too."

He reached into his bag and took out a small cream-coloured envelope. He held it out to Dot.

It said: **FOR DOT. FROM EVERYBODY.**

Inside was a drawing from every single child in Year 2, and the teachers, and the dinner ladies. There was a card from Mrs Ibbs, written in hieroglyphs. There was a flower from Miss Persey, and a sticker from Mrs Felicity that said **YOU ARE LUCKY.** There was a very short note in a very big hand that said **WELL DONE, DOT. — THOR.**

Dot read every single one.

When she finished, she said, in a tiny voice, "I'm going to keep them for ever."

Mr Hermes said, "That's exactly what they're for."

## THE LESSON

At home time, Mr Hermes stood by the gate in the sun, and every child stopped to say thank you.

He told us, "The most important thing a messenger does isn't flying fast. It's making sure the right words reach the right person. A kind word is a message too."

Alex said, "Like our notes to Dot."

Felix said, "Like saying thank you."

Mr Hermes said, "Exactly. Say them out loud, and say them often. And one more thing, which is the secret of being a god."

We leaned in.

He said, "Everyone makes mistakes. The trick is to say sorry, make it right, and **keep going.**"

We said it together, all of us, even Dot:

**"I CAN SAY SORRY, MAKE IT RIGHT, AND KEEP GOING."**

Then Mr Hermes tipped his little winged cap, gave a wave, and walked away **backwards** down the road.

Halfway to the corner, he flapped his heels, and with a soft, silver *whoosh*, he lifted off the ground, and was gone.

## THE ENDING (WITH A MANGO)

We were walking out of the gate when Maya stopped.

There was a very big box on the front step of the school. It hadn't been there that morning. It was wrapped in brown paper, tied with string, and covered in stamps from places like INDIA and LANKA and HIMALAYA.

It was addressed to **THE NEW PE TEACHER.**

Alex said, "He forgot to deliver that one."

Felix said, "No. He didn't."

The box rustled. Then, from inside it, a muffled voice said, **"Is that the school? Is there a climbing frame? Does anybody here like bananas?"**

We all stared at the box. Then, slowly, we all looked at the gym, where the lights had just come on.

**The End.**
