The first time we saw Mrs Pandy was on a Monday, and she was ASKING QUESTIONS.

She asked Mr Harris why the gate was green. She asked Mrs Ibbs how many peas were in the pot. She asked the hamster, very politely, what it was thinking about. Then she asked Mr Thorley what was inside his toolbox, and he said, "Spanners," and she said, "All of them?"

She was small and bouncy, with curly hair, paint on her elbows and a necklace with a tiny jar on it. Her badge said **MRS PANDY, ART (AND WONDERING).** She was Mr Epp's wife, and she'd been away on a very long painting trip.

"Welcome home," said Mr Epp. "Did you have a nice time?"

"I had **eleven hundred** questions," said Mrs Pandy, "and I've only answered nine hundred."

## THE BOX

You might remember the box that Mr Hermes delivered at the end of the bonfire story. It was big and wooden, covered in locks, with a note from Pandora that said **DO NOT OPEN.**

Mr Epp had put it in the cupboard in the art room, under a blanket, behind the paint pots.

Mrs Pandy kept looking at that cupboard.

On Tuesday she looked at it during Painting. On Wednesday she looked at it during Clay. She looked so hard that Ollie Grant said her eyes were going to make a hole in it.

Alex and Felix Bailey are twins, so they have a system. Alex notices things and Felix asks about them. Then they swap, and it works every time.

Alex noticed that Mrs Pandy kept touching her little jar necklace whenever she looked at the cupboard. Felix asked, "Mrs Pandy, what's in the box?"

Mrs Pandy said, in a small voice, "I don't know. I was told never to look."

Alex noticed that every time she said "never", she wriggled, like a person who needs the toilet.

They looked at each other and said, "We need Maya."

## WHO IS PANDORA?

Maya Singh was behind the compost bin, with her clipboard. Kenji Mori and Ollie sat on their upside-down buckets, and Dot from Reception sat on the lid.

Maya said, "I've been expecting this. Mrs Pandy is **PANDORA.**"

Kenji said, "Is that a type of pasta?"

Maya said, "NO, Kenji."

She explained that a long time ago in Greece, after Prometheus gave people fire, the gods decided to make a special person. Mr Heston, who is Hephaestus, shaped her out of clay, like a sculptor. Then every god gave her a gift. Athena gave her clever fingers. Hermes gave her a very curious mind. Aphrodite gave her kindness. Her name means **"all gifts"**.

Dot said, "That's a lovely name."

Maya said, "It is. And then she got a jar."

Felix said, "A jar?"

Maya said, "People call it a box now, because of a very old mix-up in translation, but it was a jar, a big one with a lid. It was a present, and it came with one rule. **Don't open it.**"

Ollie said, "That's the worst rule in the world."

Maya said, "I know. The jar was full of things the gods had put away because nobody wanted them. Worries and grumps and squabbles and sad days. If she opened it, they'd fly out all over the world."

Alex said, "So why give it to her?"

Maya said, "That's the bit nobody agrees on."

Kenji said, "What happened?"

Maya said, "She waited and waited. She could hear it **humming**. In the end, she lifted the lid, just a tiny bit, to peek."

She stopped. We all leaned in.

"And everything flew out."

## THE LIST

Alex held the pencil. Felix held the notebook, because they always share.

**1. THE JAR NECKLACE.** She's never without it.

**2. THE QUESTIONS.** Maya counted forty-one before morning break.

**3. THE CLEVER FINGERS.** Everything she paints looks real. Her apple looked so real that Mr Harris tried to eat it.

**4. THE HUMMING.** Felix put his ear to the cupboard door. It was humming a low, muffled, grumbly tune.

**5. THE NOTE.** In Mr Epp's pocket was the message that came with the box. It said, in curly writing: *Keep it shut until I'm home. Love, P. P.S. Epimetheus, I mean it.*

Maya said, "That's a very good list."

Dot put up her hand. "Is she bad?"

Maya said, "No, Dot. Curiosity isn't bad. It's how everybody learns anything. It just needs a bit of **asking first**."

## MRS PANDY TRIES

On Thursday, Mrs Pandy tried very, very hard not to look at the cupboard. She painted with her back to it. She taught a whole lesson with her eyes shut. She sat on her hands.

But at home time, when everyone had gone, she crept back into the art room on her own.

We know, because Felix had left his lunchbox behind and went back for it. And Alex noticed Felix hadn't come out, and went after him.

They stood side by side in the doorway.

## THE HUMMING

Mrs Pandy was kneeling in front of the cupboard, with the blanket pulled off. The box sat there, covered in locks, humming a tune.

It sounded like voices, a thousand tiny ones all at once.

*"Let us out… it's stuffy in here… just a little peek… we only want to say hello…"*

Mrs Pandy said, very quietly, "I'm only going to listen."

She put her ear against the lid. The voices got louder.

*"Please… we're squashed… we've been in here for ever… you're the only one who's nice enough to help…"*

Her hand crept towards the first lock.

Alex whispered, "Mrs Pandy?"

She jumped. She said, "Oh! You two! I was just..."

Felix said, "You were going to open it."

Mrs Pandy's eyes filled with tears. She said, "I **want** to know what's inside. It's the only thing in the world I don't know. It's so hard not knowing."

Alex said, "We know about that. We have a word for it."

Felix said, "**Yet.** I don't know yet."

She whispered, "But I want to **now.**"

Alex said, "We know. Do you want to say something with us instead?"

They said it together, very kindly:

**"STOP. BREATHE. THINK."**

She took a deep breath. She took her hand away. She whispered, "Thank you."

They thought that was the end of it.

## THE CLICK

But at that exact moment, there was a loud **SNAP.**

Not from Mrs Pandy. From the box.

It was the last lock. It had been getting looser all week, because the thing inside was pushing and pushing. It sprang open, and the lid flew up, and a long, thin wisp of grey smoke curled out.

Mrs Pandy shouted, "NO!"

She dived for the lid, but a whole cloud poured out of the box. Hundreds of tiny, grey, fluttery creatures, like moths made of fog, swirled round the art room, giggling and grumbling and shrieking.

They flew straight through the window, and the walls, and the ceiling, and were gone.

The art room went silent. A paintbrush fell off a shelf.

Mrs Pandy sat on the floor, with her hands over her mouth.

She said, "I didn't open it."

Alex said, "We know."

Felix said, "It popped open by itself."

She said, "But I **wanted** to. I wanted it so much. And now I've let them out."

## THE FIRST GRUMBLE

At first, nothing seemed different.

But on Friday morning, Mr Harris came into assembly with a very sour face. He said, "Good morning, Year 1. I'd like to say that I'm in a **very bad mood**, and I don't know why."

Everyone stared. Mr Harris is **never** grumpy before ten o'clock.

Mr Jackson shushed three children for **breathing** in the library. Mr Thorley slammed his toolbox shut and growled at a leaf. Mrs Ibbs put "NOTHING IS RIGHT" on the menu board.

Then Ollie Grant said, "Kenji's looking at me."

Kenji said, "I'm **not.**"

Ollie said, "You are."

Kenji said, "You're being silly."

Ollie said, "**YOU'RE** being silly."

And then they had the biggest argument of their lives, over absolutely nothing.

## THE FIVE

By break, the whole school was in a state.

There were **five** kinds of grey wisps floating about. Maya saw them first, through the window, and she drew pictures of each one on her clipboard.

**THE GRUMBLES** were round and puffy, and they sat on people's shoulders, whispering, "Everything is annoying." Anybody they touched got very cross.

**THE SQUABBLES** were thin and zigzaggy, and they zipped between friends, making them argue about who was first and who was best.

**THE WORRIES** were tiny and shivery, and they crept into people's ears, saying, "What if it all goes wrong? What if you're not good enough? What if?"

**THE FIBS** were slippery and quick, and they tickled people's noses until they said things that weren't true. Dev from Year 3 told Mrs Tibbs a dragon ate his homework, and the dragon wasn't even real.

**THE SADS** were big and heavy and slow, and they hung over people like rain clouds. Anybody they touched wanted to lie on the floor.

Maya whispered, "They're all out of the jar. That's the whole set."

Kenji said, in a small voice, "Mr Hanu has a Sad."

We looked. He was sitting on the climbing frame with his head on his knees, and a huge grey cloud was sitting on top of him.

## THE GODS TRY

The gods tried everything.

Mr Thorley said he would **blow them away with thunder.** Everyone said, "No!" and he looked even grumpier.

Mr Hermes flew round the school, trying to deliver the Fibs back to wherever they came from, but they kept giggling out of his bag.

Mr Shah tried dancing, but a Grumble sat on his head and he sulked.

Mrs Felicity tried sharing her basket, and a Squabble made two children fight over a grape.

Ms Athene tried asking clever questions, and a Worry whispered, "What if you get the answer wrong?" and she went quiet.

Mr Prome said, "I can't think ahead. The Worries have got my forethought." Mr Epp said, "Oh, **now** I'm thinking about it. This is awful."

By lunchtime, the whole school was in a mess. Dinner ladies were arguing. Teachers were sulking. Year 1 were sitting in a heap on the field, and not one of them wanted to play.

Mrs Pandy sat in the art room, with her head in her hands. She said, "It's my fault. I should never have wondered."

## THE TWINS

Alex and Felix hadn't been touched.

They didn't know why. They only knew that they were still all right, and that everyone else wasn't.

They sat under the oak tree, side by side.

Alex said, "Why aren't we grumpy?"

Felix said, "I think it's because we're looking after each other."

Alex said, "Maybe the grey things only get you when you're on your own."

Felix said, "Maybe that's how we fix it."

They looked at each other.

Alex said, "Maya said the jar was full of the things nobody wanted."

Felix said, "But if the jar was full of **bad** things, why was it a present?"

Alex said, "Maybe there's something else in it."

They said, together, "**Yet.**"

They ran back to the art room.

## THE BOTTOM OF THE JAR

Mrs Pandy was sitting on the floor, still, with the empty box in front of her.

Alex said, "Mrs Pandy, can we look in the box?"

She said, "It's empty. They've all gone."

Felix said, "Please. Just in case."

She sighed, and nodded.

Alex held the lid. Felix reached in. The inside was dark and grey, and it smelled of damp socks. They felt around the bottom.

Felix's fingers found something small.

It was **warm.**

He lifted it out, very gently, in both hands.

## THE BIRD

It was the tiniest bird they had ever seen. It was about the size of a thumb. It had feathers like golden sparks, and a beak like a pin, and a body that glowed a soft, steady yellow, like a candle behind a curtain.

It sat on Felix's hand, and it blinked at them with two shining eyes.

Mrs Pandy gasped. She said, in a whisper, "It's **Hope.**"

The little bird chirped. It was a very small chirp, like a bell on a mouse's collar.

Alex said, "She stayed in the jar."

Felix said, "She didn't fly out with the others."

Mrs Pandy said, "She never does. She stays at the bottom, waiting. Nobody ever told me that part."

She held out her finger. The bird hopped onto it.

She said, "She's very small."

Alex said, "She doesn't need to be big."

Felix said, "She needs to be **shared.**"

## HOW TO CATCH A GRUMBLE

They carried Hope out into the playground, one hand each, close together. She glowed a bit brighter as they went, and wherever the light touched, something happened.

The first one they met was Ollie, who was glaring at Kenji. A Squabble was sitting on his nose.

Hope fluttered over, and landed on his shoulder. She gave a tiny chirp.

The Squabble shrivelled up like a raisin, and floated off in a puff.

Ollie blinked. He said, "Kenji, I'm sorry. I don't know why I was cross."

Kenji said, "I'm sorry too. I don't know why I was either."

They hugged.

Alex whispered, "She works."

Felix whispered, "But there are hundreds of them. And only one of her."

## THE BIG IDEA

They stood in the middle of the playground, looking at the whole school, covered in grey. They didn't know what to do.

Dot came and stood between them. She was very small, and her plaits were messy, and she had a little grey Worry sitting on her head. But she took the twins' hands anyway.

She said, "Hope is little. But maybe it gets bigger when we all have some."

Alex and Felix looked at each other. They both grinned.

Alex said, "Dot, that's it!"

Felix said, "We don't need to catch them all. We need to give everyone a bit of **hope.**"

Alex said, "And we already know how."

Felix said, "The words."

## THE WORDS

They stood on the climbing frame, one on each side, and they shouted so that the whole school could hear.

Alex shouted, "Everybody! There's still something good!"

Felix shouted, "And it's in **you!**"

They held up the little golden bird, and the glow spread across the field, soft and warm.

Alex said the first words, and Felix said the second. Then they swapped.

"**I AM A SEED. THE DARK IS WHERE I GROW.**"

A Sad shrank.

"**I CAN BE SCARED AND BRAVE AT THE SAME TIME.**"

A Worry shivered and fell off Dot's head.

"**I CAN SAY SORRY, MAKE IT RIGHT, AND KEEP GOING.**"

A Squabble popped like a bubble.

"**I DON'T KNOW YET.**"

A Fib turned bright pink and floated away, giggling.

"**I KNOW YOU CAN.**"

A Grumble flopped off Mr Hanu's shoulder, and he leaped to his feet.

"**I AM NOT ALONE.**"

The biggest Sad of all, the one hanging over the oak tree, gave a long, wobbly sigh. It sank down, down, down, and settled on the grass like morning mist, and then it melted away.

## HOPE SPREADS

One by one, the children said the words. Then the teachers. Then the dinner ladies, and the gods, and the mums and dads, who had come to see what the fuss was about.

And with every word, the little golden bird glowed a bit brighter, and a bit bigger. She grew from the size of a thumb to the size of a robin, to the size of a swan. Her feathers shone like the sun. She spread her wings over the whole school, and a warm golden light poured down like honey.

Every grey wisp that was left curled up and drifted away, and was gone.

Nobody said a word. Then Mr Harris, who had a smile on his face for the first time all day, said, "Well. That was a rotten morning."

Everyone burst out laughing.

## MRS PANDY SAYS SORRY

Mrs Pandy was standing at the edge of the field with her hands clasped. Her eyes were red. She walked slowly up to Mr Harris.

She said, "I'm so sorry. I let the grumbles out. I should have listened to the note."

Mr Harris looked at her kindly. He said, "You were curious."

She said, "Curiosity isn't an excuse."

Mr Harris said, "No. But it's a very good thing. You asked questions, and you wanted to know, and I'm sure it will lead to lots of wonderful art." He paused. "You just need to ask **first.** And be prepared to wait."

She said, "I will. I promise."

Mr Epp came up and took her hand. He said, "I'm sorry I put the box in the cupboard where you could hear it. I should've thought."

She said, "You did think. Eventually."

Everybody laughed again.

She turned to Alex and Felix. She knelt, and took a hand each.

She said, "Thank you. You found the one thing everybody forgot."

Alex said, "It was in the bottom of the jar."

Felix said, "It always is."

## THE LESSON

At home time, we sat under the oak tree. The little golden bird, now back to the size of a thumb, perched on Mrs Pandy's shoulder.

Mrs Pandy said, "I'll tell you something the old stories forgot. Everyone remembers the bad things that flew out. But the most important thing stayed inside."

Maya said, "Hope."

Mrs Pandy said, "Yes. Even when things go wrong, and they sometimes will, there's always something left at the bottom. A little bit of light. A friend. A good idea."

Alex said, "And it grows when you share it."

Felix said, "Like the fire."

Mrs Pandy said, "Just like the fire."

We said it together, with the little bird chirping along:

**"THERE IS ALWAYS HOPE."**

## THE ENDING (WITH A SHELL)

On the way out, we noticed that the school pond, the little one behind the Reception classroom, was making a noise.

It was a bubbling, burbling, rumbling sound, as though something very big was waking up.

We ran over and peered in. The surface rippled. A pair of huge, wise, sleepy eyes opened under the water.

Then, slowly, a very large, very old head rose out of the pond. Behind it came a huge, dome-shaped shell, covered in waterweed.

It was a **turtle.** It was as big as a table.

It looked at us, and in a slow, deep, creaky voice, it said:

**"Excuse me. Is this the school with the Chinese goddess? I've come to hold up the sky."**

We all stared.

Maya whispered, "That's **AO.**"

Kenji whispered, "From the Nüwa story!"

Alex looked at Felix. Felix looked at Alex.

They said, together, "**Monday.**"

**The End.**
