# Story consistency check

Read `docs/Story_Bible.md` and `docs/Story_Archive.md` fresh, then check the outline or draft against every item below. Report a list of **real problems** with the exact passage and a suggested fix, then a short list of **minor notes**. Do not rewrite the story unless asked.

## Standing rules
- [ ] Cast matches the Bible's current cast rules (Alex, Felix, Willow, Maya; Dot, Kenji, Ollie, Mister Harris and others as tertiary). No Elfine or Marion unless the user asked for them.
- [ ] Alex and Felix both speak, both act, and share every big moment. Neither is the follower throughout.
- [ ] Willow does one brave or clever thing and never speaks.
- [ ] Maya works out who the god is and tells a real myth from the tradition.
- [ ] A child "we" narrator throughout, with a few "That's when" turns.
- [ ] About 4,000 words in 16 to 18 parts, each with a `## Part N. Title` heading.
- [ ] Reading level Year 2 to 3: short sentences, short paragraphs, a few big words carried by context.
- [ ] An affirmation woven in, spoken by the god late in the story. No notes page, no questions for the children, nothing after "The End."
- [ ] The story opens on the previous hook (or the user's chosen god) and ends with a new, unnamed hook and the twins saying "Monday" at exactly the same time.

## Continuity
- [ ] The god's badge follows the format "Title, School Job" and does not clash with a badge already in the roster.
- [ ] Any returning staff are described as in earlier stories (check the roster and the story text). Facts that have changed since (Pachamama's meadow, the sky seam, Soup Club, the Lost Property cupboard, the apple trees) are respected.
- [ ] Days and settings make sense: stories begin on a Monday morning; the hook leads to the next Monday.
- [ ] A new staff member does not duplicate an existing job without a joke or a reason (Maui's badge already says Lost Property; Simurgh runs the cupboard).
- [ ] Anything new that should be remembered (a place, an object, a rule) is noted for the Bible.

## Myth and sensitivity
- [ ] The myth is recognisably the real one, told gently. Say which parts were changed for children.
- [ ] No child sacrifice, torture, graphic death, or romance. Dark episodes are softened or replaced.
- [ ] Gods from living religions are shown as good and wise, never mocked. Practices (offerings, festivals, names) are described correctly and respectfully.
- [ ] Names, places and terms are correct (check spellings and which language they belong to).
- [ ] The tradition has not been covered recently (check the tracker).

## Variety
- [ ] The moral differs from the last three stories.
- [ ] The main set-piece differs from the last two (a leaning tower, a child in peril, a bickering staff meeting, a sunset speech by a bench).
- [ ] The peril is real but resolved with kindness, asking or sharing, not force.
