The first time we properly saw Mrs Ibbs was on a Monday, and she was CORRECTING THE MENU.

We all remembered the dinner lady with the long, thin nose who waved at us from the kitchen window at the end of the Anubis story. Nell Brooks had said, "I think we might meet him next," and now we had.

She was standing on a stool in the dinner hall with a red pen, and she was crossing out words on the menu board. Where it said **CHIKEN NUGETS** she wrote **CHICKEN NUGGETS**. Where it said **SPAGETTI**, she wrote **SPAGHETTI**. Then she stepped back and nodded, with her head going **forwards and backwards** like a pigeon, and said, "Much better."

Mr Harris said, "Er, that's our menu."

Mrs Ibbs said, "It was. Now it's correct."

## THE ONE LEG

Nobody could stop watching her after that.

She stirred the gravy standing on ONE LEG, with the other foot tucked up. She served the peas standing on one leg. She did the washing up on one leg, and she didn't wobble AT ALL.

Kofi Mensah said, "Is she doing yoga?"

Nell said, "No. I think she's doing BIRD."

At lunch, Mrs Ibbs leaned over the counter, peered at Kofi's plate with one eye, then the other eye, and said, "You have fourteen peas. Last week you had sixteen. Are you well?"

Kofi said, "How do you KNOW that?"

Mrs Ibbs said, "I count things. It's a hobby."

## WHO IS THOTH?

At break, Nell called an emergency meeting behind the climbing frame, and she had a very serious face.

She said, "Mrs Ibbs is THOTH."

Archie Bell said, "Is that a sneeze?"

Nell said that Thoth was one of the most important gods in ancient Egypt, and that you say it a bit like "Toht", though nobody agrees exactly, so you shouldn't worry about it.

She said that Thoth has the head of a bird called an **ibis**, which has a very long, curved beak and long thin legs, and which stands in the mud by the river Nile, poking about for food. (Archie said, "Mrs Ibbs. Ibis. Ibbs." Nell said, "YES, Archie, well done.")

Nell said that Thoth was the god of **writing**, **numbers**, **wisdom** and the **moon**.

Kofi said, "That's four jobs."

Nell said, "He's very busy."

## THE SCRIBE OF THE GODS

Nell said that the Egyptians believed Thoth invented WRITING. Before that, nobody could write anything down, so everybody forgot everything all the time, and arguments went on for hundreds of years.

She said Thoth made up **hieroglyphs**, which are tiny pictures that stand for words and sounds. There's a little picture of an owl, a wavy line for water, and a reed for a letter. He's the scribe of the gods, which means he writes everything down for them, with a reed pen and a palette of ink.

Archie said, "Like a school secretary?"

Nell said, "Like a school secretary who invented secretaries."

Then she remembered something and went very pale.

She said, "Remember Mr Jackson's phone call? He said, 'Thoth, could you write it down?' Thoth writes down the result when Anubis weighs your heart. He keeps the record. And she's been standing in the kitchen WRITING DOWN WHAT WE EAT."

We all looked at the kitchen window. Mrs Ibbs was holding a clipboard. She waved.

## THE LIST

Nell said that a good theory needs a list. Kofi had a pencil.

**1. THE BEAK.** Mrs Ibbs has a very long, thin, curved nose. Archie said it was as long as a banana. Nell said it was NOT as long as a banana, but it was longer than a pencil.

**2. THE FEET.** She stands on one leg. Ibises do that. Nobody knows why, but they do.

**3. THE PEN.** She never uses a biro. She has a long pointy reed pen, and she dips it in a little pot of black ink. She wrote "NO RUNNING" on the dinner hall door with it, and it looked like a work of art.

**4. THE COUNTING.** She counted the sprouts on every plate, and when Ollie tried to hide two under his fork, she said, "Eleven. I counted eleven. Where are the other two?"

**5. THE MOON.** She had a small notebook with a picture of the moon on it, and she looked at the sky every lunchtime, even on cloudy days.

Archie said, "It's a very long list."

Nell said, "It's the best I've ever seen."

## THE HIEROGLYPHS

On Tuesday, we decided to test her.

Nell said we couldn't ask Mrs Ibbs if she was Thoth directly, because gods like to keep it quiet. But we could ask her to WRITE something for us.

So at the hatch, Kofi asked, "Mrs Ibbs, could you write my name?"

She smiled. She took out her reed pen and a scrap of paper, and she wrote four little pictures: a **basket**, a **bird**, a **reed**, and a **wavy line.**

It looked wonderful. It also didn't look like Kofi at all.

She said, "That's your name in hieroglyphs. The sounds are K-O-F-I, as near as I can get."

Kofi stared at it. Then he whispered, "That's the coolest thing anyone's ever done."

He carried it round for the rest of the day, and he showed it to a big kid from the class above, who said, "That's just doodling," and Kofi said, "NO. It's WRITING. It's the oldest kind."

## THE LITTLE DICE GAME

On Wednesday, something strange happened.

Mr Harris came into the dinner hall holding a calendar and looking quite worried. He said, "Mrs Ibbs, there's a problem. According to the school timetable, we've got **five extra minutes** every day that we can't fit anything into. I don't know where they came from."

Mrs Ibbs said, "I can help with that."

She took out a little pair of dice from her apron. She said, "Do you play?"

Mr Harris said, "A bit."

They rolled. Mr Harris got a two and a one. Mrs Ibbs got double six.

They rolled again. Mr Harris got a three and a one. Mrs Ibbs got double six.

They rolled **five** more times, and every time, Mr Harris lost, and Mrs Ibbs got something wonderful. Mr Harris scratched his head, and a faint, silvery glow settled over the dinner hall, like moonlight.

Nell stood perfectly still and whispered, **"IT'S THE MOON GAME."**

## THE MOON GAME

She explained very quickly.

She said that long ago, the sky goddess Nut was going to have babies, but a very angry god had said she was NOT allowed to have them on ANY day of the year. And there were only three hundred and sixty days in the year back then.

But Thoth loved Nut, and he wanted to help her.

So he went up to the moon, which was shining brightly, and he challenged the moon to a game of **dice**. Each time Thoth won, the moon had to give him a tiny bit of its moonlight.

Thoth won, and won, and won, and it added up to **five whole days**. He used them to make five extra days in the Egyptian year that didn't belong to anyone. And on those five days, Nut was allowed to have her babies, in peace.

Kofi said, "That's the BEST dice game ever."

Nell said, "That's how the Egyptians got three hundred and sixty-five days."

Archie said, "So she's got five MINUTES from Mr Harris, because she's ALWAYS winning those."

Nell said, "She is STILL doing it. She does it every time."

## THE FIVE MINUTES

When we went outside after lunch, Mr Harris rang the bell **five minutes later** than usual.

"I don't understand it," he said. "I could have sworn I rang it on time."

So we had five extra minutes of playtime that afternoon, and we used them to play a very good game of football with a very flat ball. Nobody mentioned it to Mr Harris, and he never worked out where the time went.

## THE PHONE CALL (AGAIN)

On Thursday, we heard Mrs Ibbs on the phone behind the kitchen.

She said, "Anubis. Yes. Hello, dear. No, no, I'm fine. I've been writing down everything. The little ones have been quite tidy. Very few spelling mistakes in the dinner hall, though I did find a **'sossage'**. Yes. I corrected it."

She listened for a moment.

"What? A loud man? Yes, I've seen him. A hammer, a red beard. He keeps knocking on the stage and doing 'a bit of thunder'. And another one? The thin one with the eyebrows? Oh, yes. I know. He did something to the lion. I've written that down, too."

She turned around, and she saw all four of us standing in the doorway.

She said, "Ah. You've heard that."

Nell said, "You're THOTH."

Mrs Ibbs looked at us for a very long time, with her head tilted. Then she said, in a soft, rustling voice, "I am. But I shall write that I never said so."

## THE TRUTH ABOUT WORDS

She sat down on a stool, put her clipboard on her knee and looked at each of us in turn.

She said, "Do you know why writing matters?"

We all shook our heads.

She said, "Because it keeps things. Think of everything you've learned today. All the kind things, all the clever things, all the funny things. If nobody writes them down, they get forgotten. But if you write them, a child five thousand years from now can read them, and know what you thought and felt."

Kofi looked down at his hieroglyph name.

Mrs Ibbs said, "That's why I'm here. To help you notice the good bits, and write them down."

She took out three more pieces of paper and her reed pen, and she wrote something on each one, in neat little pictures. When she handed them out, we saw they were the names of our favourite things.

Mine was a picture of a **cat**, a **bowl** and a **star**. Mrs Ibbs said, "I think that says 'Cat Dinner Wish.' I'm not quite sure. Hieroglyphs are tricky."

Archie got a picture of a **ball** and **three feathers**.

Nell got a picture of a **book**, an **eye**, and a **crown**. She stared at it for a long time. Then she whispered, "It says 'Clever Reader Queen'."

Mrs Ibbs said, "It says what it says."

## THE ENDING (BUT NOT THE END)

On Friday, on the way to PE, we went past the gym cupboard.

It was dark and a bit smelly, and there was a very big pile of beanbags. And inside the beanbags, something was **moving**.

It was quick and furry and brown. It leaped from one beanbag to another, and then it jumped up onto a high shelf, where it hung upside down by its **tail**.

It was a **MONKEY.**

We all gasped. Kofi said, "That's NOT a hamster."

Archie said, "That's the biggest hamster in the world."

Nell turned to us, with her eyes like dinner plates. She said, "Thoth is sometimes drawn as a baboon too. But **this** isn't a baboon. This is something else."

At that moment, the monkey took a deep, deep breath, and in a very small voice, it said:

**"Does anyone have a mango?"**

We all stared at it. We looked at each other. Then very slowly, Archie held out his lunchbox.

The monkey grinned.

**The End.**
