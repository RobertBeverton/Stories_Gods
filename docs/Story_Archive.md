# Story Archive

Every story written, in order, 1 to 38, with its details. Stories 1 to 18 are the school-setting stories written first; stories 19 to 38 followed. All the closing-scene quotes are taken from the story files. Add a new entry at the bottom for each new story (the next is number 39): cast, length, summary, the god's words, the hook, and one honest note about what to watch for next time.

## What to watch for across the series

- **Length drift.** Stories 1 to 7 are short (about 1,000 to 2,000 words). From story 8 they run about 2,400 to 4,450 words, and from story 19 about 4,000 to 4,700. Pele's (25) was expanded to about 4,200.
- **Hook chaining.** Most stories follow the previous hook. Story 21's hook (Hadad) was not followed immediately, because Raven and Coyote were asked for first.
- **Cast changes.** Elfine and Marion are retired from story 30 onwards.
- **Repeating beats.** Tower or structure about to fall, a small child in danger and rescued by the twins, a staff meeting that goes wrong, and a sunset speech have all been used many times. Vary them.
- **Repeated phrases.** "Underlined it three times", "at exactly the same time" (this one is the ritual), "looked at each other". Keep the ritual, vary the rest.
- **Twin balance.** Check each time: lines, actions, who goes first.

## Notes on the order and the early stories

The order of stories 1 to 18 was reconstructed from the hooks and clues inside the texts, and the files' `seriesOrder` matches it. It is not 100% confirmed. The one open pair is Felicitas (4) and the Seven Lucky Gods (5), which could swap. See "Order check" below. Stories 19 to 38 are in the order they were written and published.

- **Where the facts come from.** File, word count, headed sections, badge, cast, closing scene and hook for stories 1 to 18 are taken from the story files. "Not recorded" means the file does not show it; the only field still not recorded for stories 1 to 18 is the date written (the files carry no date).
- **Word counts** for stories 1 to 18 are the body of the file after the front matter, headings included, counted by script. **Sections** are the `## ` headings; each story also has an untitled opening before the first heading. Stories 1 to 18 use plain headings such as `## THE LIST`, not `## Part N. Title`.
- **Not in the series** because they are not set in the school: Shiva and the Blue Throat, The Moon in the Tree, The Thunder Drummer Who Went Quiet, The Winter Visitor Who Kept Changing His Coat, The Friend Who Came Back, and the Celtic, Slavic and other stand-alone myths.
- **Early cast.** Stories 1 to 7 use a different class (Dev, Ruby, Priya, Nell, Kofi, Poppy, Archie, Freddie and others) and an unnamed "we" narrator. Maya's compost-bin briefings start in story 8. The "twins have a system" paragraph (Alex notices, Felix asks, then they swap) first appears in story 9 (Hermes), and Pan (11) uses it without the opening sentence. The twins are named Alex Bailey and Felix Bailey in the files. Dot (Reception) first appears in story 9.
- **Format change.** Stories 1 to 7 are short (about 1,000 to 2,000 words). From story 8 they run about 2,400 to 4,450 words. The shift to the full 4,000-word form started at story 17 (about 4,450 words). None of stories 1 to 18 has a notes page or questions afterwards. Affirmations are spoken inside the story from story 8 onwards (8 is the first).
- **Closing ritual.** The twins say "Monday" at exactly the same time only in stories 11, 16, 17 and 18. Story 13 has Athena say "Monday". Stories 10 and 12 have no hook, and stories 14 and 15 end with a different shared word ("Yes", "STOP. BREATHE. THINK!").

### 1. The Caretaker Who Was Thor
- **File:** `stories/the-caretaker-who-was-thor.md`
- **God / tradition:** Thor, Norse
- **Staff on the badge:** Mr Thorley, Caretaker
- **Cast:** Alex (leads), Felix, Maya, Kenji, Ollie, Willow, Mrs Tibbs (class teacher), Mister Harris, Mr Pickering (lollipop man)
- **Length:** about 4,540 words in 18 parts (`## Part N.` headings). Rewritten in place with the series cast; the old short version used a different class.
- **Summary:** Clue trail (Thorley is Thor plus ley, the BOOM and pigeon, the hammer test, pillow pies and jug, the goat-cheese van), Maya's famous "strongest list" (item 19, "he smells a bit like goat", read out with Mr Thorley behind the compost bin and never mentioned again), and her telling of Thor failing to lift the "cat" at Utgard-Loki's hall (it was the World Serpent). Prank rain clouds (the seed of story 6) plague the school; Mr Thorley murmurs "Not tonight, brother" at the gate and thunder cracks two seconds after he drives off. On Friday a huge cat-shaped cloud sits on the hall roof; Mr Thorley will not ask for help. Alex sees it is not heavy, only wide; the twins say "PLEASE" together; the school lifts it off with the PE parachute. Willow faces the cloud's mist tail.
- **Affirmation:** "Asking for a hand is a strong thing to do." Echoed by the twins ("it's strong to ask" / "and strong to say yes").
- **Closing scene:** Mr Thorley on the ladder with tea, Willow lets him scratch her ears once, Maya adds "Number twenty. He asked."
- **Hook at the end:** from somewhere very high in the school a tiny drum goes dum-dum, and the clouds, the pigeons and the grown-ups all go still; Mr Thorley bows his head as if to an old friend (Shiva). Alex and Felix say "Monday" together. "The End."
- **Open thread:** "Who is his brother? Unknown." is left for story 6.
- **Watch:** the storm-and-roof peril is a gentler version of the "building about to fall" beat; the cloud is harmless in the end.

### 2. The Music Teacher Who Was Shiva
- **File:** `stories/the-music-teacher-who-was-shiva.md`
- **God / tradition:** Shiva, Hindu
- **Staff on the badge:** Mr Shah, Music Teacher; Mrs Parvati Rao, School Secretary
- **Cast:** Alex and Felix (Felix leads), Maya, Willow, Kenji, Ollie, Elfine and Marion (big kids, helping at the soup), Mr Harris, Mrs Tibbs, Mr Thorley, Mr Pickering, Mrs Rao, Ganesh Rao (a few classes above), Mrs Dunn (dinner lady), the reptile man and Brenda the snake; cameo Ms Fortune
- **Length:** about 5,250 words in 17 parts. Rewritten in full in the series cast (the first version was a 1,177-word sketch with a different class).
- **Summary:** A tiny drum high in the school calms everyone; Mr Shah sits on the grass with a pigeon on his head. Maya's list (still for twenty minutes and a pigeon, the damaru, the blue scarf in the heat, wet hair, Brenda the Science Week snake) names Shiva, and she tells the Great Churning and the poison he held in his throat. After the dance in assembly and the family clue-hunt (Mrs Parvati Rao, Ganesh and his laddoo), Mr Harris's "everybody's soup" (everyone adds one thing, so it is nobody's) throws up a green bubble that pops into a sleepy green fog. Grown-ups run, fan it and blame each other. Felix notices it only spreads where the running goes and stands on a chair to say "stand still"; Willow sits on the spinning paddle until it stops; Alex fetches Mrs Rao through the fog; the twins together lift the ladle so Mr Shah can drink the poison, with Mrs Rao's hand on his throat. Ganesh clears the path. Mrs Dunn is thanked for a "very brave soup" and everyone says sorry.
- **Affirmation:** "Calm is a kind of strong." Felix tries it on Alex and the pencil.
- **Closing scene** (Part Sixteen): the twins sit still with him for a minute, pigeon on his head. At home time: "Kenji said that Mr Shah had only been wearing a **normal blue scarf**. And Maya said, "Yes. Exactly. *Normal.*""
- **Hook at the end:** a perfectly straight line of lost things (glove, sock, old lunchbox, a sleepy hamster in a sock) leads to the library door, with a tall, long-nosed, pointy-eared shadow walking on two legs and one long sniff in the dark. The twins say "Monday". Sets up Anubis (story 3).
- **Watch:** the soup is the comic seed and the green fog the real peril, echoing the poison coming first and nobody owning it. Elfine and Marion appear here, before story 21 introduces them as new.

## Continuity conflicts to decide

Each recommendation below is a recommendation only. Nothing in the stories has been changed.

1. **Nüwa appears twice: story 17 and story 33.**
   - Story 17 (Ms Wa, "Pottery (and People)"): a jade-green skirt that swishes like a tail; the crack is made by Mr Gong running into the playground flagpole after a fight with Mr Zhu; Ao the turtle holds the sky up; five stones are found by the twins, each given freely, and Ms Wa melts them; Ms Wa says she "used up all five stones last time". It happens in November, with the early cast and no Willow.
   - Story 33 (Mrs Nuwa, "Art and Clay"): a green-gold snake tail; the crack appears overnight with no cause shown (Maya says "two powerful beings had a terrible fight"); no turtle and no Zhu or Gong; Mrs Nuwa already has five big stones on her table; the sky is mended with a quilt of every staff member's creation story, told into the stones; Mr Harris calls her "our new Art teacher"; the whole staff is the later cast (Maui, Oshun, Raven, Lakshmi, Bast, Simurgh, Coyote, Hadad, Pele, Baba Yaga and others) and Willow is present.
   - Both retell the same ancient myth (clay people, then five stones mending the sky) and both open as if she were new. Neither mentions the other. Story 33 says the patch "worked the last time, thousands of years ago" and that this crack is "wider than before", which fits the ancient mending but ignores story 17's recent one.
   - **Recommendation:** treat story 17 as the first modern mending and story 33 as a second crack. Add one line to story 33 so Mrs Nuwa and Ms Wa are clearly the same woman with a new badge (or change the badge to Ms Wa) and mention that the last mending used a turtle. Alternatively, if you want one canonical Nüwa story, keep story 33 and recast story 17 as an earlier story outside the Staff Room. Do not renumber either.
   - **Decided:** keep both stories. Story 33 now has Mister Harris recognise Mrs Nuwa as Ms Wa, the pottery lady with the turtle, and she says she has been promoted. The sky has cracked twice.
2. **Sky mended in story 17, but the Bible and story 18 say it was mended once.** Story 18 says "Since the sky had been mended, it had rained every day". Story 33 then mends it again. **Recommendation:** state in the Bible that the sky cracked twice, and keep the rain in story 18 tied to story 17.
3. **Thor and Loki: stories 1 and 6 versus story 37.** In stories 1 and 6 the caretaker Mr Thorley and the supply teacher Mr Locke are Thor and Loki and already known to the children. Story 37 introduces Mister Thor (Hammer and Heavy Lifting) and Mister Loki (Magic Tricks) as new arrivals from Asgard, and Maya has to explain them. Nobody mentions Mr Thorley or Mr Locke. The Caretaker with the hammer is still around in stories 31, 34, 35 and 36 ("the Caretaker"). **Recommendation:** make the story 37 pair the same gods, with one recognition line (for example Maya or Dot says "That's Mr Thorley's other badge"). If you would rather keep story 37 untouched, record in the Bible that Mr Thorley and Mr Locke are their school names and Mister Thor and Mister Loki are their Asgard badges.
   - **Decided:** use Mister Thor and Mister Loki as the names in all new text. Stories 1 and 6 keep Mr Thorley and Mr Locke as their disguise names.
4. **Story 18 (Yu) hooks into story 19.** These agree: the feather, the puddle, the "school with the corn" line and Saturday. Differences are small: story 19 uses "Mister" and "Missus" where stories 1 to 18 use "Mr" and "Mrs", and it says Missus Tibbs called "Home time!" before the snake slid down the drain, which story 18 does not show. **Recommendation:** no change; this is a clean join.
