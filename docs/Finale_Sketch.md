# Finale sketch, version 2: working title "The Thin Place" (8 chapters)

Status: sketch only, light touch. Not canon until the user approves. Version 1 (a war god quits and a door opens) was dropped at the user's request: the school drifting towards the gods' world, and a swap, is the engine instead. Persona and adversarial review notes are at the bottom, with fixes already folded in.

## Shape

- 8 chapters, each about 4,000 to 5,000 words (15 to 20 minutes), about 2 to 2.25 hours in all.
- Each chapter works as a night's story: a one-line "last time" at the start and a small settled ending before the hook.
- Child "we" narrator throughout, with the twins always together, so the point of view never has to leave them. Leads alternate, Felix first (Alex "notices", Felix "asks", then swap).
- Ending: no next-god hook. The last "Monday" points at the new term and the new little ones. Elfine and Marion cameo in chapter 8 only.
- Arc placement (decided): the arc **replaces the stand-alone Mama Quilla story** and runs as stories 40 to 47. Story 39's hook (the moon sitting too low over the roof, a silver drop on Willow's nose) becomes the first sign of the thin place in chapter 1: the moon is low because the gods' sky is pressing on the school's. Mama Quilla is folded in as the moon, who is crying because she can't tell which sky is hers; she lights the way home in chapter 7 and goes back up in chapter 8. She has a small part and is not a full story of her own.
- The tracker's "aim for story 50 to 55" now becomes "finish at 47", with the reserve gods unneeded. That is the user's call; update `God_Tracker.md` and the Bible's "What is waiting next" once this is approved.

## The idea

With nearly forty gods on the staff, the school is getting heavy. Where one world is heavy, it presses against the other, and where two places match (the same game, the same spot, the same moment) the two worlds touch and **swap**. First the swaps are small and funny: Ollie's sandwich swaps with a god's roast leg of boar, Dot's lost sock with a lost sock of Odin's. Then the twins play *What's the time, Mister Wolf?* on the field. At the same moment, on a hillside in the gods' world, two god children are playing the same game. The twins swap with them: the twins are on the hillside, and a wolf pup and a pale-and-sunny girl are in the school playground.

The god children are **Fenris**, a wolf pup (a growing one, the size of a big dog and getting bigger), and **Hel**, his sister, a serious girl with one half of her face pale and one half warm. They are Loki's children, and Loki is on the school staff. They are watched over by **Tyr**, the war god, who is tired and sad: the other gods have a plan to tie the growing wolf up with a ribbon, and Tyr is the one who will be asked to hold it. He has never wanted to be the god who does that.

The twins don't fight anything. They get home by putting the same game on both sides again, and Tyr gets out of the plan, with a promise that is mended instead of a ribbon.

## The rules (planted early, never broken)

1. **Thin places:** the worlds touch only where two places match: same game, same place, same moment. (Maya works this out in chapter 1 from small swaps.)
2. **Swaps are even:** each person lands in the exact place of whoever they matched, and fits it. Nobody is lost, only misplaced.
3. **To swap back:** the same match must be made again on both sides, at the same moment, with the same words. This needs the school and the gods to cooperate across the gap, which is the plot of chapter 7.
4. The school can't just have fewer gods. The thin place is stitched, not removed (Ms Wa and the sky's patchwork seam, chapter 8).

## Loki and his children (decided: yes)

Loki's reunion with Hel and Fenris is in chapter 7. They aren't close in the myths, so it can be awkward and funny before it turns warm: Mister Loki doesn't know what to say to a wolf pup, and Hel is deadpan with her dad. There are funny myths to draw on. The best is Loki dressing up as a mare to stop a giant finishing a wall in time, which ended with Sleipnir, the eight-legged foal who is another of his children; this could be told by Maya as a one-line gag and Sleipnir could appear as a cameo. Jormungandr (the sea serpent, the third child) is optional and would need softening; check the lore. Keep the sad note to one line (why they have been apart) and avoid Ragnarok.

After the ending, Hel and Fenris come for **play dates**, arranged properly across the stitched thin place, with a rule that the match is made on purpose, so it is a pre-arranged swap or visit and not an accident. Chapter 8 can end with the first one.

## Version 3 decisions (from the second review, before drafting)

1. **Narrator.** The class "we" cannot see the gods' world. Device: from chapter 2 the class tells it as the twins told it to them afterwards ("Alex and Felix told us all of it the next week, and Maya wrote it down, and Ollie asked a great many questions"). The twins stay named in the third person, the class can interrupt with jokes (Ollie would have eaten the boar), and chapter 6 is the school side, which the class saw themselves. Chapter 8 is back to a plain "we".
2. **The game and the swap.** On the school field the twins play *What's the time, Mister Wolf?* Alex asks, Felix is the wolf. On the hill in the gods' world Hel asks and Fenris is the wolf, with Tyr watching. Same words, same moment: Alex swaps with Hel, Felix with Fenris. Swapping back needs the same match again, at the same moment, on both sides.
3. **The deadline.** Mrs Ibbs's Record (story 7) is sealed at the full moon, "after which what is written is how things are", and she may mend spelling but never facts. The full moon is Friday. If the swap is still in place when the Record is sealed, it stays. Mama Quilla is the moon, so she is part of the clock. Chapters 1 to 7 run Monday to Friday; chapter 8 is the last day of term.
4. **Time at home.** Mister Janus keeps the clocks: for the rest of the world it stays Monday afternoon while the twins are away. No parents, ever; nobody at home is worried because nothing has happened yet.
5. **The ritual.** Chapters 1 to 7 keep the hook and the twins saying "Monday" together (it is the series' ritual, and each hook points at the next chapter). Chapter 8 ends differently and without a next-god hook, as the user asked; "The End." still closes it.
6. **Reading order of the lore.** Loki's children are planted in chapter 1 (Mister Loki says he has no children; the gold thread slips). Tyr is planted by Mister Thor in a passing line. Neither is named as the gods' world's visitors until chapter 2.
7. **Cumulative reviews.** Each chapter is reviewed against a continuity ledger that tallies, across all chapters so far: twin lines and actions, plants and payoffs, tics ("That's when", "looked at each other", "at exactly the same time" outside the ritual), repeated set-pieces, and canon facts. The chapter just written is re-read in full; a full read of all eight happens at the end.

## Review notes (done on this version)

**Persona review (fixed above):**
- *Alex/Felix:* the twins are never separated, so they are never sidelined; chapter 7 splits the action (Alex notices the match, Felix asks Tyr for the words, both turn their keys). Recount on drafts.
- *Five-year-old:* the swap is concrete and funny (a wolf pup in Reception, a roast boar in the dinner hall). "What's the time, Mister Wolf?" is a game they know. The scary parts are softened: Hel's house is library-quiet, the ribbon plan is told gently and never carried out, nobody is hurt. The chapters with most risk of a flat patch are 4 and 6: both have comic business (Willow's tap, Hermes backwards).
- *Parent:* "Tye", "Thor" and "Tyr" cluster, so keep them out of the same sentence. "Hel" needs a badge name. The twins must not seem to copy anything: no one goes through a wall; the swap is a magical accident, not something to repeat. One line that the twins are always told to stay together.
- *Standalone nights:* each chapter ends settled and starts with a recap line. Chapters 2 and 3 are the most dependent, so chapter 3 needs a short "where we are".
- *Editor:* the biggest beat is chapter 7; the quiet beat (6) comes after the scariest (5); the tender beat is 8. Peril is spread across 2, 5 and 7. The pattern "comedy, wonder, cosy, reveal, quiet, climax, tender" avoids repeating a tension type back to back.
- *Gap:* the listener knows what the characters don't: at school, Mister Harris says "nobody has swapped" while a wolf pup sits behind him; in the gods' world, a god insists the twins are small giants.

**Adversarial review (fixed above):**
- *Plot holes:* why can't a staff god just fix it? Rule 3 (same moment, same words, both sides) means only the swapped can say it, and the school must help from its side. Why doesn't Janus open a door? He can't: rule 1, the worlds touch only at matches. Why not have fewer gods? Rule 4. Why doesn't Tyr just refuse the ribbon? He is ashamed and afraid of the other gods; the twins and the promise give him the way out. Why does the pup swap? Because it was playing the same game. Chapters 1 and 2 must show that matching is exact (same words, same moment).
- *Unshown claims:* "the gods plan a ribbon" must be shown on the page (a ribbon on a peg, in chapter 3) before Tyr says it.
- *Copy risks:* no hand in a wolf's mouth; no running onto roads; the swap is a magic accident, and the twins never try to cause it. The game itself (chase and "dinner time!") is playground-safe; keep it on grass.
- *Canon slips:* Elfine and Marion are retired, so chapter 8 cameo only. Willow's tap, Harris's dark, Thor's startle. Mama Quilla's hook must be resolved first.
- *Dangling plants:* Hope the bird and Sir Lionel's mane are not forced in; Simurgh's feather and the keys are used.
- *Hostile reader from the tradition:* Hel is fair and a bit grumpy, not a monster. Fenris is a lonely pup, not a doom. No mention of Ragnarok. Tyr's lost hand is not played for pity. The binding is told as a mistake the gods are talked out of, which is a change from the myth, so say once that these are old stories, retold.

**Decided by the user:** Tyr, Hel and Fenris all in; Loki's reunion in; Hel keeps her own name and the pun jokes; play dates after the ending; the arc replaces and becomes story 40.
