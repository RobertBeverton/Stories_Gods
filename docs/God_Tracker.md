# God Tracker

## Next up

**Story 40: Mama Quilla (Inca), picking up the moon hook.** Story 39 ends with the moon sitting far too low over the school roof, silver, with a lady's face that looks about to cry, and a single silver drop (not rain) landing on Willow's nose. The Inca told that silver was the tears of the moon. Story 40 returns to the usual child "we" narrator. Alex led 39's climax (he noticed the silence), so let Felix lead 40. Watch that Andean (Pachamama, 35) is close: make this clearly Inca and moon, not earth.

**The Cat Sìth** was tested for story 39 and set aside: its milk-saucer offering repeats story 35's thank-you lesson, two magical visitors crowded Brigid, and its real lore (stealing a soul before burial) is tied to death. It belongs in the folklore-creatures series (see "Next series"). Keep: a big black cat with a white spot on its chest, said to walk on two legs when nobody is watching; a saucer of milk at Samhain blesses a house, and a house without one finds its cows' milk dried up. Drop: the burial lore and the "witch nine times" story (weakly sourced).

## Ideas not yet used

- Mama Quilla (Inca): the moon. Story 40 (hooked at the end of 39). Inti (sun) is still free.
- Rainbow Serpent (Aboriginal Australian): only with great care and respect for a living tradition.
- Ganesha (Hindu): beginnings and obstacles. A second Hindu tradition after Lakshmi, so only if the user asks. He is implied in story 2 (Ganesh Rao, Year 4).
- Sedna (Inuit): only in a heavily softened form.
- Eshu and Ogun (Yoruba): a second Yoruba story after Oshun, with care for a living tradition.
- Buddhist stories: with care for a living tradition.
- Kali and Durga (Hindu): a third Hindu tradition, so only if the user asks, and with great care.
- Tengri (Mongolian): the sky.
- Mami Wata, Papa Legba, Yemaya: only with care for living traditions.
- **Return stories.** Cheaper than a new god and they deepen the world. Janus coming back is the first candidate (Alex's favourite).
- **Festival stories** and **"many names, many faces" stories**: see "Living faiths, festivals and many names" in `Story_Guide.md`. Christianity is not yet covered; add it only if wanted, as one tradition among many.

## Considered and declined

- **Moloch**: sources associate him with child sacrifice, so he was declined as unsuitable for children.

## Next series (after the gods)

Folklore creatures, set outside school: weekends, holidays or after school. British and Polish creatures first. The same engine applies: something strange turns up, the twins work it out, kindness fixes it. Keep Willow and the "we" voice, or adapt them.

Starter list (from memory, check the original tales): Hedley Kow, Brownie, Selkie, Cornish Knockers, Pixies, Y Ddraig Goch, Tom Tit Tot, Jack o' Lantern, Puck, Lambton Worm, Black Shuck, Nessie; Smok Wawelski, Syrenka, Skarbnik, Domowik, Leszy, Pan Twardowski, Lech and the white eagle, Lajkonik.

Handle with care: Smok Wawelski (killed with a sulphur-stuffed sheep), the Lambton Worm (a curse and a killing), Pan Twardowski (a pact with the devil) and Black Shuck (an omen of death) all need softening, as Thiazi was.

Use the original folklore only. Do not use the TV show Grimm's creature names, "Wesen" or designs, which belong to the studio.
