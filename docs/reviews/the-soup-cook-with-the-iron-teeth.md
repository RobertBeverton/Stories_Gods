# Review: The Soup Cook with the Iron Teeth
File: stories/the-soup-cook-with-the-iron-teeth.md | Words: ~4,050 (whole file, incl. front matter) | Parts: 15 (recipe is 16 to 18)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape: clear problem (Dot breaks rule three), the myth's rules/helpful objects (stove, apple tree, river, swan-geese) pay off twice, and Baba Yaga's loneliness turns the chase into a real emotional beat. One part short of the recipe, Parts 10 and 14 are thin/speechy, and Dot's family never reacts to her return.
- Writer: Clean, funny, easy to say aloud (the "Foo, foo, foo!" and the "did the children pass?" refrain work well). Mild tics: "very" is everywhere, "looked at each other" x3, "whispered" in almost every part. A few adult words ("mutual respect", "proposal", "mistress", "mortar/pestle" are explained once only).
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified Baba Yaga; the teeth and skulls are scary but relieved by humour (turnips, Mister Harris). Soup, frost and the cosy stove make a good settling ending. Dot being carried off and crying while sweeping is the one tense stretch; it is put right well.

## Twin balance
Alex: ~17 spoken lines, carries Dot on his back, shares the rye pie, says "It's okay to be scared", names the Soup Club proposal | Felix: ~18 spoken lines, hugs Dot first, offers to tie up the apple tree, "I think she's lonely", "You could just ask" | Goes first: Alex (mostly), Felix opens Part 11's key beat and Part 15's frost-spotting goes to Alex. Verdict: balanced. Small note: Felix offers to tie the branch but Marion does the actual tying (Part 6), and Alex gets the extra "noticed it first" in the hook. Willow: one brave act (leads into the wood), never speaks; Marion translates the cat, which is fine.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Cast: Elfine and Marion are retired per CLAUDE.md, yet both carry this story (Elfine's facts, Marion's oil, the string, the cat translation). Check against the Bible/Archive timeline: if this story pre-dates the retirement, leave it; if it is meant to be current canon, swap their roles to Maya/Kenji (Maya already appears with a notebook in Part 7, which is Elfine's prop).
2. Length: 15 parts. Suggest splitting Part 6 (stove, apple tree, river) or Part 14 (soup club plus long speech) to reach 16, and trimming the Part 14 speech ("I think I understand now..." through "They're not") which is a sunset speech and repeats Part 11/12's message. The affirmation ("the bravest thing you can do, when you feel lonely, is to say so") is good but could be one short line.
3. Part 13: Nana greets "my boys" but Dot's own family and Dot's welcome are skipped. Add one line (a grown-up for Dot, or Alex and Felix pulling her into the hug) so the rescued child is not left out at the reunion.
4. Part 7: a fence of real bones with glowing skulls is the darkest image in the book; Part 1 jokes it away as turnips so Part 7 lands harder. Consider "real bones" to "old grey logs" or soften so bedtime listeners can sleep after the ending of Part 7, since the relief only arrives in Part 8.
5. Part 4/8: Dot is carried off and made to sweep, and Baba Yaga is forgiven within one conversation. Consider one sentence in Part 12 or 14 where Baba Yaga does something to make it up (an apology gift, Dot's hat is that, partly). Fine as is for most readers; flagging as "unfair without being put right".
6. Hook: frost-beard man plus a girl in silver is a clear mystery and "Monday" lands for both twins. But it is a Friday morning and Baba Yaga's "He's early" suggests his season is already here; make sure the next story's first line explains the weekend gap.

## Minor notes
- Part 10 is the thinnest (repeats Part 9's structure with the same lines); consider cutting the "stove" half and giving it a funnier line.
- "Foo, foo, foo!" is a nice read-aloud moment; keep.
- Respect for tradition: Baba Yaga is feared then understood, dignified, never mocked; the iron teeth and "toothy smile" are used for warmth and fear, not ridicule. Good.
- Hard-ish words to watch at narration: pierogi, sauerkraut, mortar, pestle, Czarny (respelling in narration/pronunciations.json).
- "very" count is high in Parts 1, 7, 8; trim a handful during any future polish.
- Part 15 hook: the "Beside him sat a girl" description is good; "skin was pale as snow" could be "pale as snow" only (smaller phrase).
