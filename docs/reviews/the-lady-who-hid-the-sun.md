# Review: The Lady Who Hid the Sun
File: stories/the-lady-who-hid-the-sun.md | Words: about 4,080 (4,173 with front matter) | Parts: 17

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. The problem (a lost mirror, a hurt goddess, a noisy brother) echoes the myth cleanly, and the party-at-the-cave solution is earned by Maya's research. Part ends mostly pull the page. Susanoo's "you're being dramatic" is put right by his apology in Part 14. The sunset speech in Part 16 carries three separate lessons, and the one that matters (the mirror line) is the last, which is good, but it is the stock sunset-speech beat and could be shorter.
- Writer: Funny, specific details (poached-egg sun, "still on the end of your arms", Willow's cat dance). Sentences read aloud well. Light tics: "said ... at the same time" outside the ritual, "underlined it three times". A few hard words for a six-year-old. One continuity slip about where the mirror was.
- Listener/parent: Dark and frost in Part 8 is eased quickly by jokes. Dot gets a lovely brave moment. The god is warm and dignified. The ending hook (a llama and a voice from under the floor, plus a small rumble) is a real mystery, but the last beat is a rumble rather than a settle. Parents may want a calmer last paragraph before the hook, which the sunset scene mostly gives.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. Part 12: OLD: "Then the whole of Year 1. Then the staff." -> NEW: "Then the whole class. Then the staff." (age-proofing)
2. Part 6/7 continuity: OLD: "I worked on that for six days," she said -> NEW: "I worked on that for days and days," she said (Part 6 says "all week" and the assembly is on Wednesday, so six days cannot be right)
3. Part 11: OLD: "tangled in her tail feathers, where it had been trapped" -> NEW: "tangled in the feathers on her head, where it had been stuck" (the mirror goes "clunk on top of Madam Simurgh's head", so it cannot be in the tail; "trapped since Sunday night in the dark" is fine either way)
4. Part 11: OLD: "Dot," said everybody at the same time. -> NEW: "Dot," said everybody at once. (keeps "at the same time" for the Monday ritual)
5. Part 16: OLD: Maya wrote it down. She underlined it three times, and then, because it was a good one, she drew a tiny sun. -> NEW: Maya wrote it down, and then, because it was a good one, she drew a tiny sun beside it. (tic trim)
6. Part 12: OLD: Mister Heston found an old galvanised tub and turned it upside down. -> NEW: Mister Heston found an old metal tub and turned it upside down. ("galvanised" is hard to say and to follow)
7. Part 12: OLD: Willow, in a burst of inspiration, leapt on to the tub -> NEW: Willow, in a sudden burst of wanting to join in, leapt on to the tub ("inspiration" is a hard word; or simply "Willow, all at once, leapt")
8. Part 5: OLD: and they've never gotten on. -> NEW: and they've never got on. (British voice)
9. Part 9: OLD: Mister Heston was trying to prise the stone door open with a crowbar. -> NEW: Mister Heston was trying to lever the stone door open with a crowbar. ("prise" is easy to mishear when read aloud; "lever" is also a little hard, so "push the stone door open" is simplest)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part 3 (and 12, 15): the kami. Shinto kami are sacred beings, and they appear here as eight hundred giggling lantern-headed fireflies that "laugh when they're nervous". That is cute, but it risks flattening a living religion into a cartoon. Suggestion: have Maya say these are small helper spirits who came with her, or say "kami are the spirits and sacred presences of Japan, and these are the little ones", and keep the giggling to a few of them. Keep Ise and the royal-family line as they are, because they are respectful.
2. Part 4: Madam Simurgh says the mirror is "in the school" and "somewhere high, somewhere shut", but Part 11 says it was caught in the clouds and she was not high enough before. Suggest changing her line to "It is nearby. But it is not in a place I can easily reach" and "Somewhere high, and very dark", so the later reveal is a fair clue and not a contradiction.
3. Part 16: three lessons in a row (hiding is okay but don't stay; somebody always comes; hold up a mirror). The mirror line is the plot-earned affirmation and is the strongest. Suggest trimming the middle to "It is all right to hide when you are hurt, but don't stay in there forever", and keeping "What if no one comes?" and the mirror line. This also shortens the stock sunset speech.
4. Part 17: the final beat leaves the children with a rumble and a voice from the ground. The hook is good, but a parent reading at bedtime may want one calming line before the rumble, for example Mrs Amaterasu's glow in the window as the twins leave. Optional.
5. Part 1/12: Mister Susanoo is "Weather and Sports Day" and Mister Hadad is already "Weather and Playground Equipment" (Bible). Not an error, but both appear in the same scene in Part 9. Consider one line acknowledging the two weather gods ("two weather teachers, and neither could help"), or leave as is.

## Minor notes
- "That's when" appears twice (Part 4, Part 10), well within the limit.
- Dot "sucking her thumb" (Part 11) may read as babyish to older listeners; "chewing her sleeve" or "holding her mitten" would age better. Optional.
- Willow follows the brief: she does a brave and funny thing (dancing, leading in the dark) and never speaks.
- Maya's lecture in Part 3 is long; it is broken up well by Kenji and Ollie.
- Part 14 apology and Part 15 "chair under the stone door" are the best resolution beats; the "tell someone where you are" idea is a good lesson for a child of any age.
