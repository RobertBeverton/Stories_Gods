# Review: The Fisherman Who Slowed the Sun
File: stories/the-fisherman-who-slowed-the-sun.md | Words: ~4,000 | Parts: 17

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. Clear problem (dark early, no fire, grumpy sun) that echoes both Maui myths, every part ends on a turn-the-page line, and the Mahuika scene is properly put right (apology, gift, test). Slight sag in Parts 3 to 4 (two long Maya monologues back to back with only one "Ew" for laughs). Hook (WHO OWNS STORIES? plus "Anyone but him") is a real mystery and "Monday" lands with both twins.
- Writer: Clean, speakable, consistent "we" narrator, funny specifics (drain fishing, "Seriously", centipede in coats, Dot as a wall). Few tics: "Everyone looked at" twice in Part 12, "That's when" four times (at the limit). A couple of mildly hard words (trickery, curious).
- Listener/parent: Warm, cosy and settling; the lantern walk and long gold light are a good wind-down, and the spider is funny rather than scary. Mahuika is dignified and the tradition is treated with respect. Willow does not appear at all (cast list omits her).

## Twin balance
Alex: ~11 spoken lines (evidence two, the lesson, the sticks/fire point, the jar idea, "We have a flame"), key actions: spots the hook, designs the jar, carries it | Felix: ~13 spoken lines (sun counted, "sorry" speech to Mahuika, "We've got you" to the jar), key actions: steps forward to Mahuika, carries it | Goes first: Alex (Part 2 evidence; Felix first in Part 3 questions). Verdict: balanced. Felix owns the emotional peak (Part 11), Alex the practical peak (Part 12), and both carry the jar. Felix's "It's Alex's idea" gives Alex credit nicely.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. Part 5: OLD: "Now, Year One," he said. -> NEW: "Now, everybody," he said. (age-proofing; explicit year group)
2. Part 12: OLD: Someone had stabbed a few holes in the lid with a fork. -> NEW: Someone had poked a few holes in the lid with a fork. (softer verb for bedtime)
3. Part 12: OLD: Everyone looked at the pile of lost property in the corner, which Mister Maui had brought down in a bucket. -> NEW: Then Felix spotted the pile of lost property in the corner, which Mister Maui had brought down in a bucket. (trims the repeated "Everyone looked at"; also needs no other change since the next sentence starts "And at the top of the pile")
4. Part 8: OLD: but he did it by trickery. -> NEW: but he did it with a trick. (simpler word for a six-year-old)
5. Part 12: OLD: Something that's not made of anything that burns." -> NEW: Something that can't burn." (hard to say in one breath)
6. Part 11: OLD: Mahuika looked at him for a very long time. -> NEW: Mahuika watched him for a very long time. (trims "looked" repetition; optional)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part 11: Felix's speech to Mahuika is a bit of a "wise child lecture" and ends with a moral ("Everyone makes mistakes. The good part is fixing them.") that Part 16 then repeats. Consider cutting the last two sentences or letting Felix say it shorter, so the closing affirmation in Part 16 stays the single clear one.
2. Part 9: Maui says "Stay here... I mean it" then lets them all come, including Dot who sneaks in. It is a small mixed message after "Brave doesn't mean going where you're told not to." Consider having Maui notice and allow it ("Fine. Behind me.") or have only the twins named, so the lesson is not undercut.
3. Part 8: "The whole world caught fire" is large for a bedtime story; it is softened by the rain, but consider "a great fire spread across the world" with no extra detail. Judgement call.
4. Whole story: Willow is absent. Brief says she does one brave thing and never speaks; she could be the one crouched beside Dot in the wall (Part 13) or the one who holds the lantern door.

## Minor notes
- Part 5: the weather/time is tied to autumn clocks going back, which is fine for any age and gives a cosy anchor.
- Part 3 nosebleed bait is handled lightly with "Ew" and "It's a very old story"; fine.
- Part 17: "Mister Maui... looked up" and the spider tip of the hat is a good closing laugh; the last beat before Monday ("We all turned and looked at Alex and Felix. They looked at each other.") is the ritual and can stay.
- "Reception" for Dot is acceptable; no other age or year references found.
