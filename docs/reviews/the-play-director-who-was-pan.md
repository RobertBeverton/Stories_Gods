# Review: The Play Director Who Was PAN

> **Status: superseded by a full rewrite (story 11, about 5,800 words, 18 parts).** The DIRECT fixes below were applied to the old text (commit a2222bb) and the story was then rewritten, so the quotes and part names in this report describe the OLD text. Expanded and restructured in the series cast (Alex leads). Resolved: Willow's brave thing (wading the stream she hates), the two affirmations plus lecture (now one enacted affirmation, 'I can be scared and brave at the same time', with 'Stop. Breathe. I am safe.' kept as the breathing rhythm because story 13 recites it), the Thorley ladder line, the long sentences, no year groups. The hook is a sign of Hanuman.
>
> Weak spots recorded after the rewrite: A long middle; Parts Fourteen to Sixteen are three quiet parts in a row by design.

## Verdicts
- Editor: Clear, funny, well shaped (rule, fright, panic, rescue, play, hook), and the panic/Pan link is a lovely myth echo. But it is far too thin at about 2,400 words (guide is 4,000+), so the wood search and the middle of the story feel rushed. It also has two affirmations plus a lecture part, which is one too many.
- Writer: Child "we" narrator is consistent and the jokes land (EATING THE SCENERY, a boy dressed as a hat, the two Middle Billy Goats, the ladder like a runaway train). Dialogue is clear in short exchanges. A few over-long sentences in Parts 7 and 15, and "looked at each other" twice.
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified Pan, and Mr Thorley and Pan forgive each other nicely. Good "scared and brave" line for sleep. The ending is a silly, bouncy hook rather than a settle, which is fine, but the very last quiet beat is the clapping, then monkey jokes. Willow (the tree narrator) never gets her one brave thing.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 12 spoken lines, plays first note, reassures Pann first, coaxes out the flower girls | Felix: about 13 spoken lines, gets the sheep-boy and the hat boy, leads the second affirmation | Goes first: Alex (knees line, first to speak to Pann, first note on the pipes). Verdict: balanced. Felix gets slightly more of the rescue spotlight (two of the three finds), but Alex has the first-speaker and first-note moments.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Length (whole story): at about 2,400 words this is roughly 60 percent of target. Best places to grow it: (a) a second rehearsal or the warm-ups so Pann's oddness builds, with a clue found by each twin; (b) a longer wood search with a small obstacle for each find; (c) a funny staff-room beat after the panic (Mr Harris and the velvet curtain, Mrs Tibbs in the supply cupboard) instead of only naming them in the walk back. Aim for 4,000 or a little over.
2. Part 7 and Part 8: Alex, Felix and Maya do not run, with no reason given, even though the panic is meant to hit everyone who hears the shout "even if they don't know why". Suggest one line that they did feel it (heart thumping, legs wanting to go), and that they stayed because they were already holding each other's hands on the bridge. That also seeds the affirmation properly.
3. Parts 10 and 14: the affirmation "I CAN BE SCARED AND BRAVE AT THE SAME TIME" is good and earned, but then Part 16 adds a third line, "STOP. BREATHE. I AM SAFE.", delivered as a grown-up lesson ("I want to tell you something about panic"). That is the standing-rule "sunset speech" and a second affirmation. Suggest cutting Part 16 or shrinking it to two or three lines of Pann, and keeping only the first affirmation (which the twins use in the wood, so it is already plot-earned).
4. Willow (the tree narrator): she does no single brave thing. Suggest she does one quiet brave act in the wood (for example, standing still as a tree so the very small boy has something to walk towards, or holding up the torch branch), still without speaking.
5. Part 6 (Mr Thorley / the toolbox "said **sorry** on the side"): a toolbox that says sorry is a nice joke but unclear. Consider "It had **sorry** written on the side in chalk" or cut it, since Mr Thorley apologises in person later.
6. Hook (Part 18): "Monkey prints" up the wall and across the ceiling is a good mystery, and "Monday" lands for both twins. The only softness is that Kenji and Ollie give the answer away with jokes; consider leaving the last question open (Maya's line "Monkey prints" is enough, plus one of Kenji's).

- "Reception children" is acceptable by the brief; left alone.
- "Alex Bailey" and "Felix Bailey" in the intro, then just first names; fine.
- "Hard" words for a six-year-old (scenery, nymph, muffled, tidal wave) are all carried by context; no change needed.
- Syrinx's story is handled gently and respectfully; Pan is warm and dignified throughout.
