**Sagging middle.** Yes, mildly, and it is 42 and 43 together: two nights in a row that are warm and safe (a feast, then porridge in a candlelit house), with the same rule restated: «Cats go through the *edges*.» [42.11] and then again «The edge,» said Alex. «Memory said cats go through the edges.» [43.11]. The stakes are stated but not felt until 44. What holds them up is character, not plot: Ganglöt, the mouse, Old Ulf, the drawing. I think it works for listeners aged five because every part has a laugh or a small ache. For an adult reading it straight through it is the soft stretch. **Smallest fix:** none required; see P2 for one scene in 44 that would harden the stakes instead.

**Does each night stand alone?** Mostly. Every chapter opens with a one-line recap. Three do not tell a new listener *why* the twins are on the gods' hill:
- «Last time, we left Alex and Felix in a house at the bottom of a hill, watching a thin line of silver creep along the floor, with a cat sitting in front of it.» [44.1]
- «Last time, we heard how the twins were getting on, up on the gods' hill.» [45.1] (Hel at school makes no sense without the swap)
- «Last time, a puppy ran away to jump at the moon, and a cat carried a note that said *Tonight*.» [46.1]

**Smallest edit:** 45.1 add «(they had swapped places with Hel and her wolf puppy)» after "hill"; 44.1 add «after swapping places with a girl and a wolf». 46.1 can stay, since by then the finale audience is following.

**Standalone check for the rules.** The two rules a stray listener needs are given in the chapter that uses them: swap rule in 41.11 and 43.8, thin places in 40.5. Fine.

## 3. Set pieces

**Tyr's apology (46.7 to 46.9): works.** The sequence is right: the oath voice fails («The puppy turned his back.» [46.6]), a child says «"Not yet,"» [46.7], then Tyr takes off his cloak and kneels so his face is level with the puppy's. The three promises are in Tyr's own register, which is a smart choice: he is "the god of exact words", so the numbered list is how he says *plain*. One thing to note, not fix: Felix has just said «"Plain," ... "And you say sorry first."» [46.7], and Tyr answers with a structured three-part speech. It is saved because the speech opens with «It's Tyr. I am so sorry.» [46.7]. The detail that does the work is the wooden hand held out palm up.

**The replay (46.11 to 46.13): works, and it is short.** One page. The payoffs are exact and tiny: «like two sides of a zip» [46.12], «Fish-finger o'clock» said by both, Dot's snort. The one weak note was Quilla's line, now fixed (D1).

**The three-times plan.** The method is told in 43.8 (the rule), 45.7 (the letter: «THEN WE ALL SAY "DINNER TIME" AT THE SAME BREATH.» [45.7]), practised in 45.9, then done in 46.11 to 46.13. Four occurrences. For a five-year-old listening one night at a time this is useful repetition; for a parent it is a lot. **Smallest edit:** drop the procedural sentences in the letter (45.7) and keep «PLEASE ASK HER TO SAY WHEN.» and «THE MOON CAN SEE BOTH.» The practice scene already shows the steps.

**The last-day party (47.8 to 47.11): a lot of payoffs, well made.** See 5.

## 4. Voice, repetition and read-aloud

**Hel's «It's not personal»:** three times, 41.11, 45.2, 47.9: «"It's not personal," said Hel, for the fifteenth time. "He eats everything."» [45.2]. It is the only contraction-heavy thing Hel says (Phase 2: 4.1 contractions per 100 words, against 1.2 for Tyr). I read it as a deliberate catchphrase and it lands as one: it is the same line each time and it keeps meaning more. **Keep.**

**The «say sorry first / a promise can be mended» moral.** It is said or enacted five times across three chapters:
1. «"So we'll make another one," said Alex. "Which is true," said Felix.» [44.13]
2. «"Plain," said Felix. "And you say sorry first."» [46.7]
3. Tyr's apology itself [46.8]
4. «A man said sorry to a wolf. And it was mended.» [46.17] (the Record, which is a good, short, true version)
5. «You do it by saying sorry first.» [47.13], and then the twins repeat it back: «"A promise can be mended," he said.» and «"If you keep the next one,"» and «"Yes," said Tyr. He closed his eyes. "That is exactly what I mean."» [47.13]

Items 1 to 4 are story. Item 5 is a lecture after the story has finished making the point. **Smallest edit:** keep Tyr's line «I did not know that it could be mended.» in 47.13 and cut the twins' echo and «"That is exactly what I mean."» This keeps one speech from the god whose speech it is, and keeps the twins in the scene (they still ask «"How?"»). See P4.

**Muddled line (a real stumble), 44.13.** «"Tyr," said Felix. "You were *right*. You said it was a promise you knew you couldn't keep. That's the wrong thing. The wrong thing isn't the wolf."» [44.13]. "You were right" has no object; "That's the wrong thing" points the wrong way. Read aloud, a child cannot follow it. **Smallest edit:** «"Tyr," said Felix. "You said the wrong thing was the promise. Not the wolf."» then Tyr's «"It is the promise,"» is a plain agreement. (This is a clear fix, not a matter of taste; included in the P1 batch.)

**Other read-aloud notes (none need fixing):** Ganglöt is pronounced through `narration/pronunciations.json`, not the text. 44.5 gives only five of Gleipnir's six things («the spit of a bird, and the... well.» [44.5]); the skipped one is the bear's sinews, a sensible kid-safe omission. 47.10 has «Skadi had come up the hill behind them» and «Mister Loki, who had come up the hill behind him» two paragraphs apart; a small echo, easy to vary.

**Long sentences:** left as they are (your decision). Noted only that they cluster in 45 to 47, which is where the voice has the most to carry.

## 8. Readings by a specialist (what each would wince at)

**A Norse scholar.** Mostly sound: Tyr's hand, Gleipnir, the willing-binding and the pledge, the Thing, Huginn and Muninn as Thought and Memory, Geri and Freki, Ganglöt as Hel's servant. Three places a specialist would raise an eyebrow, none a defect for children:
- The moon-eater in the sources is the wolf *Hati* (a son of Fenrir's line), not Fenrir. Here: «"He did that on Sunday," said Skadi.» [44.4]. The arc folds Hati's role into Fenris. Fine as a children's retelling; worth knowing.
- Hel's face: «One half of her face was pale as milk. The other half was warm and pink as toast.» [40.11] softens the sources' living-and-corpse-coloured halves. A respectful, deliberate softening.
- Hel as a gentle keeper of the resting dead and the hall's bureaucratic quiet is an invention. Rules in this world allow it.

**An Andean reader (Inca).** Quilla keeps the calendar and her tears are silver: both accurate and warm («I keep the calendar. I tell people when the festivals are, and when to plant, and what month it is.» [40.15]). Two things would give them pause:
- Quilla is mostly apologetic and weepy: «"I'm doing my best to hold myself up. It's only that I'm a bit heavy, with all the crying."» [41.1] and «it's the only use I am» [45.11]. She is a great goddess reduced to a nervous guest for six chapters. She does get her moment: «in the biggest voice that any of us had ever heard, from one end of the world to the other» [46.12]. **Smallest edit:** give her one line of authority earlier (for example in 45.11, before «Now»: «I have counted the months of the world for ten thousand years. I can count to one.»). Needs your call (P6).
- The Inca explained eclipses as an animal attacking the moon and made a noise to drive it off, which lines up with the puppy jumping at the moon, a nice accident, but nothing in the text acknowledges it. No change needed.

**A bereavement counsellor.** The house of the resting is carefully done and honest: «Quiet is not the same thing as sad» [43.4] and Gary the goldfish: «He's under the oak tree now. We said some words.» [43.4]. Two things to look at:
- **Sleep and death are blurred.** «"Are they asleep?" whispered Felix.» [43.4] gets the answer «"Resting,"» but the same part has «Somebody was asleep.» [43.4], and later «somebody was snoring, gently, like a cat.» [43.9]. Standard advice is not to let death look like sleep for young children, because it can make bedtime frightening. **Smallest edit:** «Somebody was asleep» → «Somebody was reading» [43.4]; «somebody was snoring» → «somebody was humming» [43.9]. Old Ulf already hums, so use «somebody was knitting, slowly».
- **The dead come back for the party.** Old Ulf is «woken» to play: «Somebody must have woken him. We think it was Hel.» [47.11], echoing «Wake me, if there is a game.» [43.5]. It is moving and it is the series' best joke payoff («"Fast as a gull,"»), but a child who has lost somebody may ask why theirs cannot come to the party. **Options:** keep it (it is clearly the gods' world and a special day), or make him a visitor from a different hall. Needs your call (P5).
