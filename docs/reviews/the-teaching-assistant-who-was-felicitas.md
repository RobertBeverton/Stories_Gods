# Review: The Teaching Assistant Who Was FELICITAS
File: stories/the-teaching-assistant-who-was-felicitas.md | Words: about 1,790 (body) | Parts or sections: 9 (plain all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Clean, well-shaped little story (raffle, theory, evidence list, lucky day, lesson, Fortuna, wheel, sisters) with a clear problem that echoes the myth. It is under half the usual length and feels a little thin, but it does not sag. Ends on a warm joke, not a hook (none, as the Archive records); no affirmation beyond "I know you can".
- Writer: Short, speakable lines, consistent child "we" narrator, good specifics (a lawn thrown at her, the bean bags, "fun chaos"). Mild tic: "looked at / looked down" used about six times. One odd phrase ("ONLY just full").
- Listener/parent: Warm and respectful of Felicitas; Fortuna is playful, not mocked. Felix's name-means-lucky moment is lovely. The ice-cream-van ending is happy but lively, not a settle-down ending; fine for a first-era story.

## Twin balance
Alex: 0 (not in the story; early class, so expected) | Felix: about 14 spoken lines, stars throughout (experiment, lesson, shares sponge, spins the wheel) | Goes first: Felix. Verdict: Felix-only by design (his name means lucky). No Alex to balance; the "Monday" ritual is absent in this early story.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. Part "THE EVIDENCE": OLD: "and then she won a game of Snap against the whole of Year 3 at once." -> NEW: "and then she won a game of Snap against the whole of the class above at once." (age-proofing)
2. Part "THE NEW PE TEACHER": OLD: "It was as tall as Freddie in Year 6, painted in red and gold" -> NEW: "It was as tall as Freddie from the top class, painted in red and gold" (age-proofing; keeps Freddie)
3. Part "THE NEW TEACHING ASSISTANT": OLD: "It was ONLY just full. It didn't have anything in it when she walked in." -> NEW: "It was ALREADY full. It didn't have anything in it when she walked in." (the line contradicts itself: "only just full" then "didn't have anything in it"; and para after says it was full from the start. Maya's claim should be that it never needed filling, so this fix is a best guess; see ASK 3)
4. Part "THE SISTERS": OLD: "Then they both looked down and saw us all standing there." -> NEW: "Then they both noticed us all standing there." (repeated "looked" tic trim)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Ending: no hook for the next god and no "Monday". Fine for the era, but if the library wants a bridge, a hint at the Seven Lucky Gods (a boat-shaped minibus, or a distant "knock, knock") would link 4 to 5 and settle the order. Optional; do not do it if you want to keep story 4 as written.
2. The Sisters: Mrs Felicity says Felix should be "brave when it is low", but Felix is never shown being brave. The wheel's low moment (tidy the cupboard) is only comic. Consider one small line where Felix, after TIDY THE CUPBOARD, is first to pick up a bean bag, so the "brave when low" promise is earned.
3. Part 2 (basket): Maya's "it was full when she walked in" is the story's only magic clue and is muddled in the wording. Suggest rewriting as: "It was empty on the coat hook at registration and full when she walked in" only if you want it clearer; otherwise just take fix 3.
4. The Lesson: Felix is gently shamed for pocketing the coin and taking the sponge, though he never knew anyone minded. It is put right kindly (half the sponge), so acceptable; just note the fairness is borderline.

## Minor notes
- "Reception boy" is acceptable under the brief.
- Order of 4 and 5 (the open question): the texts give mild evidence that 4 comes BEFORE 5. Story 5's opening line "Even Felix, who is lucky, looked like he'd lost a pound" and its sticker joke ("Felix's said YOU ARE FELIX. Nobody knows how he knew") both lean on Felix's name meaning lucky, which is only revealed in story 4, and story 4 is where "Felix Bailey, who sits next to me" is introduced to the reader. The Archive also says Felix is first introduced in 4. Both stories open "The first time we saw ... was on a Monday", which fits either order, and story 4 has no mention of the Seven Lucky Gods or the boat-bus, so nothing proves 5 first. Verdict: keep 4 then 5; the current order is the better-supported one.
- Story 4 itself has no hook and no reference to what follows, so neither its opening nor its ending forces anything.
