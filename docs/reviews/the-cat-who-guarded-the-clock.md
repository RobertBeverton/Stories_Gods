# Review: The Cat Who Guarded the Clock

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong recipe: clear problem (Apep and the clock) that echoes the myth, escalating odd-week middle, a joyful bow-on-the-snake resolution, and a real hook (golden pond, honey, mirror). Part 13 and Part 14 run as two back-to-back speeches after the climax and slow the landing a little, but Part 14 (Willow is not too old) is the emotional heart. Main problem is that Willow talks (see ASK 1).
- Writer: Voice is lively and read-aloud friendly; jokes land (Pastoral Care and Pest Control, "Yailey Balex", the blue bow, "Is it Thursday?"). Tics are light: "looked at each other" twice, lots of "whispered" in Parts 2 to 8, heavy "very". A few long sentences (Part 15 finale). "Monday" ritual is the only "at exactly the same time".
- Listener/parent: Warm, funny, a little scary with quick relief (bow, "It's tidy"). Both twins have real moments; Willow gets her brave act and her own tribute. Ends on a hook rather than a wind-down, but the Friday party then the golden pond is gentle enough.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Parts 11 and 12: Willow speaks ("Nice bow", "I couldn't until now", "No"). The Bible says Willow never speaks and does one brave thing. The bow joke is the funniest beat, so suggest keeping the exchange but giving it to Mrs Bast or Maya ("Willow says no" is also funny: Mrs Bast translates, "She says no."). That keeps Willow silent and deadpan, and Part 14's "She says thank you... and she says she knew" already sets Mrs Bast as her interpreter.
2. Part 10: Maya's mention of the old story where the cat "cuts off his head with a knife" is graphic for bedtime. The "No knives... Not in a school" reply rescues it as a joke, but consider softening Maya's line to "she defeats Apep" for the youngest listeners.
3. Parts 13 and 14: two consecutive adult speeches ("strong and needing anyone", "gentle and fierce", "you can hold two things at once", then Willow's age). The affirmation ("You can hold two things at once") is good and plot-earned, but Part 13 could be trimmed by one paragraph (for instance cut "Many people think you must choose..." run-up to two lines) so it does not become a sunset speech.
4. Part 6: Nana is placed at the sleepover (deckchair, flask, knitting) and then never appears again, nor does she fall asleep on the page. Either add a one-line beat in Part 7 or 13 (Nana snoring through it, or remarking "Lovely sleep") or drop her.

## Minor notes
- Hard-ish words for a six-year-old, fine in context: "headdress", "recoiled", "faltered", "humiliating", "pastoral". Consider "headdress" -> keep, it is repeated and explained.
- "Thoth... our dinner lady" connects to Mrs Ibbs; Felix's "Mrs Ibbs?" is a fun callback for series listeners but does not stand alone for newcomers. Fine.
- Part 2 and Part 8 have near-identical titles ("Maya Has a List", "Maya's List"); harmless, but Part 8 could be "Maya Reads the List".
- Tone and respect: Bast is warm and dignified throughout; Apep is villainous but not mocked beyond the bow, which is gentle. Eyebrow fact is hedged ("It's in a very old book"). Nothing flattens the tradition.
- "Whispered" appears about 9 times in Parts 2 to 8; trimming two or three to "said" would help the narrator's voice.
- Length is about 10 percent over the 4,000-word target; Part 13 and Part 2 are the easiest places to trim.
