# Review: The Quiz Mistress Who Was ATHENA
File: stories/the-quiz-mistress-who-was-athena.md | Words: about 3,200 (3,208 with front matter) | Parts or sections: 21 (plain all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape and a myth-echo that fits (Arachne's boast becomes a quiz-hall web that feeds on "I know everything"). The problem is clear, the affirmation "I DON'T KNOW YET" is short and earned by the plot. It is on the thin side at about 3,200 words, but the pace suits the story, so it is not padding-hungry. The middle holds up because the web escalates (table leg, wall, ceiling, drop). Athene stays calm and passive while everyone, including her, is tied up, which slightly undercuts the idea that the class could have solved it earlier (see ASK 1). The hook is real (Prometheus with a fennel stalk) but the ending word is "Yes", not "Monday".
- Writer: Clean, easy to say aloud, with funny specifics (owl with no postcode, "He's not very imaginative", Kenji's unlisted Cooking round, Dot's best guess). The narrator drifts out of "we" in the twin-only scenes (Parts "The Twins", "The Knot"), which is acceptable but noticeable. "Looked at each other" appears four times. Arabella is described with knitting needles though the myth is weaving.
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified Athena. Arabella is helped rather than punished, and the twins' kindness to her is the heart of the story. The web drop is scary for a moment but is relieved quickly by the twins' discovery. The last scene is a lively hook rather than a settling close, which is not ideal for sleep.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 15 spoken lines; notices the clues, spots the thin thread, first says "yet", frees the Reception boy, and has the key emotional line to Arabella ("Are you just afraid of being wrong?") | Felix: about 14 spoken lines; asks the questions, says "It works!", comforts Ollie, and has the "you look really scared" line to Arabella | Goes first: Alex (notices first, speaks first in the web and in the discovery). Verdict: balanced overall, with a slight lean to Alex on the biggest moments (the discovery and the Arabella breakthrough). The ritual swap line in "The Lesson" ("Alex noticed, and Felix asked. Then you swapped") is a nice way to confirm equality.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. Part "THE ARRIVAL OF THE CLUES" (opening, before first heading): OLD: "It was Year 1 against Year 1, and the winners would get a golden trophy shaped like a pencil." -> NEW: "It was our class against theirs, and the winners would get a golden trophy shaped like a pencil." (age-proofing; the only year-group reference in the story)
2. Part "THE ARRIVAL OF THE CLUES": OLD: "The twins looked at each other and said, "We need Maya."" -> NEW: "The twins said, "We need Maya."" (trims a repeated tic)
3. Part "THE TWINS GO TO ARABELLA": OLD: "Alex and Felix looked at each other. They were still shaky, and a bit cross." -> NEW: "Alex and Felix were still shaky, and a bit cross." (trims a repeated tic)
4. Part "THE WEAVER": OLD: "She carried a pair of silver knitting needles and a ball of shimmering thread" -> NEW: "She carried a pair of silver needles and a ball of shimmering thread" (she is the Arachne weaver; "knitting" pulls away from the myth)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part "THE GODS TRY" / "THE TWINS": Ms Athene taught the phrase on Tuesday and is the one person who clearly knows the answer, yet she stands tied and calm and says only "I can't beat her by knowing more." This leaves a small logic gap about why no grown-up or team member tries it. Suggestion: give her a quiet nudge (for example, she catches the twins' eye and says nothing, or says "Keep thinking, you two"), or have a tied-up Maya or Ollie try it and fail because they only say it half-heartedly, so the twins' discovery of how to mean it is the new step.
2. Part "THE ENDING (WITH A TORCH)": the shared word is "Yes", not "Monday" (already noted in the Archive as a known deviation). If the series ritual is to be applied to this story, change "Yes" to "Monday" and have Prometheus say he will start on Monday. If it is kept, the ending still works as a hook but leaves the children wound up rather than settled; consider a short calming beat before the gate scene (for example, the owl asleep on Ms Athene's shoulder as the sun goes down).
3. Part "WHO IS ATHENA?": Zeus asking "someone to crack it open with an axe" is brief and funny with Ollie's "That's disgusting", but it is the most graphic image in the story for a six-year-old. Optional: soften to "split it open" or let Dot's gasp carry it. Not required.
4. Willow does not appear. Early stories may predate the "one brave thing" rule, so only add her if the library pass wants it.

## Minor notes
- The twin intro "Alex and Felix Bailey are twins, so they have a system" and the clue-spotting swap (Alex notices, Felix asks) is a fun device that pays off in the closing lesson.
- "Pridefield" is a neat name for Arachne's pride, and the "I like the one who weaves... I do not like the one who boasts. But she's gone now" resolution is kind and respectful.
- Arabella's cube-root boast is a joke for the parent; the six-year-old will not need it.
- "Bonfire Night is in two weeks" ties the hook to the calendar; harmless, but it will not match every season of re-listening.
