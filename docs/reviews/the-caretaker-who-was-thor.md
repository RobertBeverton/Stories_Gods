## Verdicts
- Editor: Tight, funny detective shape (name, hammer, lunch, goats, warning, storm, twist) with a genuinely good turn in "Not tonight, brother" and the "TWO SECONDS later" thunder. But at about 1,000 words it is a quarter of the length guide: it reads as a charming sketch, and each clue gets one short beat. The hook (who is his brother? Loki) is a real mystery and the Mr Pickering wave is a lovely release. No "Monday" (early-story format, not raised).
- Writer: Clean, short, easily read aloud, consistent child "we", specific jokes (water bottle rattling, holding the door for a bin, pigeon falling off the roof). Tics are light: "We all" opens many sentences, "That's when" twice. One point-of-view slip ("I") and one inaccurate joke line, below. Caps emphasis is heavy but suits a read-aloud.
- Listener/parent: Warm and safe; Mr Thorley's shiny eyes at the drawing is the heart of it and he is dignified, not mocked. Mrs Tibbs laughing for two minutes is kind, not cruel. Ends on a smile, but the last beat is a "look around for a smirk" so it is playful rather than a sleepy landing. Fine for the time it was written.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Length: about 1,000 words against a guide of at least 4,000. Options: leave it as a short founding story (it has charm and an early-story identity), or expand with one or two more clue scenes (for example a lost-property "hammer" moment, or the class trying to make Mr Thorley cross to prove it) and give the storm day more build. Your call; I would not pad it.
2. Part "THE LUNCH" / "THE GOATS": the goat cheese bag "solved it" in the same breath as Ruby saying the van is a clever disguise. Fine as a joke, but the parts end flatly ("we were quite hungry", "we had solved it"). Optional: a sharper last line on one of them to pull the page turn.

## Minor notes
- "the sky doesn't have a very good pension" is a grown-up joke a six-year-old will not get; it will land for the parent. Keep or swap to something like "the sky doesn't have any chairs" if you want it to work for the child.
- Mrs Tibbs is the class teacher here but the dinner lady in later canon; the Archive already notes this ("the class teacher here"), so not re-raised.
- Mr Thorley's "Nobody's ever drawn me before" is the emotional best line; the shiny-eyes sentence after it slightly explains it, but it is a gentle explanation for young listeners and I would keep it.
