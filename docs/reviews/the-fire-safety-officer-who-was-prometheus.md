# Review: The Fire Safety Officer Who Was PROMETHEUS
File: stories/the-fire-safety-officer-who-was-prometheus.md | Words: 3,729 (whole file) | Parts or sections: 23 (opening plus 22 all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. Myth echoes cleanly (Prometheus and Zeus reconcile, Epimetheus acts first and thinks later), the fire rules are planted on Tuesday and paid off on Thursday, and the Thor storm held back until every child is out is a clever turn. Slightly under the length guide and fast-moving, but not thin. Two real problems: no "Monday" in the ending (see ASK 1) and a few places where the safety lesson wobbles (ASK 2 and 3).
- Writer: Clean, funny, easy to say aloud (the pigeon falling off the roof, "Is that a sneeze?", Mr Epp muddling the song). Weak spots: "looked at each other" seven times, the clunky "We can hold both of those", and an opening joke ("TORCH WASN'T PLUGGED IN" in caps) that is a little hard to read aloud.
- Listener/parent: Peril is real (a child trapped under a table, smoke, a wall alight) but each beat is relieved quickly and Kenji's rescue, the firefighter's praise and the small, safe bonfire settle it well. Kind to Mr Epp, dignified for Prometheus and Zeus. Acceptable for bedtime. Copy-risk is low, with the caveats in ASK 2 and 3.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Ending ("THE ENDING (WITH A BOX)"): the twins shout "STOP. BREATHE. THINK!" and the story ends. There is no "Monday". The standing rule is the twins saying "Monday" at exactly the same time. Suggest keeping the shout (it is a good echo of the Epp lesson) and adding one beat after it: Maya, or the narrator, wonders what is in the box, and Alex and Felix both say "Monday." before "The End."
2. Part "THE SHOE": the lesson is "get out, stay out, never go back", yet the twins kneel on the doorstep of a cabin with smoke creeping under the door. A six-year-old could copy "go back and talk to someone in the smoke". Suggest Felix calls from the grass a few steps back from the open door, with Alex beside him, and Mrs Tibbs (the grown-up) standing at the door. Change "on the doorstep, where the air was clear" to something like "on the grass, well back from the door, where the air was clear". Mrs Tibbs's "Stay at the door" becomes "Stay back."
3. Parts "THE FIRE RULES" and "THE SHOE": Kenji hides under a table, and the story never says hiding is the wrong move; "stay low" appears only in the crisis. Suggest adding one short rule line ("If there is smoke, stay low under it and get out. Never hide.") or a line from Mrs Tibbs afterwards. The song needn't change.
4. Part "MR EPP": a grown-up lights a match near dry leaves and a cabin of children, and the only consequence is being sorry. The forgiveness is lovely, but a small concrete consequence would help a literal six-year-old (e.g. Mr Epp hands every match to Mr Prome, "for ever"). One line is enough.
5. Part "THE SHOE": Mrs Tibbs runs back to the doorway while the other eleven are at the oak tree. Add one line putting another teacher (Mr Shah is already nearby) in charge of the line, or the twins' count would be left unprotected.

## Minor notes
- Three mottos compete: "I can be scared and brave at the same time" (mid-peril, earned), "Careful is brave" (the closing line), and "Stop. Breathe. Think." (Mr Epp and the final shout). Consider keeping the scared-and-brave line as the affirmation and letting the other two serve as echoes.
- Bible lists Mr Zed as "probably Zeus; not named", but Maya states "That's ZEUS" outright and Mr Prome calls him Zeus. Update the Bible wording rather than the story.
- Costumes (bumblebee, goat ears) appear without explanation; a half-line ("it was fancy-dress story time") would make them natural.
- The Wednesday-night bedtime scene lets Felix and Alex both be right, a nice nod to Maya; it is the only quiet reflective beat, and is good.
- Hard words (forethought, afterthought, Epimetheus, reconsidering) are all explained or clear from context.
