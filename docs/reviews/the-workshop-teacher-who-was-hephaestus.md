# Review: The Workshop Teacher Who Was HEPHAESTUS
File: stories/the-workshop-teacher-who-was-hephaestus.md | Words: about 3,300 | Parts or sections: 22 (plain all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape: clear week (Mon to Fri), the myth (thrown off the mountain, the chair, Talos) drives the plot, and the Gideon mockery pays off twice. The chase is the most urgent stretch in the early stories. Slightly thin (about 700 short of 4,000), mostly because the three lap parts are very short. The "Monday" ending is missing (see ASK).
- Writer: Lively, funny and easy to say aloud; the child "we" voice holds, with good small jokes (the kettle, "Practice.", Mrs Ibbs correcting spelling). Tics: "looked at each other" about 5 times, "Mr Heston looked at ... for a long time" several times. "Bolt" and "plug" are used for the same thing.
- Listener/parent: Warm and dignified god; the limp is treated with real respect and "Different is clever" is earned. Hera throwing the baby and the Talos rampage are scary but relieved quickly. Ends on a monkey cliffhanger, not a settling image, but the Lesson part just before it settles well.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 15 spoken lines, notices first, calls "Now!", finds the bolt location, "I've got the spanner" | Felix: about 15 spoken lines, asks the questions, spots the bolt, first to stand up to Gideon | Goes first: Alex (their stated system). Verdict: balanced. Felix is the one who panics ("I can't hold on") and Alex encourages; one reversal somewhere would help but is not needed.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. The End (With a Banana): The twins say "Next week?" / "Obviously." instead of both saying "Monday" together. The series ritual is the same word at the same time. Suggest: "The twins looked at each other. 'Monday,' they said, at exactly the same time." then "The End." Also confirm the monkey (Hanuman, Mr Hanu) fits where story 11 (Pan) comes next; the Archive says 10 comes before 11, so the monkey hook may not lead directly to the next story.
2. The Chair (Again): Mr Heston leaves a child trapped, with friends laughing, until he apologises, and the chair was left in a shed full of children. It reads as slightly cruel and a safety slip, and sits oddly after Heston's own gentleness. Suggest: Heston says "Oh dear, I forgot that one was still plugged in" (or similar) and tells Gideon the chair "likes to hear something kind", so it feels like the chair's magic and a gift to Gideon, not a punishment set by the teacher.
3. The Bolt / The Second Lap: Heston promises "I'll be right behind you" and is never seen again until the end; and Dot's cushion does the real work of making Talos stumble (small-child-in-danger beat plus a rescue). Suggest one line during the chase where Heston is limping close behind and calls the twins on ("Left foot, the back of the heel!"), or gives Dot a plainer role, so the twins' victory is clearly theirs.
4. Length: about 700 words under the guide. The three lap parts could each take a laugh (Mr Harris, Mr Bish, the Inspector's clipboard) and a beat of the twins deciding together, without changing the plot.

## Minor notes
- Affirmations: three appear (scared and brave, I can do hard things, Different is clever). All are earned, but the first two are shouted in the same chase; could merge into one.
- "Robots" and "golden robot girls" for the helpers is fine for a child narrator.
- Respect: Hera's cruelty and Heston's trap are both handled with "feelings can be big, and we still need to be kind", which works well.
- Heading says "THE END (WITH A BANANA)"; harmless.
