# Review: The Lady Who Wasn't Asked
File: stories/the-lady-who-wasnt-asked.md | Words: ~4,350 | Parts: 16

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape and a clear myth echo (sixteen and one, "wasn't asked", forgiveness, "everybody needs a chair"). Affirmation is earned and the twins' "seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth chair" lands well. Weak spots: Part 15 is a long sunset speech with three lessons, and Mrs Oshun delaying while Dot is in danger feels slightly unfair. Hook is a real mystery but it is a big, looming shadow, not a settler.
- Writer: Lively, specific and funny (Coyote's sandwich, Hadad's cloud going pale, Jackson "should have counted"). Mostly breathable. Stock beats: leaning tower, small child in peril, staff meeting that goes wrong, sunset speech. Tics: "shouted" (about 15 in Parts 8 and 11), "whispered" (Part 4), "looked at ... looked at", "at the same time" outside the ritual, "underlined it three times". One unsuitable simile ("drunken giraffe").
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified portrayal of Oshun; the Osun river and sacred forest are mentioned with respect, and nothing is mocked. The twins' own "nobody saw our hands" hurt is a very relatable hook for a six-year-old. The ending (huge shadow, bristling cat, dropped pencil, voice "from the top of the world") is more thrilling than sleepy.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 15 spoken lines, key actions: asks Oshun why, sits beside her first, says "Take my hand" to Dot, gives the apology | Felix: about 16 spoken lines, key actions: kneels in the mud and asks "please", "Listen" at the gate, "I've got you", last word of Part 15 | Goes first: Alex (Part 2 "What about the pond?", first "Excuse me", first to sit with Oshun). Verdict: balanced. Felix has the single biggest moment (the kneeling "please") and Alex the apology and the staff-room opening, which evens out. Both get "I've got you" and the chairs lines.

2. Part 8: OLD: "The tower gave a sickening lurch." -> NEW: "The tower gave a great lurch." (gentler; also trims a hard word)
3. Part 8: OLD: "He looked at Felix. Felix looked at Alex. They both said it at the same time." -> NEW: "He turned to Felix, and Felix was already nodding. They both said it together." (removes "looked at" tic and saves "at the same time" for the Monday ritual)
4. Part 14: OLD: "Persephone's garden and Baba Yaga's cabbages grew side by side." -> NEW: "Miss Persey's garden and Baba Yaga's cabbages grew side by side." (continuity: she is Miss Persey everywhere else in the story)

## ASK fixes
1. Part 9: Mrs Oshun says "I wasn't asked" and stays still while Dot is stuck at the top of a falling tower. For a small listener this makes the goddess seem to bargain with a child in danger. Suggest keeping the "please" (it is the heart of the story) but have her already half-rising, with her hand on the mirror, so the pause is a beat, not a refusal.
2. Parts 7 and 15: the lesson says "tell someone how you feel", yet Oshun herself never told anyone; she waited, then told the twins only after the fact. Suggest one line in Part 13 or 15 where she admits she should have said "I'd like a chair" to Mister Harris, and that next time she will. It closes the gap between her advice and her behaviour, and stops Part 7's "stay, be gentle, hold out the honey, even when it hurts" reading as "just put up with it".
3. Part 15: the sunset speech holds four ideas (not your fault, tell someone, you may say yes, sweet is strong). Suggest cutting to "not your fault" plus "sweet is strong", since the second is the affirmation thread. Keep the twins' praise and "That is the whole trick."
4. Part 13: the twins' own staff-room hurt (their raised hands ignored in Part 5) is never directly acknowledged by the grown-ups. Suggest Mister Harris adds one line: "And we are sorry to Alex and Felix, who put their hands up and were not seen." Part 14 gives them small chairs, so this just names it.
5. Part 16: the hook is good ("small, lost things" is a real mystery) but the shadow, the bristling cat, the dropped pencil and the dark shape rolling over the field are scarier than a bedtime ending should be. Suggest softening: drop "a dark shape rolled over the school field, like a giant bird passing in front of the sun" (it repeats the previous sentence) and keep Oshun's calm smile as the last beat before "Monday".

## Minor notes
- Name clusters for the reader-aloud: Harris, Hadad and Heston all start with H, and Prome, Pann, Persey and Pele all start with P. The 16-name list in Part 4 is the hardest passage in the story to read aloud; it works because it is a list, but a parent will want to slow down. Canon names, so left alone.
- Part 5: Maya says "We'll go and tell Mrs Oshun", but in Part 6 Alex simply goes to sit beside her; nobody tells her anything. Harmless.
- Part 14: "seventeen chairs for the staff" does not obviously count Mister Harris, so "an eighteenth" in Part 13 is slightly muddled. Not worth fixing.
- Part 10: "Her skin glowed like polished wood" (Part 1) is fine and respectful; no concerns about Oshun's portrayal. Not fact-checked against the tradition, per the brief.
- Willow says "Meow" in Part 11; she is "never speaks" in the brief. It is an animal noise, so acceptable, but check the Bible's preference.
- Part 1 refers to Oshun "from the gate", which assumes the previous story's hook; fine in sequence.
