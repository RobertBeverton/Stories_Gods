# Review: The Sandwich Inspector Who Woke the Hill
File: stories/the-sandwich-inspector-who-woke-the-hill.md | Words: 4,006 | Parts: 15

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong comic engine (Coyote's monologue, the walking sandwich, the net of jumpers) and a clear problem that echoes the trickster's hunger. Only 15 parts against the 16 to 18 recipe, and Part 14 carries a long, double-moral wrap-up. Part 3 (Elfine's star story) is a talky stretch with no laugh.
- Writer: Voice is consistent and mostly breath-sized. Tic is "looked at each other" (five times). A few hard words (rangy, rhythmic, tarmac). One real continuity slip in Part 13 (sheep in the throat) and Willow seems to speak in Part 8.
- Listener/parent: Warm, funny, and Coyote is redeemed rather than punished. Dot being swallowed (Part 7) is frightening but is relieved fast in Part 9. The ending is a thunder-and-rain cliffhanger, so it is more exciting than sleepy; that is the series hook, but a parent may want to read it slowly.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 17 spoken lines, key actions: hides lunchboxes, works out where Coyote will come from, first to run toward Gobbler, "So we feed it" idea, leads the "ask and share" moral | Felix: about 19 spoken lines, key actions: sign on piano stool, asks Coyote "why", kneels to the little creature, names the hill-spirit | Goes first: Felix speaks first (Part 2), Alex acts first (Part 2 hiding). Verdict: balanced. Small asymmetry in Part 8 ("Alex took a deep breath. Felix took his hand") makes Alex the brave one and Felix the comforter; harmless, but one line of equal footing would help. The big moments (Dot's shout, Willow's sneeze) belong to others, which is fine.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Structure: 15 parts, recipe is 16 to 18. The easiest split is Part 14 (apology, Gobbler thanks, the Friday sandwich routine) into two, or give Part 9 and Part 10 a short extra beat. Also Part 15's title "The Ending, with a Hook" announces the mechanics; rename (e.g. "The Van at the Gate").
2. Part 14: the moral is stated three or four times (Gobbler, Felix "Everyone gets hungry", Dot "share", then Coyote's long speech, then "Everyone's good at something / everyone can be better"). The last pair of lines is not earned by the plot and muddles the "ask and share" affirmation. Suggest cutting Felix's and Alex's final two lines and trimming Coyote's speech to the "I listened" beat.
3. Part 7/9: Dot swallowed is the stock "small child in danger" beat. It works here because relief is fast and Dot is brave and loud, so just keep Part 9 as the immediate reassurance; do not lengthen the gap.
4. Part 3: Elfine's star story is a long lecture in the middle with no joke. Consider one Marion or Dot interruption with a laugh, or trim a few lines.
5. Part 8: Elfine says it is "the Nez Perce story" and that "we know how it goes". I cannot verify this and the Gobbler reads as invented for this story. For respect and accuracy, suggest a hedge such as "It's like an old story where a monster swallowed everyone." The same hedge in Part 7 ("There's an old story") is already safe.
6. Part 15: ends on thunder, a drumming ground and a stranger's voice, so it is not very settling. Consider one calming line (the hill's sleepy eye is a good start) just before the van arrives, or end the final image on the rain softly starting rather than a stranger calling out. The hook itself (Hadad, "I've come about the job") is a good mystery. Check "the same van we had seen the week before" against the previous story in the Archive; I did not verify it.
7. Part 6: Coyote's howl waking the hill is a sweet turn (joy causes the trouble). The Gobbler's swallowing of Mister Harris's car and the sheep is cartoonish but unthreatening; no change.

## Minor notes
- Willow's brave thing (the three-tap sneeze) is lovely and wholly silent. Her growl in Part 2 is fine.
- "Willow looked at the pebble. Then she looked at us. Then she looked at the door" is a nice use of the looking pattern; keep it.
- Coyote's tone is warm and he is respected by the end; mocking is aimed at his habits, not the tradition. Nothing flattens a living religion.
- Elfine and Marion appear: correct for story 5 (they are only retired from story 12 on).
- Front matter `characters` lists them too, which is right.
- Part 5: "Felix and Alex looked at each other. They had a way of doing that..." is the one look-at-each-other worth keeping.
- No "Year" references remain after fixes 1 and 2; "nine years old" for Willow is fine.
