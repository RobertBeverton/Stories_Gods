# Review: The Man with Two Faces
File: stories/the-man-with-two-faces.md | Words: ~4,120 (whole file; about 4,070 body) | Parts: 17

Note: this story already has narrated audio (audioDuration 1576). Any text change forces re-narration, so the DIRECT list is kept to age-proofing and a tic/continuity trim only.

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. The problem (two halves of one god refusing to speak, doors going wrong) echoes Janus well, and escalation (everywhere, locked, closing) is clean. Willow in the doorway is a real cliffhanger; the "both at once" solution is earned by the twins' first day. Affirmation is plot-earned. Hook (Brigid-style cow) is a real mystery, "Monday" lands. Parts 15 and 16 are two speeches back to back, but Part 15 is funny and warm enough to carry it.
- Writer: Voice is consistent and funny (Snap, bag of ducks going to sleep, "I cried at a lollipop", the sausage smell). "That's when" appears exactly four times (Parts 1, 8, 12, 17), at the limit. One out-of-ritual "at exactly the same time" (Part 5). Staff-meeting/tower stock beats are avoided. "Wretchedly" (Part 7) is the hardest word, but fine in context.
- Listener/parent: Gentle, funny, and the ending (key-turning, "Goodbye, Monday / Hello, Tuesday", rain stopping, one star) settles a child well before the hook. The "sad and glad at once" message is a good one for a child who cries at drop-off, and the ending Brigid hook is light. Mister Harris's confession is moving without being mocked.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 17 spoken lines, key actions: walks through the green door (Part 1), the "both" realisation (Part 10), "door you wave through" (Part 12), first key and "Goodbye, Monday" (Part 17) | Felix: about 21 spoken lines, key actions: notices the doors (Part 1), diagnoses "it's about the faces" and starts the first-day memory (Parts 9-10), goes first to the quiet face (Part 11), "Hello, Tuesday" (Part 17) | Goes first: Felix (Part 11, titled). Verdict: balanced. Felix leads Parts 9-11, but Alex gets the conclusion in Part 10 and the best line in Part 12, and each gets a face, a key and a closing line. Opening of the first-day memory gives each twin a sad turn and a glad turn, which is even-handed.

## Minor notes
- Part 12: "And we were still sad about the old ones. But you can be." is slightly unclear read aloud ("you can be" what?). Could be "But you can be sad and glad." Optional; not worth re-narration alone.
- Part 13: "I've been shouting at you for two days" sits oddly with the doors only misbehaving since Sunday night. Harmless.
- Part 2/Part 7: "We had seen him in the doorway on Friday night" is never followed up. Harmless.
- Part 4: the Janus temple-doors detail ("almost always open") is a sombre note; handled gently by "holding the door for people on their way out" and the sigh. Fine for the audience.
- Respect for tradition: Janus is warm, dignified and the point of the story; no mockery. Bedsheet/plum joke is aimed at Mister Harris, not the Roman world.
