# Review: The Lady Who Kept the Apples
File: stories/the-lady-who-kept-the-apples.md | Words: ~4,265 | Parts: 18

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. The giant's loneliness and Idun's "all you had to do was ask" (Parts 9 to 10, 15) make the myth echo and the theme land. The weak point is a logic hole: golden apples litter the lane (and each twin owns one), yet the staff stay old all day. Part 14 has Thor raising his hammer at a helpless creature, which is a mild stock beat. The hook (two-faced keeper of doors) is a real mystery.
- Writer: Funny, specific details (Baba Yaga going young, Harris's "moustache today", "It's compost", the fly saying "Left!"). A few slips: "we" narrator is used in Part 9 while the twins are alone, Willow speaks, and some phrasing is odd ("I did the first wrong"). The "looked at each other" tic appears three times.
- Listener/parent: Warm and funny, with a good flying set-piece. Ending is mostly settling, but doors banging in the dark is a slightly jumpy last image. Part 17's three messages in a row (getting older, ask, say sorry, look after each other) is a long sunset speech.

## ASK fixes
1. Part 1 / Part 5: Plot hole. Golden apples are all over the lane and each twin kept one, yet nobody thinks to feed them to the aged staff. Suggest either (a) Maya or Heimdall explains in Part 3 or 5 that the scattered apples only work when Idun herself hands them over (a line like "They're only apples without her. They keep the glow but not the magic"), or (b) the twins' kept apples are quietly dropped from Part 1. Option (a) is one extra line and keeps Part 1's lovely image.
2. Part 7 to 12: Willow's one brave thing is weak (hissing in the hood). Setup in Part 6 ("She'll know what to do") has no payoff. Suggest Willow doing one decisive silent act in the chase, for example pulling the loose feather back into place or nudging the cloak's pocket shut so the nut cannot fall out.
3. Part 14: Thor raising the hammer at a fallen, frightened giant. The twins stopping it is good, but it is a stock "grown-up about to do harm" beat. Consider Thor only gripping the hammer and growling, or Mrs Freyja, rather than Thor, being the cross one. Also clarify that the giant is an eagle with a beard (Part 14 "bird", Part 15 "beard"); one clause would do.
4. Part 17: Idun's speech stacks four messages (getting older is a gift, ask, say sorry, look after each other). Suggest keeping "ask" (plot-earned) and "say it out loud" in one short exchange, and cutting "Getting older is not a punishment" and the "secret" paragraph, or moving one to Part 16. This also helps the child settle.
5. Part 18: Hook is real and well-planted, but the doors "banging open and shut... trying every handle" in the dark may be jumpy at the very end of a bedtime story. Consider "swinging gently open, one after another" and keeping the two smiling faces.

## Minor notes
- Dot's "I'll look out for eagles!" has no payoff; fine as a throwaway.
- "Maya mentions Odin but he never appears"; harmless.
- Part 4 twin call-and-response ("It's okay to be scared" / "But it's not okay to pretend") is sweet but near-formulaic; leave it.
- Part 18 heading "The Ending, with a Hook" reads as a note to self; a title like "Welcome, Goodbye" is more in keeping with the other headings.
