# Review: The Cook Who Was Quetzalcoatl
File: stories/the-cook-who-was-quetzalcoatl.md | Words: about 4,260 | Parts: 21 (recipe says 16 to 18)

Note: I read the brief and the story closely. I did not open the Bible, Guide or Archive, so affirmation count, roster and the "seriesOrder: 1" question are checked only against the brief.

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. Clear problem (the ants will not share), a clever and kind solution (listen, make yourself small), and a real echo of the myth. Parts 2 to 5 are four lore-and-list parts in a row before the Bump scene, so the front is slow. At 21 parts it is three to five over recipe. Hook (Maui's fish hook, "I think I've got a bite!") is a good mystery and "Monday" lands with both twins.
- Writer: Clean, funny, easy to read aloud. Best lines: Thorley's "Popcorn.", the 11-page speech, "Nobody ever listens to us." Weak spots: "He said," / "She said," carries almost all the dialogue from Part 9 on, "looked at each other" recurs, and the Tibbs / Ibbs / Ebbie names sit too close.
- Listener/parent: Warm, gentle, never too scary (the dark is relieved at once by the lantern and the affirmation). The god is dignified and kind, and the ants are treated with respect. Ending is a hook, not a wind-down, but it is light and playful. Too many affirmations at the end, see ASK 2.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 23 spoken lines; notices the carrying ants, says "It's a leak", carries the lantern, calls "Keep going!" | Felix: about 24 spoken lines; asks the first questions, says "Let's ask" (the key insight), directs the digging, is the one who gets the cold drop | Goes first: Alex (Parts 8, 9, 11, 14), though Felix opens Part 1 with the question. Verdict: balanced. Each has the big idea at a different point (Felix Part 8, Alex Part 14), and the dig is explicitly shared.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. Part 1: OLD: "Good morning, Year One," he said -> NEW: "Good morning, class," he said (age-proofing)
2. Part 6: OLD: Mister Ketz took Year One to the bottom of the field -> NEW: Mister Ketz took the whole class to the bottom of the field (age-proofing)
3. Part 8 heading: OLD: ## Part Eight. The Boys Notice -> NEW: ## Part Eight. The Twins Notice (matches "The Twins Think", Part 14)
4. Part 9: OLD: Alex and Felix looked at each other. -> NEW: Alex and Felix nodded. (trims a "looked at each other" tic; there are five in the story)
5. Part 14: OLD: They looked at each other. They both knew. -> NEW: They both knew. (same tic)
6. Part 10: OLD: Mister Harris turned into a giant, then a mountain -> NEW: Mister Harris grew into a giant, then a mountain (it is the twins who change, not him)
7. Part 17: OLD: took a handle each, lifted the great golden kernel -> NEW: took an end each, lifted the great golden kernel (a kernel has no handles)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Length: 21 parts. Merge or cut to reach 18. Candidates: Parts 4 and 5 (the list and Sholotl, both Maya reveal beats), Parts 15 and 16 (the dig and the glug), Parts 18 and 19 (the way out and the corn comes out). Parts 2, 3 and 5 are three Maya lectures; trimming one would tighten the early middle.
2. Parts 9, 12, 15, 20: five affirmations ("scared and brave", "I am a seed", "I can do hard things", "I am not alone", then the festival round-robin of all of them). The standing rule is one affirmation woven into the story. Suggest keeping one (the seed one fits the dark and the corn best) and cutting the Part 20 recital. Dot's "I am not alone" is not earned by anything Dot does in the story.
3. Part 21: the star is called Ketz's "morning star", but it is night and the story told us (Part 5) that Sholotl is the evening star. Suggest Maya says "That's the evening star", Kenji asks whose it is, and the dog sits beside her. It is tidier and a lovely brother echo.
4. Part 14: Felix quotes "Mister Yu said, make a path, not a wall." Mister Yu only appears as a channel in Part 1, and the line is never given in this story. The front matter says seriesOrder 1, so listeners have no source for it. Either plant the line in Part 1 or cut it from Part 14.
5. Part 20: Mister Ebbie's "every time anyone asked for butter, we had to knock on the table" is unclear aloud; a parent will stumble and a child will not get it. Clarify the gag or cut it.
6. Part 20: Ketz's "Everything big begins very small" speech is a stock sunset-speech beat and repeats the Part 9 lesson. Consider trimming to the one line "Listen small, grow big."
7. Willow is not in this story at all (cast: Alex, Felix, Maya, Dot, Kenji, Ollie). Check the Bible for whether she should have her one brave silent moment.

## Minor notes
- "He said," / "She said," dominates the exchanges in Parts 9, 13, 16, 17. Mixing in an action beat or a plain line would help the read-aloud.
- Part 3: "Sustenance Mountain" is a hard word for a six-year-old; it will pass as a name but is worth a thought for the narrator.
- Part 6: "And it's had a very long summer" is a slightly odd joke for the Bump; a child may not follow it.
- Part 19: Tibbs, Ibbs, Ebbie, plus Felicity, Hanu, Heston, Thorley, Jackson. Fine on the page but hard to track by ear. Missus Ibbs especially sounds like Tibbs.
- Part 7 and Part 8: "hitting them" is a lot of adult violence on the ants, but it is played comically and put right. Fine.
- Part 21: "an enormous, shining bone" for the hook is a nice detail. The ending light-heartedness suits the series.
