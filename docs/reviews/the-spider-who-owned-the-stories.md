# Review: The Spider Who Owned the Stories
File: stories/the-spider-who-owned-the-stories.md | Words: ~3,990 | Parts: 15

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong, clear problem that echoes the myth (ownership of stories, the pot, the shared-story knot), and the ending is earned. Two issues: only 15 parts against the 16-18 recipe, and Parts 3-4 are about 900 words of Elfine retelling the myth with almost no plot movement, so the early middle sags (the Part 4 ending is a good page-turner, though). The affirmation ("stories are something you give") is short and plot-earned. The Hadad hook is clear and "Monday" lands with both twins.
- Writer: Clean, speakable sentences and funny specifics (eight pockets, the Reading Cat lanyard, "we shall not discuss the middle", the unlucky banana). Tics to trim: "pushed her glasses up her nose" (three times), "looked at each other" (Parts 5 and 15), "exactly the same thought". "That's when" appears three times, which is fine. Part 4's retelling has a lot of "said Elfine" with no speaker problems, but the python and leopard beats are dense for read-aloud.
- Listener/parent: Warm, funny and safe; Anansi is dignified and gets a real, kind redemption, and the Akan setting is handled with respect and not mocked. The storm-van hook at the end is exciting rather than calming, which is the series pattern, but the Part 14 assembly and Willow's nose-touch give a gentle landing before it.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 16 spoken lines; key actions: asks about Loki and the hornets, "We need to find him", the "story needs a listener" speech, first to grab the rope after Marion, "That's what Maui and the brothers said", suggests telling a story, opens the final assembly tale | Felix: about 18 spoken lines; key actions: challenges Anansi in Part 5, the "what's the fun in it?" speech and "clever isn't the same as kind", starts the shared story, rhymes the assembly tale | Goes first: Felix in the key moments (Part 5 challenge, Part 8 speech, Part 12 opener); Alex opens the Part 14 telling. Verdict: Balanced. Felix has the slightly larger moral beats in Part 8, but Alex gets the "listener" line in the same scene and the Maui callback in Part 10, so it evens out. Willow never speaks and does the brave thing (stops the pot).

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Length/structure: 15 parts is under the 16-18 recipe. Consider splitting Part 4 (the four tasks) into two parts, e.g. python and leopard, then hornets and fairy, each ending on a small hook, which would also relieve the sag. Alternative is to leave as is since the word count is within range.
2. Part 4: The fairy beat ("she got cross and hit it. And she got stuck") and the leopard being tied to a tree are the myth's own tricks but read slightly mean for bedtime. Optionally soften the leopard line ("let the tree spring up" is fine) and keep the fairy's anger lighter, e.g. "she got cross and poked it".
3. Part 5/7: The cause of the vanishing stories (the pot "has got hungry", Anansi "letting it") is a little vague. A single line from Anansi in Part 7 or 8 clarifying that the pot takes any story told near it would make the cause clearer for a six-year-old.
4. Part 15: The hook is clear, but Hadad arrives with no new question beyond "who is he". Fine for the series pattern; flagging only that the ending is high-energy rather than sleepy. Option: add one calm closing line before the storm if the parent wants a softer finish.

## Minor notes
- Elfine and Marion appear throughout and were later retired; fine for this story.
- Part 1 "Willow is nine" and Part 9 "faster than a nine-year-old cat" are fine (Willow's age is not the children's).
- Hard-ish words ("teetered", "straining", "seeped", "precious") are all supported by context.
- Part 1 first Willow sentence ("in a way that showed she thought it was a bit beneath her") is a long one but speakable.
