# Review: The Dinner Men Who Were SEVEN LUCKY GODS

## Verdicts
- Editor: Fun, clear shape (arrive, one god per section, a problem, a goodbye), but thin at about half the 4,000-word guide. Parts 6 to 10 repeat one pattern (spot god, badge, Kenji explains, one gag), and the Friday problem arrives late and is solved entirely by the gods while the children watch. No affirmation, no hook, no "Monday" (early-story shape; noted, not failed).
- Writer: Very readable and lively, with strong gags (the SPOON mix-up, "It was dripping out of time", "One collar and two sleeves", "By ENOUGH"). Two small clarity slips (who says "They're lucky", Mr Daikon coming round a corner and also already sitting down). "We all ..." beats recur (looked, said, nodded, stopped), and "woodpecker convention" is an adult joke.
- Listener/parent: Warm and funny, and the ending settles well: the flag growing into a sail, seven small hands waving, then the pillow picture. Respect is good (see Tone and respect). One age-proofing flag: "KING OF YEAR 2".

## Twin balance

Verdict: Twins are not leads in this early class, so no fix required, but Alex is missing and Felix is only a bit-part. Willow is absent too (fine).

## Tone and respect (living Japanese traditions)
- Largely good. The gods are warm, dignified-funny and never mocked: Bishamon is fierce and gets the last word of thanks, Benzaiten stops the tap, the Seven save the day, and the knocking (Ebisu's shrine custom) is presented as kindness that the whole school joins in, not as ridicule. Kenji's gran and the pillow picture give a respectful family-practice frame.
- Watch: Mr Ebbie's mishearing gag ("EH?" / "a SPOON?") leans on a hearing-loss joke. It is affectionate and he is rewarded, and the story ties it to the tradition, so I would keep it. Parents may like a beat of the children being kind about it (the knocking already does that).
- Mild flag: nothing in the story says these are figures people actually honour today, or that they are drawn from Shinto and Buddhist traditions. Kenji's gran is the only living link. See ASK 2.
- Name puns (Daikon, Hot, Jury, Bish) are light and only on the staff badges; the god names are given correctly and respectfully by Kenji. I did not fact-check myth details.

## Order evidence (story 4 Felicitas vs story 5)
Weak evidence, leaning 4 then 5, and nothing conclusive:
- Opening formula: story 5 opens "The first time we saw THEM was on a Monday"; story 4 opens "The first time we saw Mrs Felicity was on a Monday". Same formula, which suggests they were written as a pair, but it does not fix the order.
- Part 1 of story 5: "Even Felix, who is lucky, looked like he'd lost a pound". Story 4 is where Felix learns his name means lucky, so this reads as a nod back to 4. That favours 4 first (weakly; it could also be a joke that 4 then explains).
- Lollipop man: story 4 has Mrs Felicity give a voucher "to the lollipop man". Story 5 introduces Mr Bish as "the new lollipop man". If 4 is first, there was an earlier lollipop man (fine). If 5 is first, 4's lollipop man could be Mr Bish, which is a pleasant tie but unprovable.
- Mr Harris, Miss Riley, Reception children and the Summer Gala/school fair appear in both; no character is introduced as new in either, so no tie-break.
- Endings: neither has a hook. Story 4 ends on an ice-cream van and "The End."; story 5 ends on the boat-bus sailing away and "The End." No hook points to the other. Verdict: unresolved; keep 4 then 5 unless the author knows otherwise.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. Part 2 (NUMBER TWO): OLD: "a cardboard crown that said **KING OF YEAR 2**." -> NEW: "a cardboard crown that said **KING OF THE PLAYGROUND**." (age-proofing: removes the year group)
2. Part 2 (NUMBER TWO): OLD: "He said, "They're lucky."" -> NEW: "Kenji said, "They're lucky."" (ambiguous speaker: reads as Mr Daikon or Kenji)
3. Part 2 (NUMBER TWO): OLD: "He had a little wooden hammer in his other hand and he was sitting on a pile of rice bags." -> NEW: "He had a little wooden hammer in his other hand, and he sat down on a pile of rice bags." (he has just come round the corner, so "was sitting" is a continuity slip)
4. Part 1 (THE FISH): OLD: "looked like he'd lost a pound." -> NEW: "looked like he'd lost a pound coin." (clearer for a young listener; "a pound" can sound like weight)
5. Part 1 (THE KNOCKING): OLD: "It sounded like a woodpecker convention." -> NEW: "It sounded like a hundred woodpeckers." (hard word for a six-year-old; the joke survives)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Parts 11 and 12 (THE BIG PROBLEM, THE DEPARTURE): the children do nothing to solve the pool problem; seven gods do it in one paragraph. Suggest giving one or two children a small part (for example, Kenji tells Mr Harris which god can help, or Alex and Felix knock on Mr Ebbie's table so he hears the fish-pie idea). This also fixes the missing Alex and adds weight to the thin middle.
2. Part 3 (WHO IS EBISU?) or Part 4: consider one line from Kenji that these are gods people still honour at home and at shrines and temples (for example, his gran says thank you to the picture each New Year), so the story states they are living figures and not only characters. Keep it short.
3. Length and shape: about 2,030 words against a 4,000-word guide. If it is expanded, the natural room is Parts 6 to 10: give two or three of the gods a small mishap or a child interaction beyond "badge, Kenji explains, one gag", and let the problem start earlier (for example, hint at the pool trouble in Part 2 or 3).
4. Ending: there is no affirmation, no hook and no "Monday". The ending is gentle and works, but if the story is brought in line, add a small hook (what the boat-bus is sailing toward) and the twins saying "Monday" together, plus a short affirmation earned by the knocking ("listening is a kind of kindness" or similar). Optional for an early story.

## Minor notes
- Mr Bish stopping a bus so hard that "all its passengers fell forward" is a mild peril played for a laugh; it is fine.
- "We all ..." (looked, nodded, stopped, said, turned) is a steady tic across the story; trim one or two on any later edit.
- Timeline: the Gala "happened" between Friday's assembly and Saturday morning, a little tight but not confusing aloud.
- Good lines to keep: Mr Hot's stickers ("YOU ARE FELIX", "Nobody knows how he knew"), the three mice with crumbs, and the sail growing out of the flag.
