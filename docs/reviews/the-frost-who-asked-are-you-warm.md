# Review: The Frost Who Asked "Are You Warm?"
File: stories/the-frost-who-asked-are-you-warm.md | Words: about 4,526 | Parts: 16

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape and a real emotional core (the question turned back on the god; the Snow Maiden's melting handled gently with the water cycle). Parts 15 and 4/9/11 all state the same moral, so the sermon in Part 15 is the weakest part, and the rescue has a logic gap (how Posy leaves the sleigh, how Frost learns she is missing). Hook (Bast) is a real mystery and lands well.
- Writer: Clean, speakable, funny details (snow dog sliding off its legs, "Two minutes. Which is a lot."). Tics: "very" appears about 57 times; "a hundred years/winters" and "for a very long time" repeat; Part 15 is a stock sunset speech. A few hard words (wistful, hesitated, plumes, headdress) are fine in context.
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified Father Frost, respectful of the Morozko/Snegurochka material. Posy's rudeness is put right, and nobody is shamed. Ending with the cat-goddess arriving and Willow trembling is exciting rather than sleepy, but it is the series pattern and the Part 14 to 15 run is calm enough to settle first.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 13 spoken lines, key actions: asks the question first, gives the scarf, leads with Willow, gives his coat | Felix: about 10 spoken lines, key actions: confirms with a nod, asks "Are you *you* warm?", gives the hat, spots the snowflake, hugs Posy first, asks Posy "Are you warm?" | Goes first: Alex (Part 4 and Part 7). Verdict: balanced, a slight Alex lead in lines; Felix has equal big moments. Dot, not the twins, carries Parts 3, 13 and 14.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part 6 to 11: Posy clings to the back of a flying sleigh, then ends up alone under a pine, and Father Frost arrives saying "They said a girl was missing" with no one having reached him. Add one or two sentences: she slips off when the sleigh hits the storm, Miss Snow's owl (or the glowing snowflake) tells Frost, and he lands because of it. Also lets Miss Snow share the rescue.
2. Part 7: Mister Harris refuses, then Missus Tibbs lets six children and a cat walk into a blizzard. Parents may wince. Suggest Missus Tibbs or Mister Harris also tells them to stay on the rope and turn back at the big oak, or have Harris follow behind with a torch, so the adults are not simply negligent.
3. Part 15: Father Frost's speech repeats the moral already shown in Parts 4, 9 and 11, in a low-gold-sun scene. Suggest cutting the "real test" paragraph and keeping only "Everyone gets another chance" and "Everything that ends is just the middle of something else", which is the best line in the story and earns the affirmation.
4. Part 2: Maya is named at the bench but Elfine does all the explaining (era-appropriate for story 11) and Maya has one line ("We shouldn't") in the whole story. Optional: give Maya the Morozko telling, or a line of evidence, so the Bible's explainer is not a ghost.
5. Front matter: `characters` omits Maya, Mister Harris, Posy; fine if intentional.
