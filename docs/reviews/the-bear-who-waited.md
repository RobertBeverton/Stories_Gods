# Review: The Bear Who Waited
File: stories/the-bear-who-waited.md | Words: about 4,270 | Parts: 17

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. The myth's "tiger gives up, bear waits" is kept and then kindly turned (the tiger is redeemed, not shamed), and the rescue in Parts 9 to 13 is the real engine. Weak spots: the 100-day arithmetic does not add up, and Part 16 stacks too many morals in a sunset speech. The hook (flying shadow, golden apples, a voice shouting "THOSE ARE MY APPLES!") is a proper mystery with a funny voice.
- Writer: Clean, speakable, specific jokes (garlic bin, "It's *a* sandwich", the hat that sneezes, "a very good dog"). Tics are light: "looked at" twice, "underlined three times" once, "That's when" once. A handful of hard words for a six-year-old. "Very" is heavy in Parts 8 to 12. The "we" narrator slips in Part 10 ("the four of us" includes Willow, then "behind us" comes the staff).
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified and respectful. Hwanung is gentle and never played for a laugh; the tiger is a protector of children, not a cartoon. Parts 8 to 11 (storm, night, flooded river) are the scariest stretch, and relief comes quickly in 12 and 13. The ending settles, but Part 17's shouted voice and flying shadow is more of a startle than a lullaby.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 16 spoken lines, key actions: sits with the bear, politely tells Baba Yaga to go, spots the trail up the hill, leads the search, comforts the tiger | Felix: about 16 spoken lines, key actions: sits with the tiger, whispers her through the temptation, runs down the bank, delivers the "enough" line | Goes first: Alex (Parts 3, 4, 5, 8, 10). Verdict: balanced. Each twin gets a partner (Alex with the bear, Felix with the tiger) and Felix owns the emotional peak. Willow only growls and leads, and her one "Meow" in Part 1 is the only sound she makes (see Minor notes).

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Parts 4, 7, 8, 14: the timeline does not work. Mister Hwanung says each morning outside is ten days inside, ready "by Friday". Ten mornings are needed for 100 days, but only Monday to Friday exist. Day Twenty is Tuesday-ish, Day Sixty is "Thursday afternoon", and Day One Hundred is "Friday". Also Part 7 (headed Days Forty to Sixty) has Dot at forty-one marks "by the end of the first week", and Part 14 says "a hundred marks, give or take". Suggest simplest fix: make it "Every break on the outside will be ten days on the inside", which gives roughly two a day and 100 by Friday, then lightly label Part 8 as "Day Sixty, Thursday break" and drop "end of the first week" in Part 7. A six-year-old will not check, but an eight-year-old will.
2. Part 16: Mrs Ungnyeo's speech carries three morals (hard things take time, look at one day, the tiger's "giving up is not the end"), and the tiger then adds a fourth ("you do not have to be a person to be good"), which repeats Felix's Part 12 line. It is also a sunset speech, a stock beat. Suggest keeping "One day at a time" (earned, and it echoes Part 5) and the tiger's "I came back. And that was the bravest bit", and cutting the "You cannot hurry a seed" paragraph and the tiger's last line. The affirmation then stays short and plot-earned.
3. Part 17 (ending): after the quiet Part 16, the final scene ends on a shouted complaint and a snatched figure, which pulls the energy up at bedtime. Suggest putting the lullaby images last: let the shouting voice fade first, then end on "The apples went on rolling... glowing softly" (already there) and keep "Monday" as the final beat. The structure is nearly right, so this is only a reorder of two or three sentences.
4. Parts 8 to 9: Mister Hwanung says "Let her go" and then the adults take hours to notice Dot is missing, and the children go into a stormy wood at night. It works as a fairy-tale, and Part 13 gives Mister Harris real relief, but a parent may flinch. Optional: have Mister Harris say in Part 13 "and nobody is ever, ever to follow a tiger into the woods again, even a nice one", which is funny and settles the worry.

## Minor notes
- Willow says "Meow" in Part 1, which is translated by the narrator. The brief says she never speaks. It is charming and probably fine as a sound, not a line; flag it if you want strictness.
- Hard words that are safe with context: "inspector", "torrent", "gully", "tremendous", "honourable" (Part 14, worth keeping for dignity), "accurate".
- Part 9's "She had never been told not to follow a tiger" and Dot's yellow raincoat are the best jokes in the rescue.
- "very" is overused in Parts 8 to 12 (the tiger "very big, very tired, very sorry cat" is good; most others can go).
- Stock beats used: small child in danger (here the tiger rescues, not the twins, which freshens it) and sunset speech (Part 16). The staff meeting that goes wrong is absent, which is a plus.
- Hook source and Coyote/Baba Yaga cameos are consistent with the Bible; Gobbler Hill continuity is correct.
