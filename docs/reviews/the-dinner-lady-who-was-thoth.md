## Verdicts
- Editor: Charming and clear shape (menu, one leg, counting, list, hieroglyphs, dice, reveal, hook), but at under half the 4,000 guide it is thin, and the middle is mostly Nell lecturing. The five-minutes logic muddles (see ASK 1).
- Writer: Funny specifics (CHIKEN NUGETS, fourteen peas, "Ibis. Ibbs."). Too much "Nell said that... She said that..." reported lecture, which is hard to read aloud; the pronunciation sentence is a tangle. Capitals and bold are heavy but suit the voice.
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified Thoth, a lovely "I shall write that I never said so", and a gentle truth-about-words scene. The monkey hook is fun but a bedtime parent gets a wake-up ending rather than a settling one.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Parts "THE LITTLE DICE GAME" to "THE FIVE MINUTES": the logic runs both ways. Mr Harris has five EXTRA minutes he cannot fit anything into, Mrs Ibbs "helps" by winning, and then the bell rings five minutes LATE, giving the children extra play. Winning time from him should leave less, not more, and "the moon game" gave Thoth extra days, not took them. Suggest: Mr Harris's timetable is five minutes SHORT, Mrs Ibbs wins the dice and the five minutes appear, and the late bell follows. Also Mr Harris is quietly cheated by a god; a line of thanks or a wink from Mrs Ibbs would keep it kind rather than sly.
2. Length: about 1,900 words. If the story is to be brought nearer 4,000 it needs a second set-piece (for example, the children's own attempt at a hieroglyph test, or Ibbs correcting something that turns out to matter) rather than padding. Otherwise accept as a short early story.
3. "THE ENDING (BUT NOT THE END)": Kofi and Archie's "hamster" jokes have no setup in this story. Either add one earlier mention of a class hamster, or change to "That's NOT a cat" / "That's the biggest cat..." type joke. Also the story never hands the monkey to a settling note; consider a closing quiet line after the mango (Archie's lunchbox mango being a calm image).
4. "THE TRUTH ABOUT WORDS": the affirmation is spoken as a lecture ("Do you know why writing matters?"). It is short and earned, but could be tied to a plot beat (for example, her noticing Kofi's hieroglyph day) instead of a sit-down speech.
