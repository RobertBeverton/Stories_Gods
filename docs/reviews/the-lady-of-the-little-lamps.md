# Review: The Lady of the Little Lamps
File: stories/the-lady-of-the-little-lamps.md | Words: about 4,600 (wc, includes front matter) | Parts: 16

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. Each part turns the page, the middle (Ravana's argument, the arrow, the lamps) never sags, and the squirrel/lamp/"small lights" idea echoes the myth and earns the affirmation. The weak spot is Part 16: the Thursday-flashback structure muddles the "Monday" hook, and the Part 16 heading is a meta label that would be read aloud.
- Writer: Lively and funny (the ten bickering heads, Hanuman's tray-of-mountain, Willow and the owl nodding). Mostly easy to say aloud. A few slips: the ring/bracelet continuity in Parts 7 to 8, "Shut up" twice, "tiny" overused, "looked at each other" three times, "shrieked" three times.
- Listener/parent: Warm, funny, and the arrow-and-snakes scare (Part 9) is relieved fast. Ravana's fate is soft (glitter, a gentle silence) and Dot's "Was he bad?" handles it kindly. Part 15 settles the children well, but Part 16 then ends on a creepy, jumpy hook after a calm Diwali night, which is slightly at odds with sleep.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 26 spoken lines, key actions: takes the bracelet, strokes Dot's stripes, speaks the offer to Ravana, holds the bow in front | Felix: about 22 spoken lines, key actions: spots the deer is a trick, notices the lamps ("Look"), holds the bow from behind, notices the tenth head | Goes first: Alex in the arc, but Felix gets the first big save (Part 5, trusting Willow). Verdict: balanced. Alex slightly ahead (Dot moment, front of the bow); Felix's deer moment and "Look" offset it. The Dot-stripes moment is Alex only; optional to give Felix a line there.

Willow does her brave thing (hissing at the deer, and at the snakes) and never speaks. Good. Elfine/Marion are in range for this story per the Bible (stories 3 to 11).

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part 16: the timeline muddles the hook. Diwali night is "Sunday", then the story jumps back to "Thursday, a few days before" for Baba Yaga, so the twins say "Monday" on a Thursday, and the school Monday has already been and gone. Suggest: stay on Diwali Sunday. Let the fireworks (or the lamps) be where the hut thumps down the hill, and the twins say "Monday" that same night. This also drops Bonfire Night, a British-calendar reference that dates the scene, and the fireworks fit Diwali anyway.
2. Part 16: the hook is a creepy beat (cackling face in the window, "I have such a wonderful recipe") after a warm Diwali ending. It is a real mystery and matches the Baba Yaga story, but consider softening one line (for example "chin like a boot", "beady eyes glinted") so the last image before sleep is more odd than frightening, and keep Mrs Lakshmi's lamps in the final image.
3. Part 15: Mrs Lakshmi's closing speech is long and slightly sermon-like (lamp/small lights, then brothers/real wealth, then "light isn't the absence of being scared"). The last line is the good one and short enough to be the affirmation. Suggest trimming the "That is real wealth. Not gold. Not coins..." paragraph, or folding it into one sentence.
4. Part 8: optional balance. Alex alone strokes Dot's stripes. Felix could give the squirrel one line ("Be the bravest squirrel" or similar), or the stripes could appear when both twins touch her. Low priority.

## Minor notes
- Part 3 retells the whole Ramayana before it is acted out, including the ending. Fine for read-aloud, and Kenji's Hanuman poster is a nice beat, but it is the longest stretch of telling without a laugh.
- Respect for the tradition is good: Mrs Lakshmi is warm and dignified, Diwali is explained clearly, Ravana's heads are comic but he is still called clever and could have been great. Mister Hanu as PE teacher is gently playful rather than mocking. A reader sensitive to the tradition may still want to glance at the ten-heads "Did anyone bring biscuits?" running joke; it reads as affectionate to me.
- Part 6: "five on top and five below" is hard to picture; "ten faces, all talking at once" is enough.
- Words to watch for age six: "villainy", "chief engineer", "loyal"; all well supported by context.
- Part 1 note "Please don't touch the lamps" is never paid off; it is a nice setup that could be echoed when the first diya is lit in Part 4.
