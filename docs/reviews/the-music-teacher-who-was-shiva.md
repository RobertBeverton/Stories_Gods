## Verdicts
- Editor: Funny, tidy "evidence" structure, and the soup/blue-throat payoff is a clever, well-seeded reworking of the myth. But at about 1,200 words it is under a third of the 4,000-word guide, so it plays as a long sketch, not a bedtime story; it is a 6-8 minute read. There is no affirmation, no hook for a next god, and no "Monday" moment; it just stops on the scarf joke.
- Writer: Short, breath-sized sentences and good read-aloud rhythm ("Mr Shah did it for twenty minutes and a pigeon"; "somewhere between a swamp and a crayon"). Tics are light ("That's when" once, "looked at each other" once). Heavy capitals and bold shouting suit the voice but will need the narrator to be steered.
- Listener/parent: Warm and dignified toward Shiva: he is calm, kind, patient with the snake and takes the "poison" on himself for others. The jokes land on the children's theories and the soup, never on the god or his family. The ending is a quiet laugh but not a settle-down; it is a little brisk for sleep.

## Twin balance
Alex and Felix do not appear (class is Poppy, Archie, Zainab, Freddie; Poppy leads, Archie second). Not applicable for a story 1-7 class. Within the cast Poppy has most of the big moments, Zainab and Freddie act as sceptics, which is a fair spread.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Whole story, length: roughly 2,800 or more words short of the guide. If the series wants it expanded, the natural room is one or two more "evidence" days (a trident/third-eye clue, the Ganga-in-the-hair moment given a proper scene, Mrs Rao and Ganesh as small characters in their own right) and a calmer middle so the soup lands harder. Alternatively leave it as a deliberately short early story and note that in the Archive.
2. The Ending: no affirmation and no hook. If the early stories are meant to match the later recipe, add a short plot-earned line (for example, being calm and kind when something frightening happens, which the snake and soup scenes already show) and a closing mystery pointing at the next god. At the very least the ending could slow down and settle: Mr Shah and Mrs Rao walking off is a good picture to end on.
3. The Soup / The Blue Throat: the myth reference (Shiva holding the poison in his throat for everyone's sake) is respectful as written and Poppy's whispered line explains it well. Only caution: the "soup" is also the dinner ladies' food, and Mrs Dunn pouring it away "and nobody ever mentioned it again" slightly dismisses her. A one-line kindness for her (Mr Shah thanks her for trying something new) would keep the grown-ups from being the butt of the joke. Optional.

## Minor notes
- Tradition check: names (Shah, Parvati Rao, Ganesh Rao) and the damaru, blue throat, Ganga hair, snake, dance and family are all used affectionately; Ganesh and the laddoo is gentle, and the children's "we didn't want to be rude to someone's son" is a sweet respect beat. Nothing mocks the faith; no changes needed on tone.
- "Mr Harris, the head teacher" matches the Bible; no continuity problem found.
- Day order runs Wednesday to the following Wednesday, then home time, with "Monday" used only as a school day here; no clash with the later ritual, but it is worth knowing if a hook is added.
