# Finale arc: Phase 3 consistency verification

Method: each row of `arc-ground-truth.md` that could conflict was tested against every scene where it matters. Only contradictions and gaps are reported, each with the conflicting quotes (tag `[chapter.part]`, machine-checked against the text). Severity: **H** a listener or parent would notice; **M** a careful reader would; **L** pedantic. Nothing has been edited yet.

## A. Contradictions (two quotes in conflict)

### C1 (H). The night clock runs backwards between 42 and 43
- «By midnight, the gods had come up with a great many ways of sending two children home.» [42.12]
- «It was very late when they left the hall.» [42.13] (so they reach Hel's house after midnight, then Ganglöt takes eleven minutes at the door)
- «She went off to the kitchen at ten past eleven» [43.8] and «Just before midnight, Ganglöt brought them porridge.» [43.8]
- **Fix (minimal, two words):** 42.12 «By midnight» → «By ten o'clock»; 42.13 «very late» → «late». The 43 timings then work as written.

### C2 (M). The two worlds' clocks differ only on Tuesday
- «It was evening there, though it had been ten past ten in the morning a moment ago.» [41.12]
- But the worlds share a clock on Wednesday and Friday: «a small, damp Bengal cat was sitting on the mat» at home time [45.6] and she arrives «in the dusk» [44.14]; «A long way away, on a hill with two moons, a cat came in at dawn.» [45.13] while «On Friday morning, the gate was open.» [45.12].
- The text does give a reason for time to misbehave: «Time wobbles first.» [40.6], but never says it settled.
- **Fix options:** (a) add after the 41.12 sentence: «Time wobbles first, Mister Janus had said, and it had.» (smallest; recommended); (b) cut the sentence and move the arrival to morning (breaks 42's feast and 43's night).

### C3 (M). Winter in week one, summer in week two
- Week 1: «A cold breeze came across the field.» [41.4]; «At five o'clock, the moon lifted herself off the school roof» [46.2] and «It was nearly dark.» [46.4].
- Twelve days later: «It was a warm one. The field was long and gold.» [47.1]; «it was the very last day of the school year» [47.1]; «She came up Gobbler Hill at seven o'clock, in the cool of the morning» [47.2]; «They were coming in September» [47.6].
- **Fix options (your call, design):** (a) make the last day the end of the autumn or spring term, not the year: change «school year» to «term», «warm»/«long and gold» to «bright», «September» to «January», sunrise at «eight», keep «Goodbye, this year» (fits Christmas); (b) make week 1 lighter: cut «A cold breeze», and move the climax later, which needs "nearly dark" at about eight (breaks "At five o'clock"). I recommend (a).

### C4 (M). Quilla is «so low» and «at the top of the sky» in the same part
- «so low over both of the hills, that she looked as if she had been poured» [46.11] and «I'm at the top of the sky,» [46.11]; promised «I shall be at the very top of the sky, exactly in the middle.» [45.11]; afterwards «She was not low any more.» [46.17].
- **Fix:** 46.11 «so low over» → «so near to»; 46.17 «She was not low any more» → «She was not so near any more».

### C5 (M). "A keeper at each end": the two keepers are both at the school end
- «It must have a keeper at each end. Somebody who says goodbye to the day, and somebody who says hello to the next.» [46.16]
- «"Goodbye, Friday," said Alex, to the window.» and «"Hello, Saturday," said Felix.» [46.16], both at the school end; the other end is Tyr: «"I'll keep this end," he said.» [46.16]. 47.3: «One at each end. You say goodbye. You say hello.»
- **Fix:** 46.16 Janus: «It must have a keeper at each end. Here, two: somebody who says goodbye to the day, and somebody who says hello to the next. And over there, somebody who minds it.» (47.3 then reads: «One at each end» → add «Two at this end, one at that.» or leave.)

### C6 (M). Quilla's own sky: her home smell is the gods' hill; her "found" sky is the school
- «Mine smells of cold stone and woodsmoke.» [40.15] and the gods' hill: «It was cold, and it smelled of stone and woodsmoke.» [41.12]. She also says «I am *a* moon... Not yours.» [40.15] and her country is «a long way off» [40.15].
- At the climax she smells the school dinner hall and says «Oh, it's *my sky*. It's the dinner hall! I know it now. It's mine. I know where I am!» [46.12]; closing line «the moon knew exactly where she was.» [47.14] while the school has its own moon: «the school's own moon had come out too» [47.14].
- The text never says where she lives now or where she goes back to. **This is a design decision** (see Decisions D1).

### C7 (M). Different Day is never lifted, and the class then does the forbidden thing
- «For the rest of today, and until further notice, it will be a Different Day. This means that nobody may do the same thing as anybody else in the same place at the same time.» [40.9]
- «The whole class stood in a line at the bottom of the field, with the bump in front of us» [45.9] (the same game, same place, same time). Janus's «Practice is not the same as the real thing... One side on its own is nothing» [45.9] explains why it is safe, but nobody ends the rule.
- **Fix:** one sentence at the start of 45.9: «Mister Harris had given up on the Different Day on Wednesday, because even he could not stand it, and Mister Janus said it did no harm as long as only one side was playing.»

### C8 (L). Skadi's «empty bag» holds the bone
- «holding an empty bag and looking embarrassed. "I have brought a bone,"» [47.9]; «He'll eat the bag» [47.9]; «Skadi had come up the hill behind them, holding the bone.» [47.10].
- **Fix:** «an empty bag» → «a lumpy bag».

### C9 (L). Hel says she has "never had a friend" after three days of Dot
- «Dot did not leave her side.» [45.1]; «P.S. I WILL TRY. HEL.» [44.14]; «I have never had a friend. I do not know the rules. But I should like to try.» [47.12].
- **Fix:** «I have never had a friend. I do not know the rules.» → «I have only just had one friend. I do not know the rules.» (keeps her shyness, removes the clash).

### C10 (L). Spatial slip inside 47.7
- «pointing out of the window. There was Mister Thor, with his hammer, mending the gate.» and then «There was a small tree that nobody had noticed before, standing at the end of the corridor, humming.» [47.7] (a corridor is not out of the window).
- **Fix:** «standing at the end of the corridor» → «standing by the bins».

### C11 (L, docs). The Bible says Saturdays; the text deliberately does not
- Bible: «Mister Loki will visit Hel and Fenris on Saturdays.» versus «And I'll come the very first time I'm allowed,» [46.15] (the earlier review fixed the text because a Saturday visit was impossible).
- **Fix (doc only):** Bible running-threads line → «the first time he is allowed».

## B. Gaps (a line the page needs and does not have)

### G1 (M). The twins' own Simurgh feather is never used or explained
- Bible: «The twins hold a feather between them: it only works if both hold it and say "We're lost, please."» On the hill the twins are lost, together, and Alex lists every lost option: «We haven't got a phone. We haven't got a *puddle*.» [43.9]. Their pockets are listed in full, no feather: «half a pencil, a very old tissue, and a conker with a hole in it» [41.12]; «a hair bobble that was not his, a crayon, and the other key» [41.12]. Only at the end, Simurgh calls her feather «an old one» that «is not for finding where you are. It is for finding *each other*.» [47.12], which is the answer, delivered 4 chapters late.
- **Fix:** one line in 43.9 after the phone and puddle: «"And Madam Simurgh's feather is in Lost Property," said Felix, "and anyway it only finds people who are lost *from* each other. We're together." » (uses her own 47.12 rule). Needs your approval because it is canon.

### G2 (L to M). Loki, a shapeshifter, is never asked to cross or carry
- «He had been hiding as a bin, a coat stand, a jumper on a hook, and for one terrible hour a small tree.» [45.3]. A page-level answer (why not become a cat?) is missing. Plausible answer on the page: he was frightened of his children, and after 45.4 nothing prevents him.
- **Fix (optional):** at 45.6 after «Then she can carry things», Kenji: «Could Mister Loki be a cat?» Loki: «I tried it on Wednesday. The edge knows the difference between a cat and a man pretending. I came out in the bin.»

### G3 (L). Heimdall and the rainbow bridge are glimpsed and never used
- «a long bridge that glowed every colour at once» [40.2]; «Of the rainbow bridge. *Our* Heimdall?» [42.10]. Nothing later. Not a contradiction; unpaid atmosphere. **Fix:** none required; could cut «a long bridge» at 40.2, or give Heimdall a line in 46.3. Your call.

### G4 (L). Hel's wooden wolf toy and the «Tea at five» sign are never paid off
- «a small wooden wolf. It was no longer than a thumb» [43.6]; «*4. Tea at five, and it is not optional.*» [43.3]. **Fix (optional):** in 47.10, Hel gives Fenris the wooden wolf to carry ("he will eat it"), or nothing; and Hel says «Tea is at five» in 47.12. Both are tiny.
