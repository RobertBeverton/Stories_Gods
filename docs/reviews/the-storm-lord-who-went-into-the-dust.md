# Review: The Storm Lord Who Went Into the Dust
File: stories/the-storm-lord-who-went-into-the-dust.md | Words: about 4,250 (4,281 with front matter) | Parts: 16

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong spine (clubs with names, then dust and water, then the sieve) and a clear echo of the myth. Parts 2 and 3 are two back-to-back Elfine lectures and Part 3 ends on a soft line, not a page-turn. Parts 14 and 15 each carry a moral speech about dry times and windows. The hook is a real arrival with a clean "Monday".
- Writer: Funny, specific details (weather badge, "Yours hungrily, Mot", puddle "I'll be back"). Part 5 has a run of "shouted" lines (7 in about 12 lines), and "very" is overused. Mostly easy to say aloud. A few hard words: sieve (explained), sulphur, tarmac, champion.
- Listener/parent: Warm, funny, and Hadad is dignified and tender. Mot's swallowing and offer are the scary peak and get relief fast (Willow's POP, water). The ending is a cliffhanger by design, so it energises rather than settles; fine for the recipe, but the Pele tone needs care (see ASK).

## Twin balance
Alex: about 18 spoken lines, key actions: calls Chaser, "Take us!", holds the lightning bolt, "garden watering cans" idea, says "We're a team" | Felix: about 19 spoken lines, key actions: first to spot the other club, comforts Dot, gives the "scared and still say no" speech, "Water" idea shared | Goes first: Alex in Part 2, Felix in Parts 3, 5 and 7 (and shouts first in Part 5). Verdict: balanced. Felix has the single biggest speech (Part 11); Alex offsets it with "Take us!" (Part 7) and the "rain's coming back" promise. Both do the "No" together. No fix needed.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part 11: Mot's offer ("One of the twins... A boy for a god. It's a fair trade") is a child-for-a-god swap, uncomfortably close to the no child sacrifice rule, though instantly refused. For six-year-olds listening to their own names, "I'd take such good care of them" is also very personal. Suggest softer framing: Mot offers a trade for "a little warmth" or "one bright thing" (a handful of the children's laughter or a sunflower), and the twins still refuse on principle. If kept, remove "boy" and "I'd take such good care of them".
2. Part 11: Felix's line "we're not afraid of you. We're a bit scared. But being scared and being afraid are different" confuses two near-synonyms for a six-year-old, and "his voice was shaking, but it didn't wobble" contradicts itself. Suggest: "We are scared of you. But we can be scared and still say no." That keeps the affirmation short and plot-earned.
3. Part 16 (hook): Pele is introduced as "the Fire Lady... Terrible temper. Always sets the furniture alight" and as Hadad's cousin "in a way". Pele is still revered in Hawaii, so this is the one place the story flattens a living tradition into a gag. Suggest Hadad says only "Not her. Not now. She's a force of nature, and she has a long memory" and keep the humour on the furniture, not the goddess.
4. Parts 14-15: Two consecutive wisdom speeches (the dry times, then the windows and "Things end. Seasons end..."). Trim one: cut Hadad's "Things end. Seasons end. Summers end..." paragraph in Part 15 and let the window itself carry the point. Also pairs oddly with Dot's "It's okay to be sad", which is sweet and could stay.
5. Part 8: Hadad said to fetch Anat "if I don't come back by morning", but the twins wait two days with a dying garden. Either make Part 8 begin "By morning he still wasn't back, and by the second morning the rain had gone" or have Elfine remind them the deadline; a plot hole a sharp child will spot.
6. Parts 2-3: Back-to-back exposition. Suggest ending Part 3 on a stronger turn (e.g. Willow's ears flatten as the first drops fall on the cat, or the sky darkens right after "Everything works better when you ask it nicely"), so Part 4 arrives as a surprise.

## Minor notes
- Mot ends as seeds in a bag with no hint of his fate; a half-line from Anat ("He'll sleep till the next dry season") would settle small listeners and set up a future cameo.
- Elfine and Marion are fine here (story 6, before they were retired).
- "Very" appears roughly 25 times; thinning a handful would tighten read-aloud rhythm.
- "That's when" is used once (Part 1); fine.
- Willow's brave act (the POP) and silence are exactly right, and the cat-nose-in-hand ending beat is lovely.
