# Review: The Postman Who Was HERMES
File: stories/the-postman-who-was-hermes.md | Words: about 3,260 | Parts or sections: 16 (plain all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape: clear problem (muddled messages), myth echo (backwards cows become backwards footprints), earned emotional turn (Dot's letter), good deadline. It is about 750 words under the 4,000 guide and the thinnest spot is the middle (the muddle chaos is one paragraph; the list is brief), so it reads a little quick but not thin in story terms. Hook (box for the new PE teacher, banana-loving voice) is a real mystery; the ending is sweet but ends on the hook rather than a settling note, and "Monday" is not used (older pre-ritual form).
- Writer: Lively, specific, funny (Mrs Ibbs serving MATHS, Mr Jackson putting his coat on, the clapping Kenji). Sentences mostly say in one breath. Tics: "He said" / "Maya said" tags are heavy but clear. The footprint logic in Part 7 contradicts itself (see fix 3). "very small voice" is used twice (Hermes, Felix) and "Hermes/Mr Hermes" flips.
- Listener/parent: Warm and respectful; Hermes is dignified and kind, and the thief-and-sorry lesson is gentle. The cold fire-escape climb with no adult at all, and a Reception child alone on a roof, may unsettle a careful parent; a one-line adult presence would fix it. Ends on a mystery rather than sleepy calm, which is fine for a hook story.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 15 spoken lines; notices things, leads the Hermes chat, first footprint clue, says the rescue mantra to Felix, a hand-written note | Felix: about 17 spoken lines; asks questions, completes the backwards deduction, own scared moment and mantra for Alex, a hand-written note | Goes first: Alex (mostly), Felix on the "toes and heels" reasoning. Verdict: balanced. The mirrored climb (each freezes once, the other talks them through) and matching letters to Dot are well done.

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
1. THE LIST (item 4): OLD: "Even the Reception children. Even the hamster." -> NEW: no change needed (Reception is acceptable).
2. EVERYTHING GOES BACK TO NORMAL: OLD: "Inside was a drawing from every single child in Year 2, and the teachers, and the dinner ladies." -> NEW: "Inside was a drawing from every single child in the school, and the teachers, and the dinner ladies." (age-proofing; the only year-group reference)
3. THE FOOTPRINTS: OLD: "And they're all pointing TOWARDS the gate, like someone walked in."" -> NEW: "And they all look like they're pointing TOWARDS the gate, like someone walked in."" (reads as a first impression the twins then correct; the next lines work out the truth. Without this the clue is stated as fact and then reversed.)
4. THE FOOTPRINTS: OLD: "Mr Hermes tried to fly after the thief, but when he flapped his sandals" -> NEW: "Mr Hermes tried to fly off to look for the thief, but when he flapped his sandals" (no thief has been seen yet)
5. THE THIEF: OLD: "she had a Peppa Pig jumper." -> NEW: "she had a jumper with a pig on it." (a branded detail that will date by eight or nine; low priority)
6. THE ROOF: OLD: "Hermes looked at them for a long time." -> NEW: "Mr Hermes looked at them for a long time." (name consistency)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. THE ROOF / THE CLIMB: Everyone climbs a rusty fire escape to a forbidden roof, including a god who says "It's far too dangerous", with no adult in charge. Suggest a line that Mr Harris (or Mister Thor) waits at the bottom holding the rail, or that the twins tell him first, so a parent can read it without wincing.
2. THE THIEF: Dot is shy, quiet and "hardly says anything" here, but the Bible has her as fearless, loves dancing and lost property, and Soup Club assistant. This is her first appearance, so it may be the deliberate origin; if so, add a sentence in the Bible's running notes that story 9 is where she finds her voice. Otherwise soften "very quiet".
3. THE ENDING: Both twins say "Alex said ... Felix said" at the hook, but no shared "Monday" moment. The brief's standing rule is "Monday" at exactly the same time; this early story predates it. Decide whether to leave as written (recommended, since the story's own rhythm is fine) or add it.
4. Mild: three mantras ("I CAN DO HARD THINGS", "I AM BRAVE EVEN WHEN I'M SCARED", "I CAN SAY SORRY, MAKE IT RIGHT, AND KEEP GOING") is generous; the third is the plot-earned one. Optionally cut or keep the first two as part of the climb. Not urgent.

## Minor notes

- Mr Thorley (disguise name) and "THOR" signing the card are settled; no action.
- "That was when" is used once; the "At exactly the same moment / At exactly three o'clock" uses are not the ritual, but "At exactly the same moment" in THE LETTER is close to a stock phrase; fine here.
- The speaker "Today for lunch we are serving MATHS. Please queue by your own shadow." is the funniest line; keep.
