## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong, clear spine (Locke's prank, panic makes it rise, calm brings it down) and the twins' solution is earned. But it is thin at about 3,500 words and front-loaded with roster: the first third is lists. The ending stacks four closers (Harris speech, gods' speeches, eight-affirmation recap, Dot's "gods too?") before the Athena hook, so the peak is long and the hook arrives after the settle-down.
- Writer: Running gags land (Thorley's "Locke.", Ebbie's knocking, "Sorry, habit"). The name density is the real read-aloud problem, and the H cluster (Heston, Hanu, Hermes, Harris, Haydes, Hot) is hard to follow by ear. Tic: "looked at each other" four times. "very small voice" twice.
- Listener/parent: Warm and funny, and the "you are all more powerful than we are" idea is good for a six-year-old. The gods are treated with affection and dignity, nothing mocking. The peril (30 crying children rising above the school) is real but relieved by the twins. Ends on a high-energy hook rather than a wind-down, so a parent may want to slow the last page.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. The Biggest Surprise: "Monday" is said by Athena alone, not by Alex and Felix at the same time, which breaks the standing ending ritual. Suggest Athena says "I believe your school has a competition coming up" and the twins, still whispering, say "Monday" together, then "The End."
2. The List of Gods (and the line-up in The Gods Say Goodbye): twelve dense bold entries read aloud, then a stall roll call, then a third roll call at the gate, is the biggest listening burden. Suggest Maya's list keeps only 5 or 6 gods with a "can do what" line each (Thorley: strong, must not be startled; Locke: watch him; Hermes: fast; Heston: Talos; Shah: slow calm), and the gate line-up becomes "all the gods lined up, even Mr Haydes in his woolly hat." Also cut some Seven Lucky Men stall gags (Bish, Benny, Fuku, Jury, Hot) down to two or three; Ebbie's knocking is the one that sticks.
3. The Gods Say Goodbye / The Biggest Surprise: Hermes says "We're not going... We're staying until the end of term," then the Seven Lucky Men sail off and Ebbie shouts "GOODBYE!" Either make Hermes say "most of us are staying" or have the Lucky Men "go for a holiday, back by September."
4. The Gods Say Goodbye: Thorley's "YOU ARE ALL MORE POWERFUL THAN WE ARE" and Hermes's "the real power isn't the thunder or the wings" lightly sets the gods' own powers against kindness. It is warm, but could read as the traditions being made small. Suggest Hermes add "thunder and wings have their jobs too, but..." or Thorley "Even more than Thor on a good day."
5. Locke Says Sorry: Locke put thirty children in danger, then is hugged within a page. "I'll never forgive myself" is heavy for bedtime and the repair is quick. Suggest Locke offers something to make it right (he fixes the castle's plastic, or runs the rest of the fair tidy-up), which also fits the affirmation "say sorry, make it right, and keep going".
6. The Panic: Maya's rule ("the more scared we are, the higher it goes") arrives conveniently. Suggest planting it earlier, e.g. Locke's sign "GRAVITY HAS BEEN ADJUSTED... AND SO HAVE FEELINGS", or one early moment where a laugh makes it bounce higher.
7. Length and ending: about 3,500 words is below the guide. The recap of eight affirmations in The Last Words works as a crossover payoff, but the story would settle better if it finished with the recap, Dot's question and Hermes's answer, then the Athena hook, with the Harris speech trimmed.
8. Cast: Willow is absent. Since the one-brave-thing role is standing canon, consider one silent Willow beat (steadying the smallest child, or holding the castle rope).
