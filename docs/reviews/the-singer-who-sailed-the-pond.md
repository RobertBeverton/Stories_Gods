# Review: The Singer Who Sailed the Pond
File: stories/the-singer-who-sailed-the-pond.md | Words: ~4,500 | Parts: 17

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape: clear problem (Sampo stolen), a proper voyage, a lock-picking set piece, a chase and tug of war, then a kind resolution (three good things) that fits the myth. Part 16's "real magic was in turning the handle together" is earned. Weak spots: the hook is a reveal of a god already on the premises, not a mystery, and Part 17's heading gives the game away. Slightly long (about 500 over target); Part 2 and Part 6 are talky.
- Writer: Voice is consistent and funny (Choir and Sauna, the bear in the car park, the Inspector scoring the library 2/10, "Tea is for people who have time to be warm"). Mostly breathable lines. Some hard words (pewter, fathoms, pedestal, figurehead, confiscated), a lot of "very", and "looked at each other" four times. Stock beats: grown-up moralising speech (Part 16), eagle "snatching at our hats".
- Listener/parent: Warm, dignified Väinö; Louhi is a lonely villain who is redeemed, which is lovely and settles well. Eagle and "THIEVES!" in Parts 12-13 are the only loud bit and are relieved quickly. Ending is a bit stimulating for sleep (Father Frost yawning a blizzard, a new girl, Willow's tail up), but the lullaby theme is good.

## Twin balance
Alex: ~13 spoken lines, plus sings the tune to the pike, plays the low strings, calls "On three", "Let us" | Felix: ~12 spoken lines, plus speaks politely to the pike, sings harmony, plays the high strings, the "you only see what's wrong" insight | Goes first: Alex mostly (climbs in first, "On three", "Let us", first speaker to Louhi); Felix first with the pike and in "Hello" to Part 1 praise. Verdict: balanced. Felix's pike diplomacy and Part 14 insight match Alex's moments. Willow: no single clear brave act (see ASK 2).

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part 17: The hook is Father Frost waking and a silver-dressed girl, but Father Frost has been on the page since Part One and the girl is a clear next-story cue; it reads as a reveal, not a mystery. Suggest one small unanswered question for the twins to chew on (what is the girl building, why does Frost not remember what he came for) and a quieter close so it settles sleepy listeners. Also check the Bible/Tracker: Frost was seeded as "already asleep in the staff room", so the next story should start from there.
2. Willow: her brave thing is spread thin (purrs, figurehead, shivers, pulls, streaks down, claws the deck). Give her one clear act, for example she is the one who first stops the eagle's dive in Part 13 or noses the last padlock open. Keep her silent.
3. Part 16: Väinö's explanation plus Elfine's "as long as we have each other" is two morals in a row. Consider cutting Elfine's line, since the twins' "We never did" is the better beat.
4. Length: about 500 words over target. Candidate trims: Elfine's exposition in Part 2 (the Lönnrot and Air Maiden detail) and Part 6.
5. Part 14: Louhi says "I made a mill" while Part 5 had her say it "was made for me". Pick one; the first is the kinder-sounding explanation and supports "everyone said I was a thief".

## Minor notes
- Age check: "Reception were building a snowman" is acceptable. No other year or age references found beyond the one in Part 16.
- "That's the best thing I've ever heard" is echoed by Felix (Part 1), Marion (Part 2); fine as a joke but Felix's version reads as the stronger.
- Ollie speaks in Parts 1, 2 and 6, then drops out without explanation when the boat boards; Mister Harris vanishes after Part 5. A passing line ("Ollie stayed to hold the fort") would tie it off.
- Overused "very" (very old, very long, very tall, very still); trim a handful.
- Respectful tone toward the Finnish material throughout; Väinämöinen is warm and dignified, Louhi gets sympathy. No concerns.
