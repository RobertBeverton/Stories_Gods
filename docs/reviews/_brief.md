# Library review brief

Each story in `stories/` is reviewed once, by a fresh reviewer, through three lenses. Reviewers **report only**: never edit a story. Write the report to `docs/reviews/<story-file-name>` (same file name as the story, so `the-man-with-two-faces.md`).

## Audience

Twins who are six now and will be listening for several years. Stories are read aloud by a parent (about 25 to 30 minutes) and later narrated by one voice. The story should still work when they are eight or nine, so it must not date itself to one age or year group.

The first review of the series covered stories 19 to 38. Stories 1 to 7 use a different class (Dev, Ruby, Priya, Nell, Kofi, Poppy, Archie) and the twins lead from about story 8, so judge twin balance only where the twins are leads. Read the Bible sections 'How the early staff fit later canon' and 'To decide' so you know what is already settled: Mister Thor and Mister Loki are the names for new text; stories 1 and 6 keep their disguise names Mr Thorley and Mr Locke; Ms Wa in story 17 is Mrs Nuwa before her promotion; the order of 4 and 5 is unconfirmed. Do not re-raise settled points. Flag only new continuity problems.

## Before you start

1. Read `docs/Story_Bible.md` and `docs/Story_Guide.md` (cast, standing rules, voice).
2. Read your story all the way through, once, slowly.
3. Skim the "What to watch for across the series" section of `docs/Story_Archive.md`.

## The three lenses

**A. Senior children's editor.** Structure and shape. Is the shape sound? (Length is a rough guide: at least about 4,000 words, up to about 6,000 if the story earns it. The 16 to 18 part recipe is a guide, not a rule, and the early stories use plain all-caps section headings instead of `## Part N.` headings. Note the word count and say whether the story feels too thin or too long for what it does; do not fail it for the heading style.) Does each part end with a reason to turn the page? Does the middle sag? Is the problem clear and does it echo the myth? Is the ending earned, the affirmation short and plot-earned, the hook a real mystery, and does "Monday" land with both twins at once? Any part that is confusing, too scary without relief, unfair without being put right, or too long without a laugh? Any grown-up behaviour that is cruel rather than funny?

**B. Working children's writer.** Line-level craft and read-aloud. Can each sentence be said in one breath? Tongue-twisters, confusing name clusters, dialogue where you cannot tell who is speaking? Is the child "we" narrator consistent? Are the details specific and funny? Overused phrases and tics ("looked at each other", "underlined it three times", "at exactly the same time" outside the ritual, "That's when" beyond about four uses)? Stock beats (tower about to fall, small child in danger, staff meeting that goes wrong, sunset speech)? Words too hard for a six-year-old even with context?

**C. Six-year-old listener and reading parent.** Alex and Felix: count each twin's spoken lines and key actions, note who goes first, who has a moment of their own, and any lopsided big moment. Willow does one brave thing and never speaks. Respect for the tradition: the god is warm and dignified, nothing played mockingly, nothing that flattens a living religion into a cartoon (this is about tone and respect, not fact-checking myths; do not assert facts about the myth that you are not certain of, and do not try to verify myths). Does the ending land warmly for a child?

## Age-proofing rule (apply to everything you quote)

The children must not be pinned to an age or school year, so the stories keep working as they grow. Flag every explicit reference to a year group or age ("Year One", "Year 1", "Year Three", "Reception" is acceptable, "aged five", "five years old", "nine years old" for Willow is fine). Propose replacements in the same register: "in class", "the whole class", "the older children", "some big kids from the class above", "a child from a class above", "the little ones". Do not invent a new school structure; keep the sense.

## Output format

Write the report in this shape, and keep it tight (a page or two):

```
# Review: <story title>
File: stories/<file>.md | Words: <n> | Parts or sections: <n>

## Verdicts
- Editor: <1-2 lines>
- Writer: <1-2 lines>
- Listener/parent: <1-2 lines>

## Twin balance
Alex: <spoken lines>, <key actions> | Felix: <spoken lines>, <key actions> | Goes first: <who>. Verdict: <balanced / gap and where>

## DIRECT fixes (safe to apply without asking)
Only: age-proofing wording, typos and continuity slips, hard-to-say sentences, one-word-level cleanups, repeated tic trims. Give each as:
1. Part N: OLD: "<exact text copied from the story>" -> NEW: "<replacement>" (reason)
OLD text must match the story exactly and occur once; include enough context to make it unique.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Part N: <what and why, and a suggested change>

## Minor notes
- <optional>
```

Be honest and specific. If something is good, say so briefly; the aim is a useful list, not a long one. Do not pad. Do not rewrite sections wholesale.
