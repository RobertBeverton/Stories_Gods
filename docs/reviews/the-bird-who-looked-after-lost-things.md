# Review: The Bird Who Looked After Lost Things
File: stories/the-bird-who-looked-after-lost-things.md | Words: ~4,650 (body) | Parts: 17

## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape. Two myths (Zal's feather, Conference of the Birds) woven into one problem, and the puddle reveal ("si morgh", the birds make the Simurgh) is the best beat. Fog problem is clear and resolved fairly. About 15% over length; the middle (Parts 3-5) and the Part 16 speech are where to trim if anything is. Hook is a real mystery (cracked sky, five coloured stones, a tail) and Monday lands with both twins.
- Writer: Clean, specific, funny (Dot and the feather bed, "I will fold", the fainting sparrow, the wren dangling by one foot). One POV slip in Part 7 ("the six of them"). Mild tics: "Alex looked at Felix. Felix looked at Alex." twice, "at exactly the same time/moment" three times outside the ritual, Maya's three-times underlining. "That's when" is used four times (Parts 1, 6, 8, 13), at the limit. Part 16 is a sunset speech, a stock beat, but it is short and warm.
- Listener/parent: Warm and settling. Mister Harris crying with relief is lovely, not cruel. Simurgh is dignified and kind, and the tradition is treated with respect. Zal's abandonment (Part 3) is brief and relieved straight away by Dot's reaction and "it's okay". Fog in Parts 7-8 is scary but has Maya, Willow and the friends close at hand throughout.

## Twin balance
Alex: ~14 spoken lines; holds feather first, opens the call "Together", asks "And if nobody comes?" | Felix: ~14 spoken lines; finds the birds first, asks "What do you do?", "But you didn't lose us" | Goes first: Alex (Part 1), Felix on the birds, Alex on "Together". Verdict: balanced. Twins call in unison, share "Ours", and Simurgh credits both.

2. Part 7: OLD: "They bumped into each other in the dark, and held on. The six of them stood in a tight knot" -> NEW: "We bumped into each other in the dark, and held on. The six of us stood in a tight knot" (child "we" narrator slip)
3. Part 12: OLD: "and they did it at exactly the same time." -> NEW: "and they did it together." (keep "exactly the same time" for the Monday ritual)
4. Part 9: OLD: "And both twins, at exactly the same moment, took a breath and said" -> NEW: "And both twins took a breath together and said" (tic trim)
5. Part 14: OLD: "Alex looked at Felix. Felix looked at Alex.\n\n\"Ours,\" they said." -> NEW: "Alex and Felix grinned at each other.\n\n\"Ours,\" they said." (repeated look beat; already used in Part 5)
6. Part 16: OLD: "She underlined *someone always comes* three times. And then, very quietly, she underlined it a fourth." -> NEW: "She wrote *someone always comes* and, very quietly, drew a box round it." (tic; the "underlined three times" gag is overused across the series)
7. Part 14: OLD: "Dot sat at the front, because she said she was the navigator." -> NEW: "Dot sat at the front, because she said she was in charge of the map." (word a bit hard for a six-year-old; optional)

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. Parts 7-12: Willow's "one brave thing" is gentle (staying still so the wren can climb on her, being kind to thirty birds when she is a cat). It reads as warm but is not brave, and she does nothing in the fog. Suggest she is the first to find the birds in Part 8 (walks into the grey on her own, crying leads her), or that she holds still on the rock while the Simurgh lands. Current moment is sweet; decide if it counts.
2. Part 16: the speech is long and spoken as a sermon. The "become the one who comes for someone else" line is the strongest; consider cutting "You are all a little bit lost..." to two or three sentences. Not essential.
3. Part 4: a doll "Priya, who is eight now... still sad about it" is a faint downer in a cosy scene; consider "she has been looking for it ever since" or making Priya a grown-up's childhood toy. Low priority.
4. Length: about 650 words over target. Part 3 (Maya's lecture) plus Part 4's inventory are the easiest trims if length is a concern for 25 to 30 minutes of reading.
