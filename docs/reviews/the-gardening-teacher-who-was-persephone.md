## Verdicts
- Editor: Strong shape and a clear myth echo (the dark as a beginning; "he didn't ASK"; two homes). Slightly under the 4,000 guide but it does not feel thin; the middle (stairs, dog, whispers) moves and has relief at every beat. Weak spots: the hook rests on a redundant reveal, and there is no "Monday" ritual (see ASK).
- Writer: Funny, specific and very sayable (the potatoes turned to stone, "HIGHLY UNUSUAL" / "BACK TO NORMAL", Cerberus snoring in three rhythms). Mild tics: "looked at each other" three times, "at exactly the same time" twice outside the ritual.
- Listener/parent: Handled gently. The abduction is softened (picked up "as gently as you'd pick up a kitten", woolly hat, no face), Hades is lonely, apologises about eight times and gives soup, and the story names the wrong ("he didn't ASK her") and puts it right with a proper invitation. The Underworld is explained as where people go when they die, in one calm line. Two things could sit heavily at bedtime: Mrs Demmer's collapse in the snow, and the whispers (see ASK 2). Both are resolved well and the ending is warm and settling.

## Twin balance

Verdict: balanced. Fear is Felix's moment, courage under the whispers is Alex's, and both feed the dog and share "IT CAN BE BOTH!". Willow is absent from this story, so no brave-silent beat applies.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. THE SANDWICHES: Felix cites "The Playground Dog. In Wigglesbottom, Susie Keys gave him her cheese sandwich", and Maya answers "That was a story, Felix." This names a real published series (the Guide says the tone is borrowed but never the text) and is the only break of the story's own world. Suggest replacing with an in-world memory, for example a dog that stopped barking when Felix shared his sandwich at the park, and dropping Maya's "That was a story" line (keep Alex's "all dogs just want a sandwich").
2. THE WHISPERS: "Nobody will notice if you give up…" and "Kenji started to cry" are the darkest lines in the story for a six-year-old; "You're too small…" and "Go home…" already do the job. Suggest swapping the "Nobody will notice" line for something like "*Just sit down and rest…*". Optional; the affirmation answer is lovely and well earned. Also five affirmations in a row is a lot to say aloud; consider dropping one (Kenji's "I AM NOT ALONE" is the best to keep).
3. THE POSTMAN: Maya "noticed something odd" about the postman and whispers "That's HERMES", but the children met Hermes properly an hour earlier in the same trainers. The mystery is spent. Suggest the children recognise him at once ("Silver wings. Again.") and let the real hook be the PE-teacher parcel and the mango monkey. Also the story ends "The End." with no twins saying "Monday" at once; as an early story this may be accepted, but a one-line Monday ritual after the monkey would match the later series.
4. THE DEAL / SPRING (logic): the rule is six months underground, but she is home the same afternoon, then "leaving again in the autumn" when it is already October. A literal child may ask when the six months happen. Suggest one line from Miss Persey in THE DEAL such as "Next autumn I'll go down for the first time properly, and Mr Haydes has promised to ask nicely every year."

## Minor notes
- Ollie's seed ("something that looked a bit like gravel") is never paid off; a tiny gag in THE DEAL (his sprouts a pebble-flower) would close it.
- Alex is first introduced as "his twin brother, Alex" and Felix is "Felix Bailey"; fine for the early run, and consistent with the Archive's note on names.
- Mr Hermes, Mr Haydes, Mrs Demmer and Miss Persey match the Bible roster. No new continuity slips found.
- Respect for the tradition is good: Hades is "not evil, fair", Demeter's love is dignified, and nothing is mocked.
