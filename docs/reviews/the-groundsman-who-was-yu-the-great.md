# Review: The Groundsman Who Was Yu the Great
File: stories/the-groundsman-who-was-yu-the-great.md | Words: about 4,100 | Parts or sections: 21 (plain all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: Sound shape and a proper midnight set piece (creak, leak, burst, stage, dig, door, channel). Right length for what it does. The wall-versus-path idea echoes the myth well, and the Gun and Yu reconciliation is the emotional core. Front half is talky (Maya's retelling plus THE LIST, which is never used again), and the ending piles up five affirmations. The hook into story 19 joins cleanly (see below).
- Writer: Mostly easy to read aloud, with good concrete beats (frog hot water bottle, swan on the goalpost, Dot's lilo, "Excuse me, ground"). "looked at each other" appears 4 times plus two "Alex looked at Felix. Felix looked at Alex." beats, so trim a couple. Heavy "He said," runs in the staff-meeting and Mr Yu scenes.
- Listener/parent: Peril is real (burst wall, crying children, chest-high water at night) but there is constant relief (Dot's lilo, "turn everyone into ducks", Mr Thorley's NO) and it settles into hot chocolate, a baby and sunrise. The ending is warm. Good for sleep, with the feather and snake as a gentle mystery.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 13 spoken lines; wakes first, hears the creak, opens the door, wakes Mrs Tibbs, spots where the water wants to go, says "You are not alone" to Mr Yu, makes the final cut | Felix: about 14 spoken lines; asks the ground and finds the crack, asks "why are you in such a rush?", offers to finish the last bit, opens the gate | Goes first: Alex (notices, then Felix asks). Verdict: Balanced overall, with a slight Alex lean in the big moments (the leak discovery and "you are not alone" are Alex's, and Felix is the one who flags with "I can't do this", comforted by Alex). Felix's ground-asking scene is a real moment of his own, so no fix needed. Optional: see ASK 2.

## Join to story 19 (checked)

"Monday" lands with both twins at once. No change needed.

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. THE ENDING of Part THE LESSON: five affirmations are said in a row ("MAKE A PATH, NOT A WALL", then Alex, Felix, Dot, Mr Gun), and "I can be scared and brave at the same time" and "I can do hard things" have both already landed in the action. Suggest keeping "Make a path, not a wall", Mr Gun's, and one twin line each, or dropping Dot's, so it feels earned rather than a checklist.
2. Part THE TWINS and THE DIGGING BEGINS: optional twin tweak. Give Felix the first wake-up or the "you are not alone" line, or let Felix be the one to say "I can do hard things" to Alex at some point, so the comforting runs both ways.
3. Part THE LIST: five bullets that only restate what we already know and never pay off. Suggest cutting to two or three, or having a later beat use them (for example the limp when he wades). Low priority.
4. Part THE THREE DOORS: Mr Yu says of the house, "I did, once. Long ago. It's very like it." but the woman then greets him as her own ("You came back"). It is unclear whether this is his real house or a lookalike. Suggest making it plainly his, for example change to "It's mine. I mean, it was, a very long time ago."

## Minor notes
- Willow is absent from this story entirely. Non-negotiable cast includes her, so consider whether a silent brave moment (carrying a bucket, holding the torch) is wanted.
- Mr Harris's "learned more from you in one night than I did in the last twenty" after "twenty-three years" is a slightly muddled joke, but harmless.
- Words that may be hard for a six-year-old: "weathered", "stoop", "murmur". Fine in context.
- Gun is "blamed" and "very sad" and no more, which is gentle and fine for the age. Yu "lived" four thousand years ago is decided and left as is.
