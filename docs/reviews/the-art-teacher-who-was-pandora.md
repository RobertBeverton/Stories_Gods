# Review: The Art Teacher Who Was PANDORA (re-review after peril rewrite)
File: stories/the-art-teacher-who-was-pandora.md | Words: 4,119 | Parts or sections: 21 (all-caps headings)

## Verdicts
- Editor: The rewrite works. The shape is now cause, spread, low point, twins, Hope, spread, sorry, lesson, hook, and the Hope payoff is properly earned. The weak spot is a heavy middle: from "THE FIRST GRUMBLE" to "THE TWINS" is about a third of the story, and Aches, Empties and Squabbles are introduced in "THE FIVE (AND TWO MORE)" and then shown all over again in "THE SCHOOL GOES GREY". Tighten there; do not cut the peril.
- Writer: Voice is consistent and the jokes land (Mrs Ibbs's menu board, "growled at a leaf", Mr Harris's "That was a rotten day"). "They looked at each other" is used four times plus the hook. One too-hard word ("forethought"). The hook has a continuity slip (Kenji names the Nüwa story before it exists).
- Listener/parent: The peril is about right for six. The illness is blankets, hot heads and "looking very small", and nobody is seriously hurt. The near-fight stops short ("Nobody did"). The adults and gods fail because of the magic, and the twins say "That wasn't us", which gives a child a way to understand it. Relief is steady. The darkest stretch (the "SCHOOL GOES GREY" section) has almost no joke for about 400 words. It settles for sleep: Hope, laughing, the oak tree, then a turtle.

## Twin balance
Alex: about 17 spoken lines; notices Felix's shaking hand and holds the lid, puts a hand on Felix's back ("I've got you"), calls first from the frame ("There's still something good!"), gets the fear peril (the Worry) | Felix: about 16 spoken lines; reaches into the jar and finds Hope, gets the illness peril (the Ache), carries Hope with Alex, calls second ("And it's in you!") | Goes first: Felix at the doorway; Alex in the peril section. Verdict: balanced. Each twin has one peril beat, one rescue of the other and one big discovery. Small notes: Alex's Worry is "cured" in the text and Felix's Ache is never explicitly lifted (see Minor). Dot supplies the big idea, which suits her but slightly sidelines the twins at the key moment (see ASK 3).

## ASK fixes (change plot, a character's arc, an ending, a joke that matters, the hook)
1. THE SCHOOL GOES GREY / THE FIVE (AND TWO MORE): Drag risk. The seven wisps are listed in full, then Aches, Empties and Squabbles are dramatised again. Suggest shortening the descriptions of the Aches and Empties in the list to one line each and letting the dramatised scenes carry them, or cutting about 100 words from the Aches/Empties paragraphs. Also add one light line to the 400-word darkest stretch, for example Mrs Ibbs's curtain blankets making the benches "look like a row of sad sofas", so a six-year-old gets a breath before the near-fight. Do not soften the near-fight; "Nobody did" is the right relief.
2. THE LESSON: The affirmation is enacted up to here (Hope is found by the twins and spreads), but at the very end Mrs Pandy explains the moral ("there's always something left at the bottom...") and the children then recite "THERE IS ALWAYS HOPE" as an echo. Suggest letting a child say it first, for example Felix: "There's always something at the bottom," with Mrs Pandy nodding, so the saying arrives as the children's own discovery. Also, THE WORDS is a montage of six earlier mottos with one effect each. It is fun, but mechanical; it could stay short, with only "I AM NOT ALONE" given extra weight because it is the one the twins just lived.
3. THE TWINS / THE BIG IDEA: The twins already work out "the grey things only really get you when you're on your own... maybe that's how we fix it", then in THE BIG IDEA "they didn't know what to do" until Dot says "it gets bigger when we all have some". The insight is split and slightly repeated. Suggest the twins arrive at the playground stuck on numbers ("only one of her"), and Dot adds the sharing idea, with Alex linking it back ("Like when we looked after each other") so the twins and Dot share the key moment.
4. THE BOX / THE LIST: The note ("Keep it shut until I'm home. Love, P.") is from Pandora, who is now home as Mrs Pandy, and she says "I was told never to look" without saying by whom. A parent may ask. A one-line fix: Mrs Pandy says "Mr Epp told me" or "My note told me", so the rule has a source.

## Minor notes
- Felix's Ache is never explicitly lifted. After the Squabble drifts off in THE TWINS, add "Felix's head felt cooler" or similar so a child listener knows he is better.
- "THE FIVE (AND TWO MORE)" is a cute title but Maya's earlier list names four things (worries, grumps, squabbles, sad days), so "five" is only true once Fibs are in the list. Fix 4 above makes this tidier.
- Continuity checks passed: Hermes delivering the box in story 15's hook matches the text (note and "Epimetheus" line fit); Mr Thorley and Mr Shah are fine under the settled naming and Mr Shah dancing fits Shiva; Mr Hanu, Mr Jackson, Mrs Ibbs, Mrs Felicity, Ms Athene and Mr Heston match the roster; Hope persists into story 18 as the Bible says. The "Chinese goddess" line matches story 17's recap, and Mr Harris notices nothing there, as in this story's ending. Mrs Tibbs appears here as a teacher, consistent with story 15.
- Hard-to-say line: THE SCHOOL GOES GREY, "Mr Thorley and Mr Shah stood on either side of them, both of them red in the face, both of them shouting at the same time about who ought to say stop first." It is readable aloud in two breaths; fine. It is also a good joke, since the grown-ups argue about who should say "stop".
- Good: the apple payoff (Mr Harris tried to eat it, then it goes grey), Dot's Worry falling off her head, and the football resolved with "nobody counted who was winning."
